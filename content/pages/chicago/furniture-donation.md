## The short version

Chicago has several charities that will come get good furniture: **ReStore Chicago** offers a free pickup, **Brown Elephant** runs a curbside pickup, and the **Salvation Army** schedules free pickups by ZIP code [[rs-donate]] [[be-pickup]] [[sa-faq]]. Almost all of them need the furniture clean, undamaged and **already outside or at ground level**.

If no charity wants it, the City will haul it as a bulk pickup at no extra charge for homes it serves, but you have to arrange it first [[chi-san]] [[chi-fee]].

## ReStore Chicago (Habitat for Humanity Chicago)

ReStore Chicago takes new or gently used furniture, appliances, housewares and building materials. You can **drop off** or **request a free pickup** [[rs-donate]].

- **ReStore Chicago North,** 6040 N. Pulaski Rd., Chicago 60646
- **ReStore Chicago South,** 6057 S. Western Ave., Chicago 60636
- **Donation drop-off:** Wednesday to Sunday, 11 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., at both stores [[rs-home]].
- **Questions:** 312-563-0296 or restore@habitatchicago.org [[rs-home]].

**What it takes:** furniture with no rips, scratches, stains or pet hair, such as bookshelves, buffets, chairs, coffee tables, dressers, nightstands, side tables, sofas and sectionals, and TV stands [[rs-guide]]. Appliances must work and have all their parts, with age limits by type (for example, refrigerators, stoves and washers up to 10 years old) [[rs-guide]].

**What it won't take:** clothing, **mattresses**, televisions, used doors and windows, paint, **sleeper sofas** and **china cabinets**. Because of inventory, **bed frames and dining tables** aren't being accepted right now [[rs-donate]]. Its guide also turns away armoires, baby furniture, box springs, exercise machines, pianos and TV cabinets [[rs-guide]]. Staff review every item and can refuse it [[rs-guide]].

## Brown Elephant (Howard Brown Health)

Brown Elephant resale shops raise money for Howard Brown Health [[be-donate]].

- **Curbside pickup:** fill out the online request form and upload photos. They reply with available dates and typically book a few days to a week ahead [[be-pickup]].
- **It's curbside only.** They don't do move-outs. Items must be outside and ready, at ground level outdoors, in a garage or on a loading dock [[be-pickup]].
- Movers can decline anything with stains, rips, excessive pet hair, mold, missing hardware, discoloration or other damage [[be-pickup]].
- To change or cancel, email BEdonate@HowardBrown.org at least 24 hours ahead [[be-pickup]]. Brown Elephant says it can't book pickups by phone [[be-donate]].

**Drop-off:** the Northalsted store (812 W. Belmont Ave., 773-549-5943) and the Andersonville store (5404 N. Clark St., 773-271-9382) are open 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. every day [[be-stores]]. The Oak Park store has closed [[be-donate]].

**Accepted:** upholstered furniture in sound condition with no rips, stains or pet hair, sofa beds with clean mattresses, tables, dining sets, dressers, bookcases, small desks and working electronics [[be-donate]]. **Not accepted:** mattresses, box springs, headboards and bed frames, children's furniture, large appliances, large entertainment centers, armoires, china cabinets, pianos and exercise equipment [[be-donate]].

## Salvation Army

The Salvation Army describes a **free furniture pickup**. Enter your ZIP code on satruck.org to book, or call **1-800-SA-TRUCK (1-800-728-7825)** [[sa-faq]].

Its stores generally take unbroken furniture, such as beds, chairs, coffee and end tables, desks, dining furniture, cabinets, mattresses and sofas [[sa-faq]]. They generally won't take broken, rusty or dirty appliances, **any gas appliance**, built-in appliances, TVs older than 5 years, or paint and chemicals. Rules vary by area, so call to confirm [[sa-faq]].

## Chicago Furniture Bank

Chicago Furniture Bank gives furniture free to Chicago families in need, so it accepts some things others don't, including **mattresses and box springs** and couches [[cfb]].

- **Pickup isn't free.** It asks a suggested donation from **$100** (one large item plus a few small ones) up to **$400 to $600** for a full estate, and needs at least a week to schedule. Pickups run Monday to Saturday, 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. [[cfb]]
- **Drop-off:** 4801 S. Whipple St., Chicago 60632, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. Call **312-752-0211** before you head over [[cfb]].
- **Won't pick up** from the third floor or higher without an elevator, and items must be clean and pest-free [[cfb]].
- **Doesn't take** large sectionals, sleeper sofas, California king beds, wooden or platform bed frames, headboards, armoires, large TV stands, outdoor furniture, pianos or large appliances [[cfb]].

## If no charity will take it

1. **Arrange a City bulk pickup first.** If the City collects your garbage, call your ward Streets and Sanitation superintendent or 311 (312-744-5000 from outside Chicago, or the CHI 311 app) and ask for a bulk pickup **before** you put anything out [[chi-san]] [[chi-311]]. There's no extra charge beyond the monthly garbage fee [[chi-fee]]. Our [Chicago bulk pickup guide](/chicago/bulk-pickup/) explains who qualifies.
2. **Fridges and freezers:** remove the doors or lids before set-out [[chi-san]]. See our [Chicago refrigerator guide](/chicago/refrigerator/).
3. **Five or more units?** The City only collects from residential properties with four units or fewer, so larger buildings aren't eligible [[chi-fee]]. Ask your property manager how bulky items are handled.
