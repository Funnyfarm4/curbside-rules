## The short version

If you live in a **single-family home or a building with four units or fewer**, Chicago's Department of Streets and Sanitation (DSS) can take an old refrigerator or freezer as a **bulk pickup** [[chi-san]] [[chi-grid]]. Two rules come first:

1. **Remove the doors or lids** from any discarded refrigerator or freezer [[chi-san]].
2. **Arrange the bulk pickup** with your ward Streets and Sanitation superintendent (or through 311) **before** you put it out [[chi-san]].

There's **no extra charge** for bulk waste beyond the City's monthly garbage fee [[chi-fee]].

## Who qualifies for City pickup

DSS crews service all single-family homes and apartment buildings of **four units or less**, about 600,000 households, with weekly pickups Monday through Friday [[chi-grid]]. Everyone else must arrange private garbage collection [[chi-grid]].

**Five or more units:** the City says owners of apartment buildings must arrange private or licensed scavenger pickup [[chi-san]]. Ask your property manager how the building handles appliances.

**Businesses** must use a licensed scavenger service [[chi-san]].

## How to schedule a fridge pickup

1. **Find your ward.** Use the City's [ward and alderman lookup](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/mayor/iframe/lookup_ward_and_alderman.html), then open your ward's chicago.gov page for its Streets and Sanitation office.
2. **Call the ward office or 311** and ask for a bulk pickup of a refrigerator or freezer. For example, the 18th Ward Sanitation Office (**312-747-3655**) asks residents to contact it **before** putting bulk items out so a ticket isn't issued [[chi-ward18]]. The 44th Ward lists its ward yard at **312-744-5028** [[chi-ward44]]. From outside Chicago, 311 is **312-744-5000** [[chi-311]].
3. **Take the doors or lid off** before the appliance goes outside [[chi-san]].
4. **Set it out when the ward tells you to.** Don't leave it in the alley or at the curb before the pickup is arranged [[chi-san]] [[chi-ward18]].

## What it costs

City-serviced residents pay **$9.50 per dwelling unit per month** for garbage collection, and the City says there is **no additional cost** for bulk waste collection [[chi-fee]].

## Freon and the Illinois white goods law

You don't need to drain the refrigerant yourself, and you shouldn't try. Here's what the official rules say:

- **Illinois** bans offering or collecting white goods for landfilling until their "white good components" are removed. White goods include refrigerators, freezers, ranges, water heaters, air conditioners and humidifiers. The components include CFC refrigerant gas, mercury switches and PCB-containing parts [[il-whitegoods]].
- **Federal rules** put the job of recovering refrigerant from household fridges, freezers and window air conditioners on the **final person in the disposal chain**, such as a scrap metal recycler or landfill [[epa-608]]. If an appliance arrives with its refrigerant already removed, that final processor must keep a signed statement naming who recovered it and when [[epa-608]].

In practice, that means whoever hauls your fridge to a recycler has to deal with the refrigerant properly. If you hire a private hauler, it's fair to ask how they handle it.

## What the City drop-off won't take

Chicago's Household Chemicals & Computer Recycling Facility (HCCRF) on Goose Island does **not** accept household appliances, including microwaves, stoves, refrigerators, washers, dryers, humidifiers and dehumidifiers. It also turns away air conditioners, HVAC equipment and refrigerants [[chi-hccrf]]. For what it does take, see our [Chicago hazardous waste and electronics guide](/chicago/hazardous-waste-electronics/).

## If your building doesn't qualify

- **5+ unit buildings** and **businesses** need private or licensed scavenger service [[chi-san]].
