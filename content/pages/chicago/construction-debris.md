## The short version

Chicago's garbage service does **not** take remodeling waste. The Department of Streets and Sanitation says that if you're remodeling, **you're responsible for removing discarded building materials**, and that dumpsters can be rented for the job [[chi-san]].

That leaves three realistic routes: **reuse** what's still good (ReStore Chicago takes some building materials), **rent a dumpster**, or **hire a hauler** to load it and take it away. Whatever you do, don't leave it in the alley; Chicago fines illegal dumping heavily [[chi-fly]].

## What counts as construction debris

The City describes construction and demolition (C&D) debris as non-hazardous waste from construction, remodeling, repair or demolition, including [[chi-cd]]:

- Bricks, concrete, rock and other masonry
- Wood, including non-hazardous painted, treated and coated wood
- Scrap metal
- Plaster and gypsum drywall
- Plumbing fixtures and piping
- Non-asbestos insulation
- Roofing shingles and other roof coverings
- Glass and plastics

## Why you can't put it out for bulk pickup

City-serviced homes (single-family homes and buildings of four units or fewer) can arrange a bulk pickup with their ward Streets and Sanitation superintendent **before** putting out a large item or an extra load of trash [[chi-san]]. Remodeling debris is called out separately: the homeowner removes it [[chi-san]].

If you're getting rid of a couch, mattress or appliance during a renovation, those can still go through the City's bulk process. See our [Chicago 311 bulk pickup guide](/chicago/bulk-pickup/).

## Option 1: Reuse what's still good (ReStore Chicago)

ReStore Chicago accepts new or gently used **building materials** along with furniture and appliances, and you can drop off or request a free pickup [[rs-donate]]. Items are subject to staff review and must be clean and in good condition [[rs-guide]].

**Building materials it lists as accepted** [[rs-guide]]:

- Complete kitchen cabinet sets with working doors and drawers (no scratches, nails, mold or mildew)
- Lumber and trim more than 4 feet long (not warped, splintered or with nails)
- New siding and drywall, packaged insulation, plywood, and unopened shingles and tar paper
- Bricks, stone and cinderblocks
- Sinks, toilets, bathtubs, bathroom vanities and medicine cabinets (no chips, stains or rust)
- Hardwood and vinyl flooring over 100 sq ft, and ceramic or glass tile over 50 sq ft
- New interior and exterior doors and new windows

**It won't take** used doors and windows, countertops, carpet rolls, shower doors, cast iron sinks or tubs, wall ovens, paint or toxic materials [[rs-guide]] [[rs-donate]].

Our [Chicago furniture donation guide](/chicago/furniture-donation/) has ReStore's store addresses and drop-off hours.

## Option 2: Rent a dumpster

### On the street, alley, parkway or sidewalk

A dumpster on the public way needs a **CDOT dumpster permit**, and it's the **dumpster provider** that applies for it, not you [[cdot-app]]. For a dumpster of 30 cubic yards or less outside the Central Business District (downtown), CDOT's fee table lists **$50** for 1 to 3 days or **$100** per month; fees double for bigger dumpsters and again downtown [[cdot-app]].

### In your driveway, yard or private lot

CDOT says the dumpster permit **isn't necessary** when the dumpster sits on private property, though other permits may apply [[cdot-app]].

Full rules, placement limits and fines: [Do you need a dumpster permit in Chicago?](/chicago/dumpster-permit/)

## Bigger projects: the 50% recycling rule

Under §11-4-1905 of the Chicago Municipal Code, new construction, demolition and renovation projects that meet the code's requirements must **recycle or reuse at least 50%** of their construction or demolition debris, by weight [[chi-reno]]. Contractors on those projects must [[chi-cd]]:

1. Track how much C&D debris the project generates.
2. Recycle at least 50% of the recyclable debris.
3. Submit a Recycling Compliance Form to the Department of Public Health at the end of the project, with an affidavit from the waste hauler or recycler.

If you've hired a contractor, ask up front whether debris removal and recycling are in their price. The City's page links to the ordinance text for which projects must comply [[chi-cd]].

## Leftover paint, solvents and chemicals

Paint is on ReStore's do-not-accept list [[rs-guide]]. Take leftover paint, stains and solvents from a project to the City's household chemicals drop-off; see [Chicago's hazardous waste and electronics drop-off guide](/chicago/hazardous-waste-electronics/).

## Don't dump it

Illegal or "fly" dumping is dumping any waste on public or private property without a permit, and construction debris is one of the most commonly dumped materials [[chi-fly]]. The City says offenders face **fines starting at $1,500** for a first offense, jail time of up to **6 months**, up to **200 hours** of community service, and vehicle impoundment at the owner's expense [[chi-fly]]. To report dumping, call **311** (or 911 if it may be hazardous waste) [[chi-fly]].
