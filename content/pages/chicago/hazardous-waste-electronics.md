## The short version

Chicago has one permanent drop-off for household chemicals and old electronics: the **Household Chemicals & Computer Recycling Facility (HCCRF)** at **1150 N. North Branch Street** on Goose Island, two blocks east of the Kennedy Expressway at Division Street [[chi-hccrf]].

It's open **Tuesdays 7 a.m. to 12 p.m., Thursdays 2 to 7 p.m., and the first Saturday of every month 8 a.m. to 3 p.m.**, year-round [[chi-hccrf]] [[chi-ewaste]]. The next Saturday opening is **November 7, 2026**, then December 5.

The City's page currently warns that repairs are under way at the facility and asks visitors to watch for schedule changes, so check [the HCCRF page](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/env/supp_info/environmental-permitting-and-inspection/hccrf.html) the day you go [[chi-hccrf]].

## Who can use it

- **All Chicagoans** can drop off their own household chemicals and electronics [[chi-hccrf]].
- **Businesses and large groups can't.** They must hire a private disposal company [[chi-hccrf]].
- The Illinois EPA lists the facility's phone number as **312-744-3060** [[il-epa]].

## What happens when you arrive

There are two drop-off buildings. The **blue building is for household chemicals** and the **yellow building is for electronics** [[chi-hccrf]].

At the chemical door an attendant meets you, you take your items out of the car, and the attendant checks everything and hands back anything that isn't accepted. Then you move on to the electronics building or leave [[chi-hccrf]].

Never leave items outside the gate after hours [[chi-hccrf]].

## Household chemicals it takes

From the City's accepted list [[chi-hccrf]]:

- Household chemicals such as bleach, harsh cleaners and drain cleaners
- **Oil-based paint**, aerosol paint and paint thinner
- Solvents: mineral spirits, stains, paint strippers, preservatives and sealers
- Batteries: rechargeable, lithium, small lead-acid and car batteries
- Compact fluorescent bulbs and fluorescent tubes
- Antifreeze, motor oil, gasoline and other auto fluids
- Herbicides, pesticides and insecticides
- All aerosol cans, such as spray paint and hair spray
- Lawn, pool and hobby chemicals
- Mercury thermometers, switches and thermostats
- BBQ propane tanks, small fire extinguishers and small non-refillable cylinders

## Electronics it takes

The City's electronics program lists computers, monitors, TVs, printers, fax machines and scanners, keyboards and mice, VCRs and DVD players, video game consoles, portable music players, digital converter boxes, cable and satellite receivers, and small-scale servers [[chi-ewaste]]. The HCCRF page also names cell phones [[chi-hccrf]].

Only the listed items are accepted, commercial electronics are refused, and **once the truck is full no more electronics are taken that day** [[chi-ewaste]]. The City's monthly neighborhood e-waste drop-off sites are **on hold until further notice**, so the HCCRF is the City option for now [[chi-ewaste]].

## What the HCCRF won't take

- Business or commercial waste, and agricultural or institutional waste
- **Latex, acrylic and water-based paint**
- **Alkaline batteries**
- Explosives, ammunition and fireworks
- Smoke and carbon monoxide detectors
- **Household appliances**, including microwaves, toasters, coffee makers, stoves, refrigerators, washers, dryers, humidifiers and dehumidifiers
- Air conditioners, HVAC equipment and refrigerants
- **Tires**, cement and concrete
- Medical waste such as sharps and needles, and pharmaceuticals
- Radioactive materials, farm machinery oil and large business copiers [[chi-hccrf]]

## What to do with latex paint

The HCCRF refuses latex paint because of its high disposal cost and low environmental impact. The City suggests you [[chi-hccrf]]:

- **Use it up** on another project or on scrap wood or cardboard, then let the empty can dry and throw it away.
- **Dry it out** by mixing in kitty litter, shredded paper or sawdust, or pouring it into a bag. Once dry, throw the paint and can in the trash.
- **Give it away** to neighbors, or to schools and theater groups that sometimes want paint for sets and art.
- **Store it** for touch-ups, with plastic wrap under the lid so it doesn't dry out.

## Free stuff while you're there

Usable leftovers such as cleaning products and auto fluids may be available free in the HCCRF's material exchange room. Ask a staff member to let you in; it's open to anyone during regular hours [[chi-hccrf]].

## Can't get to Goose Island?

- The Illinois EPA runs one-day household hazardous waste collections around the state that are open to all Illinois residents, and lists other long-term drop-off sites [[il-epa]].
- For bulbs, the City notes you can also take used CFLs to your local Home Depot [[chi-hccrf]].
