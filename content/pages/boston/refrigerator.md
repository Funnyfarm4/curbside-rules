## The short version

A refrigerator can't just go out with the trash in Boston. Massachusetts bans "white goods" (large appliances) from disposal [[dep-bans]], and appliances with Freons, like fridges and freezers, need a **special item appointment** with the City [[bos-special]]. Boston doesn't collect any special item without one [[bos-special]].

The catch: you have to take the doors off and empty it first, and you're capped at **10 special items a year** [[bos-special]].

## Option 1: City special item pickup (by appointment)

Boston Public Works collects appliances and electronics that contain Freons or cathode ray tubes at the curb, by appointment [[bos-special]].

### Which items need an appointment

- **Items with Freons:** fridges and freezers, water coolers, ice makers, air conditioners and dehumidifiers [[bos-special]].
- **Items with cathode ray tubes (CRTs):** televisions, laptops and computer monitors [[bos-special]].

### How to schedule

1. Use the **Schedule a Special Item Pickup** tool on the City's [Special Collection Items page](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/special-collection-items) [[bos-special]].
2. Or call **311** or **617-635-4500**. You can also file a request through Boston 311 [[bos-special]] [[bos-trash]].
3. Can't find your address in the tool? Fill out the City's Special Collection Form, linked in the FAQ on the same page [[bos-special]].

### Limits

- **10 items per calendar year**, and **no more than 5 per appointment** [[bos-special]].
- **No commercial-grade items** [[bos-special]].
- Past your limit? The City says to contact a private hauler [[bos-special]].

### Cost

The City's Special Collection Items page doesn't list a fee [[bos-special]]. Ask 311 if you're unsure.

## How to prepare a fridge or freezer

Follow these City rules, or it won't be collected [[bos-special]]:

1. **Remove every door**, including on mini fridges. Set the doors next to your trash bin for regular trash collection.
2. **Remove all food.**
3. **Timing:** put it out by **6 a.m. on your appointment day** or **after 5 p.m. the night before**.
4. **Location:** where trash is normally placed (curbside, public alley, or dumpster area), in a publicly accessible space. Crews can't collect from private property.
5. **Keep it clear:** don't let vehicles or other objects block it, and don't put anything on top of it.
6. **Keep it intact:** don't smash or take apart the appliance.

### Cancelling or a missed pickup

- **Cancel before 12 p.m. the day before** your appointment, using the link in your confirmation or reminder email. Call 311 for help [[bos-special]].
- **Not collected?** Call 311 within **24 hours** of your scheduled date, or you'll have to reschedule [[bos-special]].

## Option 2: You live in a large building (7+ units)

If your building gets trash and recycling collection from the City, Boston will still collect special items like fridges and air conditioners **by appointment**; call 311 to schedule [[bos-large]]. If your building uses a private hauler instead, ask your property manager how appliances are handled.

## Washers, dryers, stoves and dishwashers

Not every appliance needs an appointment. If an item isn't on the special item list, it doesn't need one [[bos-special]]. The City's Waste Sorting Tool gives these instructions [[bos-wizard]]:

- **Washing machine:** regular trash, bulky item. It must be **empty and the water drained**. Put it next to your trash container on the curb on your regular trash day.
- **Dryer:** regular trash, bulky item. Must be empty.
- **Stove:** regular trash, bulky item. Must be empty.
- For all three: set it out neatly by **6 a.m.** on collection day or **after 5 p.m.** the night before, without blocking the sidewalk. The tool lists reuse (donation) as the best option if it still works.

**Dishwashers, water heaters and plumbing fixtures** are different: the City says to donate them or hire a private hauler [[bos-special]], and it doesn't collect water heaters or plumbing fixtures [[bos-trash]].

## Option 3: Donate it or let the store take it

If the appliance still works, the City suggests donating it to a local organization that will accept it. It also notes that many retailers accept certain electronics and appliances for recycling [[bos-special]]. To find a place, use [Boston's Zero Waste Directory](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/bostons-zero-waste-directory) [[bos-zwd]].
