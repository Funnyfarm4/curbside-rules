## The short version

Boston's trash crews won't take renovation waste. The City says it **does not collect construction debris, automotive parts, plumbing fixtures, fences, water heaters or fire debris**, and tells residents to find a local private hauler [[bos-trash]].

On top of that, Massachusetts bans several common building materials from the trash entirely, so a hauler has to send them to recycling instead of a landfill [[dep-bans]].

Your realistic options are a junk-removal crew that loads it for you, or a rented dumpster (which needs a City permit if it sits on the street).

## What counts, and what's banned

Boston lists these as items a **private hauler** must collect [[bos-trash]]:

- Asphalt pavement, brick and concrete
- Treated and untreated wood and wood waste
- Clean gypsum wallboard (drywall)
- Ferrous and non-ferrous metals

These line up with MassDEP's statewide disposal bans: asphalt pavement, brick and concrete, clean gypsum wallboard, and metals are banned from disposal or transfer for disposal, and wood is banned from landfills [[dep-bans]]. MassDEP says both the people who generate waste and the haulers who transport it are responsible for following the bans [[dep-bans]].

The City also won't take **plumbing fixtures, water heaters, fences, automotive parts or fire debris** [[bos-trash]]. Dishwashers, water heaters and plumbing fixtures are on the City's list of things to donate or give to a private hauler [[bos-special]].

## Why you can't just bag it

Boston requires all trash to fit inside a lidded black or gray barrel, and an overflowing barrel can earn a code enforcement ticket [[bos-trash]]. Construction debris left at the curb won't be collected because the City doesn't collect it at all [[bos-trash]].

Zero Waste Days, the City's drop-off events, also **don't accept construction debris** or asbestos [[bos-zwd]]. They're still useful for the leftover oil-based paint, solvents and chemicals from a project; see [Boston's hazardous waste drop-off guide](/boston/hazardous-waste-electronics/).

## Renting a dumpster: the permit rules

### On the street or sidewalk

A dumpster on a public way needs a **Street Occupancy Permit** from Boston Public Works [[bos-site]] [[bos-occ]].

- **Apply at least 7 business days** before you need the space [[bos-occ]].
- Be ready to say how many dumpsters, what size, and whether you'll use a chute [[bos-occ]].
- You must show **proof that you're bonded** [[bos-occ]]. For storage containers on the street, you need a letter of authorization from the company that owns them, and that company must hold a bond with the City [[bos-occ]].

### What it costs

A **$50 base fee** plus **10 cents per square foot per day** for the first 500 square feet (5 cents after that). Signs are $4 each, and metered spaces are $20 per meter per day [[bos-fees]]. From the City's table [[bos-fees]]:

- One space (160 sq ft) for 1 day: **$66**
- One space for 7 days: **$162**
- Two spaces for 7 days: **$274**
- One space for 30 days: **$530**

Questions go to the Public Works Permitting Division at **617-635-4911** [[bos-fees]].

### On private property

Inspectional Services says any residential or commercial property using a dumpster needs a **Site Cleanliness License** ($50), but **contractors and developers don't need one for construction dumpsters** [[bos-site]]. If you're a homeowner renting your own, ask ISD or your dumpster company which applies.
