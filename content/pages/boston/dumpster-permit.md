## The short version

If your dumpster will sit on a Boston **street or sidewalk**, you need a **Street Occupancy Permit** from Boston Public Works [[bos-site]] [[bos-occ]]. Apply online at least **seven business days** before you need the space [[bos-occ]]. For a typical one-space dumpster the City's fee table starts at **$66 for one day** [[bos-fees]].

Two things trip people up: the City requires **proof of a bond** [[bos-occ]], and you have to **warn your neighbors** with signs and fliers before the job [[bos-occ]].

## Step 1: Gather what the City asks for

Before you apply, Public Works says you'll need [[bos-occ]]:

- **The job and the time:** what type of work the space is for and how long you need it.
- **Your traffic plan** and your plan for hiring a **Boston police detail**.
- **Proof of a bond**, using the City's bond form completed with a bonding company.
- **Parking meter numbers**, if you're taking metered spots (two letters and three numbers on the meter head, like AB123).
- **Dumpster details:** how many dumpsters, what size, and whether you'll use a chute or another way to drop material in.
- **Sidewalk impact:** whether you'll obstruct or close the sidewalk and, if so, whether there's an ADA-compliant pedestrian detour.

The City can withhold a permit if it thinks you haven't given enough information about the scope of work [[bos-occ]]. For Construction Management Plan review, contact the Boston Transportation Department at **617-635-4675** or btdpermitbranch@boston.gov [[bos-occ]].

## Step 2: Apply online

1. Register for an account on the [Public Works online portal](https://onlinepermitsandlicenses.boston.gov/pwdpermits/Views/Login.aspx) [[bos-howto]] [[bos-occ]].
2. Click **Apply for a Permit** and pick **Street Occupancy Permit** [[bos-howto]].
3. If you aren't the **bonded** contact doing the work, search for and add that contact on the Additional Contacts page [[bos-howto]].
4. Enter the work address, the work type, a description of the work and the required details, add attachments, then review and click **Apply** [[bos-howto]].

Companies not listed as a permittee or contractor in the portal must send a **$5,000** surety bond for occupancy permits to pwdpermitbranch@boston.gov [[bos-occ]]. So if you don't hold a bond yourself, the application needs a bonded contractor or dumpster company on it. Ask yours whether it will pull the permit.

After you apply, City staff follow up with any questions and email instructions for paying. Once the application is finalized you print the permit from the portal [[bos-occ]].

## What it costs

The City's street occupancy fees are [[bos-fees]] [[bos-occ]]:

- **Base fee:** $50
- **Space fee:** $0.10 per square foot per day for the first 500 square feet, then $0.05 per square foot per day
- **Signs:** $4 each
- **Metered spaces:** $20 per meter per day

The City's fee table, which counts one parking space as 160 square feet, gives these totals [[bos-fees]]:

- **1 space:** $66 for 1 day, $130 for 5 days, $162 for 7 days, $290 for 15 days, $530 for 30 days
- **2 spaces:** $82 for 1 day, $210 for 5 days, $274 for 7 days, $530 for 15 days, $1,010 for 30 days

Public Works takes cash, a check (with valid photo ID), money order, credit or debit card [[bos-occ]]. Fee questions go to the Public Works Permitting Division at **617-635-4911** [[bos-fees]].

## After you get the permit

- **Residential street:** post signs at least **three days** before the job. The City gives you a flier to copy and leave in doorways and on cars within a half-block, every day for at least **two days** before the work [[bos-occ]].
- **Metered spots:** post signs at least **two days** ahead and flier at least **one day** ahead [[bos-occ]].
- **Car in your space?** Call Boston Police at **617-343-4911** (non-emergency) with the plate number. If police can't reach the owner, they arrange a tow [[bos-occ]].
- **When you're done:** clean up debris, remove signs and fliers, and repair or replace any City property you removed or broke [[bos-occ]].
- **Changing dates, location or times:** email btdpermitbranch@boston.gov [[bos-occ]].

## Do you also need a Site Cleanliness License?

Boston Inspectional Services requires a **Site Cleanliness License** ($50 a year) for anyone using a dumpster on a residential or commercial property, but says **contractors and developers don't need one for construction dumpsters** [[bos-site]]. If you're unsure whether your situation counts, call Inspectional Services at **617-635-5300** [[bos-site]].

## Do you even need a dumpster?

Boston collects some bulky items itself. Furniture can go out with your normal curbside trash on collection day [[bos-trash]], and fridges, air conditioners and TVs go through a 311 special item appointment (see our [Boston refrigerator guide](/boston/refrigerator/)). Mattresses have their own [free pickup by appointment](/boston/mattress/).

But the City **does not collect construction debris**, automotive parts, plumbing fixtures, fences, water heaters or fire debris, and says to use a private hauler for those [[bos-trash]]. Remodeling waste is where a dumpster usually makes sense.
