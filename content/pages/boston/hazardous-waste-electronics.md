## The short version

Boston doesn't have a permanent drop-off center for household hazardous waste. Instead, Public Works runs **Zero Waste Days**, drop-off events for hazardous chemicals, electronics, tires and a few other waste-ban items [[bos-zwd]].

The next full Zero Waste Day is **Saturday, November 21, 2026, from 8:30 a.m. to 12 p.m.** at Millennium Park, 315 Gardner Street, West Roxbury. It's free [[bos-nov21]]. It replaces the September 26 event, which was postponed and moved because of an expected nor'easter [[bos-zwd]].

Before you load the car, check whether you need to go at all: **latex paint and regular alkaline batteries go in the trash** [[bos-zwd]].

## Option 1: Zero Waste Day drop-off

### Rules for attending

- **Boston residents only.** Show a photo ID, utility bill or lease as proof of residency [[bos-zwd]].
- **No business waste** [[bos-zwd]].
- **Arrive between 8:30 a.m. and 12 p.m.** The City asks you not to come before 8:30 and stops accepting waste promptly at noon [[bos-zwd]].
- **20-gallon limit** on paint, motor oil, gasoline and all chemicals combined, and no container over 5 gallons [[bos-zwd]].
- **Labels must be readable** on all paint and hazardous liquids [[bos-zwd]].
- **Pack by station.** Group items in your car the way the City's list groups them, so you can unload at the right station [[bos-zwd]].
- Staff can reject anything that doesn't meet the City's rules [[bos-zwd]].

### Three kinds of events

- **Zero Waste Days** take everything on the accepted list below.
- **Zero Waste Pop-Ups** take at least two types of material; check each Pop-Up's event page for its list.
- **Yard Waste Drop-Off** events take only leaves and yard waste.

All three are listed on the City's [Zero Waste Days page](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/zero-waste-day) [[bos-zwd]].

## What you can bring

### Electronics

Cell phones, computers, computer monitors and TVs, cords, chargers and wires, keyboards, microwaves, printers, power supplies, tablets and VCR/DVD players. The City links a full list on its page [[bos-zwd]].

### Hazardous chemicals

Examples from the City's list: oil-based paint, motor oil, pesticides, aerosol cans, acids, caulk, pool and chemistry-set chemicals, fire starters, flammable liquids, floor-care products, furniture polish, and lithium and nickel-cadmium batteries [[bos-zwd]].

### Mercury items

Fluorescent bulbs of all sizes, thermometers, thermostats, blood pressure cuffs, barometers and mercury switches. Everything must be **intact**; cracked items aren't accepted. Liquid mercury must stay in its original sealed container, placed inside a second sealed bag or bucket [[bos-zwd]].

### Car-related items

- Car tires and bike tires, **up to 8 of each per person**
- Car batteries
- Fire extinguishers
- Propane tanks, empty or full [[bos-zwd]]

### Paper shredding and textiles

- Up to **10 copy-paper-size boxes** of personal documents shredded on site. Remove binder clips and binders; staples, rubber bands and paper clips are fine. You can't watch the shredding because of traffic [[bos-zwd]].
- Clothing, shoes (in pairs), linens, bags and accessories, plus small household items like coffee makers, dishware, pots and pans, toys and musical instruments [[bos-zwd]].

## What Zero Waste Days won't take

No asbestos, explosives, radioactive material, commercial or industrial waste, **construction debris**, alkaline batteries (those go in the trash) or medical waste [[bos-zwd]]. The City doesn't collect construction debris at the curb either; it says to use a private hauler [[bos-trash]]. See [how to get rid of construction debris in Boston](/boston/construction-debris/).

## Paint and batteries: trash or drop-off?

- **Latex and acrylic paint** isn't hazardous. Let it dry out in the can and put it out on your trash day [[bos-nov21]] [[bos-zwd]].
- **Oil-based paint** goes to a drop-off event [[bos-zwd]].
- **Alkaline batteries** (AAA, AA, C, D, 9-volt, labeled "no added mercury" or with a green tree logo) go in your regular trash [[bos-zwd]].
- **Bring to an event:** lithium-ion rechargeables (phones, laptops, small power tools), rechargeable NiCad AA to 9-volt, lithium primary, lead-acid automotive or sealed, and button or coin cells [[bos-zwd]].

## Can't make it on November 21?

- **TVs and monitors:** schedule a curbside special item pickup instead of waiting for an event. Televisions and computer monitors need an appointment, booked on the City's [Special Collection Items page](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/special-collection-items) or through 311 at **617-635-4500**. The limit is 10 special items a year and 5 per appointment [[bos-special]] [[bos-zwd]].
- **Electronics, batteries, bulbs and more:** the City says many retailers and other locations take them year-round. Search [Boston's Zero Waste Directory](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/bostons-zero-waste-directory) [[bos-zwd]] [[bos-dir]].
- **Questions:** call 311 at 617-635-4500 or email zerowaste@boston.gov [[bos-zwd]].
