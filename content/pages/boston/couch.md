## The short version

Getting rid of a couch in Boston is easier than in most big cities:

1. **Put it out with your trash.** Boston Public Works says furniture can go out with your **normal curbside trash and recycling on your scheduled collection day**. No appointment is needed [[bos-trash]] [[bos-special]].
2. **Or donate it,** if it's clean and in good shape. Habitat's ReStore, New Life Furniture Bank and the Salvation Army all take sofas, with limits [[hab-restore]] [[nl-accept]] [[sa-faq]].
3. **Or pay to have it carried out,** if you can't get it to the curb yourself.

The one catch is anything with a **mattress** in it. Boston won't collect mattresses with regular trash; they need a scheduled pickup [[bos-mattress]]. More on sofa beds below.

## Option 1: Curbside trash pickup (no appointment)

[[bos-trash]]

- **What goes out:** couches, chairs, tables and other furniture. The City's list of items that *don't* need special collection includes "Furniture (donate or curbside trash)" [[bos-special]].
- **When:** wait until **after 5 p.m. the evening before** your collection day, and have it out **no later than 6 a.m.** on collection day.
- **Which day:** check the **Trash Day app** or the City's waste collection schedule search. Enter only your street number and name, with no unit number or ZIP code [[bos-sched]].
- **Where:** at the curb with your trash. For larger buildings on City service, the City asks that furniture be **neatly placed next to the trash receptacles** [[bos-large]].
- **Cost:** Boston's trash pages list no fee or item limit for furniture. The 10-items-a-year limit applies to **special collection** items (fridges, TVs, air conditioners and the like) and to **mattresses**, not to furniture [[bos-special]] [[bos-mattress]].

If you don't follow the trash rules, you may get a ticket from Boston's Code Enforcement Division [[bos-trash]].

### What Boston won't take at the curb

The City doesn't collect **construction debris, automotive parts, plumbing fixtures, fences, water heaters or fire debris**. You'll need a private hauler for those [[bos-trash]]. If you're tearing out built-in furniture or cabinets as part of a remodel, see our [Boston construction debris guide](/boston/construction-debris/).

## Sofa beds, futons and recliners

- **Futons:** the City's mattress program does **not** accept futons, mattress toppers or similar non-mattress items. Boston says those **may be placed out for curbside trash** [[bos-mattress]].
- **Mattresses:** Boston won't collect mattresses or box springs with curbside trash; Massachusetts bans them from disposal. You must schedule a mattress pickup, up to **10 items per calendar year**, for most buildings with **6 units or fewer** [[bos-mattress]]. See our [Boston mattress guide](/boston/mattress/).
- **Sleeper sofas:** Boston's pages don't say how to handle the fold-out mattress inside a sofa bed. Call **311 (617-635-4500)** before you put one out [[bos-trash]].

## Who gets City curbside service

- **Homes and buildings with 6 units or fewer** are covered by Boston's resident and small-landlord trash rules [[bos-trash]].
- **Buildings with more than 6 units** count as large residential buildings. The City offers trash and recycling collection to **qualifying** large buildings that buy approved containers, and the same furniture rule applies there [[bos-large]].
- **Buildings whose trash receptacles are larger than 2 cubic yards** must use a **private hauling service** [[bos-large]]. If that's your building, ask your property manager before you put a couch out.

## Option 2: Donate it

If your couch is clean and undamaged, a charity may take it. Each one has its own rules:

- **Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston ReStore:** free drop-off at **1580 VFW Parkway, West Roxbury**, **Tuesday to Saturday, 9:30 a.m. to 4 p.m.** In-home pickup is booked through ReSupply, which works with local movers for a fee. Habitat takes upholstered furniture that isn't **ripped, worn, broken, dated or faded**. It won't take **sofa beds, sofas with slipcovers or futons** [[hab-restore]].
- **New Life Furniture Bank of MA:** accepts **couches and sectionals up to 7 feet per piece**, but not **sleeper sofas, recliner sofas or futons**, and nothing stained, worn or damaged [[nl-accept]]. Its own low-cost truck serves a list of towns south and west of Boston that **doesn't include Boston**. For Boston addresses it points to Limitless Moving, an independent mover that charges market rates [[nl-pickup]].
- **The Salvation Army:** schedule a free furniture pickup by ZIP code at satruck.org or **1-800-SA-TRUCK (1-800-728-7825)**, and confirm trucks serve your address [[sa-faq]].

Our [Boston furniture donation guide](/boston/furniture-donation/) has more detail on each.

## Couch didn't get picked up?

Boston's trash pages don't spell out a missed-pickup process for furniture. File a request with **Boston 311** online, by calling **617-635-4500**, or by emailing **311@boston.gov** [[bos-trash]].
