## The short version

Boston has good places to donate furniture, but **free pickup is limited**. Habitat for Humanity's ReStore in West Roxbury and New Life Furniture Bank in Walpole both take **free drop-offs**. Their pickups either cost money or don't list Boston in the service area [[hab-restore]] [[nl-pickup]]. The Salvation Army offers a **free pickup** you book by ZIP code [[sa-faq]].

If nobody will take a piece, the City lets you put furniture out with your **regular curbside trash** on collection day [[bos-trash]].

## Option 1: Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston ReStore

The ReStore resells donated furniture, appliances and building materials to fund Habitat's affordable homes, and donations are tax-deductible [[hab-restore]].

- **Drop-off (free):** **1580 VFW Parkway, West Roxbury**. Donations are accepted **Tuesday through Saturday, 9:30 a.m. to 4 p.m.** Phone **617-327-1170** [[hab-restore]].
- **Pickup (paid):** Habitat books in-home pickups through **ReSupply**, a Boston scheduling company that works with local movers "at a lower cost to you." You still get a tax receipt. You can also email **ReStore@habitatboston.org** about a pickup for qualifying items [[hab-restore]].
- **Furniture they want:** bed frames, dressers and armoires, coffee and end tables, desks (3 by 5 feet or smaller), file cabinets, headboards and footboards, outdoor furniture, tables and chairs (not conference tables or dated pieces), and upholstered furniture that isn't ripped, worn, broken, dated or faded [[hab-restore]]. Its wish list asks for appliances less than eight years old [[hab-restore]].
- **Take elsewhere:** mattresses and box springs, futons, sofa beds, sofas with slipcovers, hutches, sideboards and china cabinets, TV armoires and entertainment centers, platform and trundle beds, and baby furniture [[hab-restore]].
- Staff can refuse anything based on condition and store need, and all donations must work [[hab-restore]].

Habitat says a second Greater Boston ReStore is opening at **44 Green River Way in Watertown**; check its page for the opening date before you go [[hab-restore]].

## Option 2: New Life Furniture Bank of MA

New Life gives furniture to families moving out of homelessness and serves Greater Boston from its Walpole distribution center [[nl-pickup]].

- **Drop-off (free):** Walpole, **Saturdays 9:30 to 11:30 a.m.**, no appointment. It doesn't take resident drop-offs at other times. Enter on **West Street**. It's closed on Memorial Day, Independence Day and Labor Day weekends and during the December holidays [[nl-dropoff]].
- **Standard pickup:** New Life's own truck, on a sliding-scale fee from **$95** (up to 10 items outside or in a garage) to **$400** (more than 10 items from inside), with a **2 to 4 week** lead time. The pickup area is Canton, Dedham, Dover, Easton, Foxboro, Franklin, Mansfield, Medfield, Medway, Millis, Natick, Needham, Norfolk, Norwood, Sharon, Sherborn, Walpole, Wellesley and Westwood. **Boston isn't on the list** [[nl-pickup]].
- **Priority pickup (anywhere):** handled by Limitless Moving, an independent for-profit company, at market rates, with extra charges for items New Life can't accept [[nl-pickup]].
- **What they take:** pieces that fit a small apartment and a narrow staircase, with no stains, wear, damage or missing parts. That includes dressers up to 6 feet wide, couches and sectionals up to 7 feet per piece, upholstered chairs and recliners, dining tables up to 5 feet, small desks and bookcases up to 6 feet tall [[nl-accept]].
- **What they don't take:** mattresses, box springs and bed frames of any size, futons, headboards, sleeper and recliner sofas, wardrobes, entertainment centers, hutches, outdoor furniture, and children's furniture [[nl-accept]].

## Option 3: The Salvation Army

The Salvation Army's site describes a **free furniture and household-goods pickup**. Schedule it by entering your ZIP code at [satruck.org](https://satruck.org/Faq) or by calling **1-800-SA-TRUCK (1-800-728-7825)** [[sa-faq]]. Confirm that trucks serve your address before you count on it.

Its thrift stores generally take unbroken furniture, such as beds, chairs, coffee and end tables, desks, dining furniture, cabinets, mattresses and sofas [[sa-faq]]. They generally won't take appliances that are broken, rusty, dirty or missing a cord, **any gas appliance**, built-in appliances, TVs older than 5 years, large console TVs and stereos, auto parts, or paint and chemicals [[sa-faq]].

## Find more places

Boston's [Zero Waste Directory](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/bostons-zero-waste-directory) lists local places to donate, resell, repair or recycle items that are too good to trash. Public Works can be reached at **617-635-4500** [[bos-zwd]].

## If no charity will take it

1. **Furniture goes out with regular trash.** Boston Public Works says furniture can be put out with your normal curbside trash and recycling on your scheduled collection day, with no appointment [[bos-trash]].
2. **Mattresses must be scheduled** for collection. See our [Boston mattress guide](/boston/mattress/) [[bos-trash]].
3. **Appliances** like fridges and air conditioners must be requested through 311. See our [Boston refrigerator guide](/boston/refrigerator/) [[bos-trash]].
4. **Big building?** Boston treats buildings with **more than six units** as large residential buildings with their own rules [[bos-large]]. Ask your property manager how bulky items are handled.
