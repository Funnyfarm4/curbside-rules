## Why you can't just leave it on the curb

Since November 1, 2022, Massachusetts has banned mattresses from disposal or transport for disposal [[dep-bans]]. Boston follows that rule: the City **will not collect mattresses or box springs with your curbside trash**, and it won't collect any mattress without an appointment [[bos-mattress]].

The good news: for most smaller buildings, the City picks it up for free [[bos-news]].

## Option 1: Free City curbside pickup (buildings with 6 units or fewer)

Boston Public Works collects mattresses and box springs at the curb by appointment for residents of most buildings with **six units or fewer** [[bos-mattress]]. The City describes this as a **free** curbside pickup [[bos-news]].

### How to schedule

1. Go to the City's [Mattress Recycling page](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/mattress-recycling#schedule-pickup) and use the scheduling tool in the "Schedule Pickup" section [[bos-mattress]].
2. When you book, include where your mattress will be: at the curb where your trash and recycling are normally collected [[bos-mattress]].
3. Watch for your confirmation and reminder emails. They include the link you'd use to cancel [[bos-mattress]].

Can't find your address in the scheduler? The City's mattress page has an address-help form in its FAQ. If you live in a building with six or fewer units but still can't book, there's a separate eligibility form on the same page [[bos-mattress]].

Need help? Contact Boston 311 by dialing **311**, calling **617-635-4500**, or emailing **311@boston.gov** [[bos-mattress]].

### What the City accepts

- **Accepted:** memory foam, box springs, innerspring, hybrid and latex mattresses [[bos-mattress]].
- **Not accepted:** futons, mattress toppers and similar non-mattress items. Put those out with your regular curbside trash instead [[bos-mattress]].
- **Limit:** 10 items per calendar year [[bos-mattress]].

### Cost

Free for eligible residents [[bos-news]].

## How to prepare your mattress for pickup

Follow these City rules, or your pickup may be missed or need rescheduling [[bos-mattress]]:

1. **Timing:** put the mattress at the curb by **6 a.m. on your appointment day**, or **after 5 p.m. the night before**.
2. **Location:** curbside, where your trash and recycling usually go. Crews can't collect from private property.
3. **Keep it clear:** don't let cars or bins block it, and don't put anything on top of it.
4. **Keep it whole:** don't crush or cut the mattress or box spring.
5. **Bag it if needed:** mattresses that are soiled, dirty or have bed bugs must be **bagged and fully sealed**. Mattress bags are sold at hardware stores and online.
6. **Bad weather:** the City suggests bagging the mattress so it can still be fully recycled.

### Cancelling or a missed pickup

- **Cancel before 12 p.m. the day before** your appointment, using the link in your confirmation or reminder email. For help, call 311 [[bos-mattress]].
- **Not collected?** Call 311 within **24 hours** of your scheduled date. After that you'll need to book a new appointment [[bos-mattress]].

## Option 2: You live in a building with 7 or more units

The City's mattress program doesn't serve buildings with more than six units [[bos-mattress]]. Here's what the City says to do instead [[bos-large]]:

- **Ask your property manager** how your building handles mattress recycling. The City notes there are many local mattress haulers that serve large residential buildings.
- **Hire a mattress recycler.** The City keeps a list of local mattress recyclers on its [large residential buildings page](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/large-residential-buildings#mattress-recycling-), with phone numbers and notes on each.
- **Search MassDEP's directory.** The City also points to MassDEP's [Beyond the Bin](https://recyclesmartma.org/beyond-the-bin-search/) search tool, which has a "Mattresses & Box Springs" category. MassDEP notes that listings are self-reported and recommends calling ahead [[dep-btb]].

> Prices for private recyclers vary widely. On the City's list they range from **$25** per item (drop-off, with proof of Boston residency) to **$245** (one hauler's king-size price), as posted on boston.gov [[bos-large]]. Confirm the price with the company before you book.

The same options work if you've already used your 10 City pickups for the year [[bos-mattress]].

## Option 3: Drop-off and recycling

The City's own program is curbside pickup by appointment. Its mattress page doesn't list a City drop-off site [[bos-mattress]]. If you'd rather drive it somewhere yourself, several recyclers on the City's list accept drop-offs [[bos-large]]:

- **Tough Stuff Recycling**, 145 Authority Drive, Fitchburg. 978-307-4118. Drop-off listed at $25 per mattress or box spring with proof of Boston residency or business. Pickup listed at $75.
- **HandUp Mattress Recycling & Upcycling**, 127 Rodney French Blvd, New Bedford. 774-271-8744. Drop-off listed at $35 per mattress or box spring.
- **Green Mattress**, 144 Field Street, Brockton. 781-720-9855. Drop-off at their facility; curbside pickup also offered.
- **Raw Material Recovery Corp.**, 461 West Broadway, Gardner. 978-730-8266. Drop-off center open to the public, with a monthly price sheet.

These details are as listed by the City of Boston [[bos-large]]. Call ahead to confirm hours and prices.

## Option 4: Donate it or let the store take it

- **Buying a new mattress?** Ask the seller to haul away the old one when they deliver the new one. Boston and MassDEP both note that many mattress sellers do this [[bos-mattress]] [[dep-mattress]].
- **Still in good shape?** MassDEP suggests donating it. Use the Beyond the Bin directory to find charities and organizations that accept mattresses [[dep-mattress]] [[dep-btb]].

## Other bulky items in Boston

Boston handles other large items differently [[bos-trash]]:

- **Furniture** can go out with your normal curbside trash and recycling on your collection day.
- **Refrigerators, TVs and air conditioners** need a pickup request through 311.
- **Textiles** must also be scheduled for collection.
