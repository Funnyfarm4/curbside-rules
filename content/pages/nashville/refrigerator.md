## The short version

You have three official ways to get rid of a refrigerator or freezer in Nashville:

1. **Sheriff's Office curbside pickup.** Refrigerators and freezers are on the Davidson County Sheriff's Office list of items it removes [[dcso-bulk]]. Call **615-880-3897** [[dcso-contact]].
2. **Free drop-off at a Metro Convenience Center.** One bulk scrap metal item, including a refrigerator, is accepted **at no charge**, and it gets recycled [[nws-bulky]].
3. **Donate it to Habitat's ReStore** if it's **7 years old or newer** and fully working. Free pickup is available for ground-floor items [[hab-donate]].

## Option 1: Sheriff's Office curbside pickup

1. **Call 615-880-3897.** You don't need the Sheriff's website contact form for bulk item removal [[dcso-contact]]. The homeowner or tenant has to make the request. You'll be asked to list the items, the service address, a phone number and the resident's name [[dcso-bulk]].
2. **Put it at the front curb by 6:30 a.m.** on your date. Crews work from the **front curb only** [[dcso-bulk]].
3. **Give it time.** Crews have **up to seven business days** from your date, not counting weekends and holidays [[dcso-bulk]].

The same call can cover other appliances on the list: **washers and dryers, ranges, microwave ovens, water heaters, air conditioners and freezers** [[dcso-bulk]].

**Who can't use it:** crews are prohibited on apartment, condo and townhome complexes, private roads, gated communities and business properties; residents of complexes are asked to put items at the front curb by the main entrance. Bulk items that resulted from an **eviction** can't be removed by law [[dcso-bulk]].

**Recycling note:** Metro says scrap metal isn't recycled through curbside pickup but **is recycled at a Convenience Center** [[nws-bulky]].

More on this service: [Nashville bulk trash pickup guide](/nashville/bulk-pickup/).

## Option 2: Free drop-off at a Convenience Center

Residents can drop off **one bulk scrap metal item, or up to three trash bags of scrap metal, at no charge**. Metro's scrap metal list includes **refrigerators, dishwashers, ovens and ranges, washers and dryers** and other large household appliances [[nws-bulky]].

**Locations,** all open **Tuesday to Saturday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.** and closed on Metro holidays [[nws-hours]]:

- **Anderson Lane:** 939A Anderson Ln, Madison, TN 37115
- **East:** 943A Doctor Richard G. Adams Dr, Nashville, TN 37207
- **Ezell Pike:** 3254 Ezell Pk, Nashville, TN 37211
- **Omohundro:** 1019 Omohundro Pl, Nashville, TN 37210

**Before you go** [[nws-cc]]:

- Bring a **Tennessee driver's license** showing you live in Davidson County.
- You **unload it yourself**, so bring a helper and a dolly.
- **No commercial vehicles** (including any with company advertising or commercial tags) and no commercial waste.
- Not sure about an item? Use the Waste Wizard in Nashville's Waste and Recycling App or call **615-924-2633**.

**Empty it first.** Take out all food and tape the doors shut before you move it.

## Option 3: Donate a newer, working fridge

Habitat for Humanity of Greater Nashville's **ReStore** accepts refrigerators, freezers and other large appliances if they are [[hab-donate]]:

- **7 years old or younger**
- **Clean**, in **100% working order**, with all parts

It **can't accept gas appliances or dishwashers**, and final approval is at the discretion of its donation staff [[hab-donate]].

- **Drop off:** 414 Harding Place, Ste 110, Nashville, TN 37211, **Tuesday to Saturday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.** [[hab-donate]]
- **Free pickup** for large or heavy items located on the **ground floor**; items must be disassembled. Scheduling depends on demand, but pickups generally happen **within two weeks**. Call the Nashville donation hotline at **(615) 942-1290** [[hab-donate]].
- **Need it faster, or it's upstairs?** Habitat offers a **priority paid pickup** through its partner ReSupply for items that need pickup within 48 hours, need disassembly, or aren't on the first floor. ReSupply charges a fee based on the number of items [[hab-donate]].

## What about the refrigerant?

Household refrigerators and freezers usually go to disposal with their refrigerant still inside. Under federal rules, the **final person in the disposal chain**, such as a scrap metal recycler or landfill, is responsible for making sure the refrigerant is recovered. If a fridge arrives already emptied of refrigerant, that facility needs a **signed statement** with the name and address of whoever recovered it and the date [[epa-608]]. Don't cut refrigerant lines yourself; ask the facility or hauler how they handle it.
