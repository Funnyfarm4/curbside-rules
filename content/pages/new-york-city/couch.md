## The short version

New York City will haul away your old couch **for free**. There's nothing to book: DSNY no longer takes pick-up appointments for large items [[dsny-large]]. You just need to put it at the curb **on the right night**, in the right spot [[311-bulk]].

For a regular fabric or wood couch, that means between **6 p.m. and midnight the night before your trash (bulk) collection day**, not your recycling day [[311-bulk]] [[dsny-furn]].

## Option 1: Free DSNY curbside collection

DSNY gives NYC residents free curbside removal of large furniture, including bed frames, bookcases, couches and sofas, desks, dressers, and tables and chairs [[dsny-furn]]. The service covers **residential buildings** [[311-bulk]].

- **Cost:** free [[dsny-furn]].
- **Appointment:** none. Appointments are no longer offered and can't be scheduled [[dsny-large]] [[311-bulk]].
- **Limit:** up to **6 items** at the curb per collection day [[dsny-large]]. With a lot of furniture, set it out over several collection days [[311-bulk]].

### Trash night or recycling night?

DSNY splits furniture by what it's made of [[dsny-furn]] [[311-bulk]]:

- **Trash night:** couches and sofas, EZ chairs, wood tables, and anything made of wood or wood composites like particle board or fiberboard. These are not recyclable, so set them out before your **trash-only** day, **not** your recycling day [[dsny-furn]] [[311-bulk]].
- **Recycling night:** furniture that's **mostly metal or rigid plastic**, like metal bed frames, cabinets, or aluminum lawn furniture with plastic webbing [[311-bulk]].
- **Glass:** glass furniture, windows and mirrors can't be recycled. Set them out with trash, tape up large glass, and double-bag broken glass [[dsny-furn]].

### Find your night

Look up the collection schedule for your address (DSNY's [collection schedule tool](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dsny/forms/collection-schedule)) and find your bulk trash ("large item") day [[311-bulk]]. The couch goes out the night before.

## How to set it out

1. **Take it apart if you can.** DSNY asks you to disassemble furniture before setting it out, and to bundle smaller pieces and tape or tie them with twine [[dsny-furn]].
2. **Sofa bed or pullout?** Remove the metal frame and put it out with metal recyclables. The rest of the sofa goes out with trash [[311-bulk]].
3. **Time it.** Put it at the curb between **6 p.m. and midnight** the night before collection [[311-bulk]].
4. **Right spot.** Place it at the curb or in your alley if that's your designated collection area. It can't be in the street, extend in front of another property, or block people walking. Items left on private property are "unserviceable" and DSNY can't pick them up [[311-bulk]].
5. **Bed bugs?** Put a sign on the couch saying it has bed bugs, so no one takes it [[311-bulk]].

If something isn't collected, NYC311 can help: call **311** or **212-NEW-YORK (212-639-9675)** [[311-bulk]].

## Moving out or renovating?

- **Clearing out an apartment:** the 6-item limit still applies, so a full move-out can take several collection days [[dsny-large]] [[311-bulk]].
- **Renovation debris** from a do-it-yourself project can go out as bulk items too, but for a **major** construction or renovation project DSNY says to arrange a dumpster through a private trash removal service [[dsny-cd]].
- **A dumpster on the street** needs a DOT permit, and the carting company gets it, not you. Storage pods for moving or storing furniture aren't permitted on City streets [[311-dumpster]]. See our [NYC dumpster permit guide](/new-york-city/dumpster-permit/).

## Option 2: When DSNY pickup won't work

DSNY's free bulk collection is for residential buildings. NYC311 points non-residential buildings to its separate "Waste Disposal for Businesses" rules [[311-bulk]]. And DSNY only takes what's at the curb. If you can't get a couch down the stairs, can't wait for your collection night, or have more than 6 pieces to move, a paid junk-removal crew can carry it out for you.
