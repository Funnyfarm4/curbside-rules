## The short version

If your furniture is clean and in good shape, **Habitat for Humanity's NYC and Westchester ReStore** will pick it up **for free**, as long as you have enough large pieces and they pass review [[hab-pickup]] [[hab-donate]]. **Housing Works** will also come to you, for a **small fee** based on your location and the items, if you have at least **five** pieces in excellent condition [[hw-pickup]].

DSNY's [donateNYC](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dsny/what-we-do/programs/donatenyc.page) program and [directory](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/donate/site/Directory) are the City's official tools for finding nearby reuse organizations [[dsny-donate]] [[dsny-dir]].

The catches are condition, quantity and timing. Charities turn away stained, torn or damaged pieces [[hab-guide]] [[hw-pickup]], Habitat needs several items at once [[hab-pickup]], and Housing Works asks you to wait **2 to 3 business days** just to hear back [[hw-pickup]]. If you're moving this week, the pieces are upstairs with no elevator, or a charity said no, skip to [what to do if they won't take it](#if-no-charity-will-take-it).

## Option 1: Habitat for Humanity ReStore (free pickup)

Habitat NYC and Westchester's ReStore accepts gently used furniture, appliances, cabinetry, building materials and other household items, and donations are tax-deductible [[hab-donate]]. They describe the pickup as a **free** service for people in their service area who have a large quantity they cannot transport themselves [[hab-pickup]]. They also ask you to consider a monetary donation to help cover operating costs [[hab-donate]].

- **Item minimums:** **6** large furniture or appliance items if the pickup is in **Manhattan, the Bronx, Queens or Westchester**; **10** if it is in **Brooklyn, Staten Island or Southeast Queens** [[hab-pickup]].
- **Stairs:** an elevator is required if items are more than **one flight** up (tell them about outdoor steps too). Otherwise you have to bring the pieces down to an accessible level [[hab-pickup]].
- **What they take:** clean, cosmetically attractive furniture with no rips, tears, stains or pet damage. That includes upholstered pieces in good condition and hardwood tables (dining tables should come with chairs), nightstands, dressers, bed frames, coffee and end tables, bookshelves and children's tables and chairs. They do **not** take cribs [[hab-guide]].
- **What they don't take:** mattresses or box springs, clothing, pianos or organs, and a longer list of building materials and hazardous items in their guidelines [[hab-guide]].
- **They can still say no.** Habitat may reject a request for oversized, overstocked, unmarketable or unsuitable items, and drivers can refuse pieces for wear, odor, staining, weight or a difficult removal [[hab-pickup]].
- **How to schedule:** use Habitat's [online pickup form](https://onlinedonationpickup.com/habitatnycwc/). They say they review requests and get back to you within **4 to 5 business days** [[hab-donate]]. The scheduler sends a confirmation email within an hour, but that email is **not** an approval; the pickup stays tentative until they finish the review [[hab-pickup]].
- **To cancel:** email them or call **(646) 808-0158** [[hab-pickup]]. For business donations or questions, Habitat lists **646-876-9460** and restore@habitatnycwc.org [[hab-donate]].

**Dropping off instead?** Email photos to restore@habitatnycwc.org with **DROP OFF** in the subject line. They say someone will reply within **48 hours** about whether the ReStore can take the items [[hab-donate]]. The store is at **470 Nepperhan Ave., Yonkers, NY 10701**, **(332) 777-5060**, Tuesday to Saturday **10 a.m. to 5 p.m.**, plus the first and third Sunday of the month from **noon to 4 p.m.** It is closed Mondays [[hab-store]].

## Option 2: Housing Works (paid pickup)

Housing Works picks up furniture for a **small fee** based on your location and what you're donating [[hw-pickup]].

- **Minimum:** at least **five pieces** of furniture, so this fits a room clear-out better than a single sofa [[hw-pickup]].
- **Photos** are required with the request [[hw-pickup]].
- **Lead time:** after you submit the form, allow **2 to 3 business days** for a response, depending on volume. A team member then contacts you to schedule [[hw-pickup]].
- **What they take:** furniture in excellent condition, plus clean bagged apparel, boxed or bagged accessories and shoes, antiques, art, decorative items, boxed housewares, jewelry, lighting, unusual finds, clean rugs up to **6 feet by 9 feet** (with occasional exceptions), and up to **4 boxes** of books (no larger than 24 by 24 inches) [[hw-pickup]].
- **What they will not take:** furniture that isn't structurally sound or needs reupholstering; upholstery with multiple stains, fading, tears or pet hair; wood or other furniture with surface damage; pieces with missing parts (shelves, doors, casters, legs, cushions); office furniture; mattresses, box springs, futons and day beds; obsolete media stands or cabinets; large glass-top dining tables; Formica, wood-composite or plywood pieces; IKEA furniture (with occasional exceptions); electronics older than two years; used household appliances; large appliances; and medical equipment [[hw-pickup]].
- **Declined on pickup day:** the most common reasons are worn, damaged or stained upholstery, a removal path that is too small, furniture that isn't structurally sound, pet hair and sun fading [[hw-pickup]].

You can also drop off gently used goods at any Housing Works thrift shop during store hours [[hw-pickup]].

## Option 3: The Salvation Army (check your ZIP)

The Salvation Army's donation site describes a **free furniture and household-goods pickup**. Schedule it by entering your ZIP code at [satruck.org](https://satruck.org/Faq) or by calling **1-800-SA-TRUCK (1-800-728-7825)** [[sa-faq]]. The Manhattan Adult Rehabilitation Center points donors to the same number and website [[sa-manhattan]].

Pickup availability is local, so confirm that trucks serve your address before you count on it [[sa-faq]].

In general, Salvation Army thrift stores accept unbroken furniture such as sofas, chairs, coffee and end tables, desks, dining furniture, cabinets, beds and mattresses, along with working (not built-in) appliances [[sa-faq]]. They generally will not take broken, rusty or dirty appliances, **any gas appliance**, built-in ovens, stoves, dishwashers or microwaves, TVs older than 5 years, large console TVs and stereos, auto parts, or paint and chemicals [[sa-faq]].

## Option 4: Big Reuse (Brooklyn drop-off; large-volume pickups)

Big Reuse takes furniture at its **Gowanus Reuse Center, 1 12th St., Brooklyn, NY 11215**, daily from **10 a.m. to 5 p.m.** Do not leave items after 5 p.m. [[bigreuse]]. Large furniture, appliances and building materials are accepted only at Gowanus, not at the Bushwick shop [[bigreuse]].

- **Furniture rules:** pieces must be clean and undamaged. They do **not** take used couches or upholstered chairs unless those pieces are new, bed-related items (frames, headboards, bunk beds, mattresses, box springs, linens), breakable composite or particleboard, or unframed glass [[bigreuse]].
- **Pickup:** they ask you to contact them for large-volume pickups. Email donate@bigreuse.org or call **718-725-8925** [[bigreuse]].
- The Bushwick shop at **378 Troutman St.** takes smaller items (clothing, home goods, books, accessories, kitchenware and media) daily from **11 a.m. to 7 p.m.** [[bigreuse]].

## Hour Children: call before you go

Hour Children's own thrift-shop page currently says it is **not accepting donations** while it works through inventory [[hour]]. When donations are open, they ask you to call **718-433-4724** to schedule a pickup of furniture and larger items, and they do not accept recliners, beds or mattresses, large appliances, medical equipment, electronics, books, exercise machines, cribs or several other baby items [[hour]]. Do not leave donations outside when they are closed; they say that creates a hazard and can bring heavy fines [[hour]]. Hour Children appears in the City's donateNYC directory [[dsny-dir]].

## If no charity will take it

1. **DSNY curbside collection (free):** NYC residents get free removal of large furniture such as bed frames, bookcases, couches and sofas, desks, dressers, and tables and chairs [[dsny-furn]]. You can set out up to **6 items** per collection day, and pick-up appointments are no longer offered [[dsny-large]]. Put mostly metal or rigid plastic furniture out with recycling, and everything else out with trash on your **trash-only** day, not your recycling day. Take pieces apart if you can, and bundle smaller parts with tape or twine [[dsny-furn]] [[dsny-large]].
2. **Mattresses** have extra rules (they must be sealed in a plastic bag) and separate fines. See our [NYC mattress guide](/new-york-city/mattress/) [[dsny-furn]].
3. **Set-out timing, placement and the sofa-bed rule** are covered in our [NYC couch and furniture guide](/new-york-city/couch/).
