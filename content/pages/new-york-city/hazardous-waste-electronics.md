## The short version

New York City residents can drop off paint, batteries, electronics and other harmful household products at one of **five year-round DSNY Special Waste Drop-Off sites**, one in each borough. They're open **Tuesday to Saturday, 9 a.m. to 3 p.m.**, and closed on legal holidays and during severe weather [[dsny-sw]].

These items are "dangerous and illegal to dispose of with trash or recycling," in DSNY's words [[dsny-sw]].

## Where the sites are

Some sites may not be visible from the road [[dsny-sw]].

- **Bronx, Hunts Point:** Farragut Street and the East River, next to the Fulton Fish Market. Enter on Farragut Street, off Food Center Drive.
- **Brooklyn, Greenpoint:** 459 North Henry Street (drop-off site near 530 Kingsland Avenue). The entrance is off Kingsland Avenue, north of Greenpoint Avenue.
- **Manhattan, Lower East Side/Two Bridges:** 74 Pike Slip between Cherry Street and South Street, under the Manhattan Bridge.
- **Queens, College Point:** 30th Avenue between 120th and 122nd Streets, at the northwest corner of DSNY's Queens District 7 garage.
- **Staten Island, Fresh Kills:** off the Muldoon Avenue exit of the southbound West Shore Expressway, past the DSNY security booth.

## What you can bring

From DSNY's list [[dsny-sw]]:

- Aerosols
- Batteries: automotive, rechargeable (lead acid, Li-Ion, Ni-MH, Ni-ZN, Ni-Cd) and single-use (alkaline, lithium)
- E-waste, including all cords, cables and wiring
- E-cigarettes and vape pens
- Fluorescent light bulbs and CFLs
- Motor oil and transmission fluid (up to 10 quarts per visit)
- Motor oil filters (up to two per visit)
- Paint (up to five gallons per visit)
- Passenger car tires (up to four per visit)
- Mercury-containing devices (up to two per visit)
- Skin lightening products

## Rules at the site

- **Residents only.** No businesses or commercial vehicles. You may be asked for proof of NYC residency [[dsny-sw]].
- **Do it yourself.** DSNY staff can give instructions and help, and the City recommends casual clothes and work gloves [[dsny-sw]].
- **Batteries:** bag them individually or cover the terminals with **clear** tape (never opaque tape). Alkaline batteries should not be bagged or taped [[dsny-sw]].

## What counts as e-waste

Only items covered by the NYS Electronic Equipment Recycling and Reuse Act count: computers and accessories, TVs and video equipment, phones, tablets and e-readers, game consoles and their cords. Most household appliances (microwaves, refrigerators), digital cameras, radios, calculators, GPS devices, power tools, medical equipment and batteries aren't covered e-waste, though they may still have special disposal rules [[dsny-ewaste]].

## Other ways to dispose

DSNY lists these options for e-waste [[dsny-ewaste]]:

- a retail or non-profit drop-off location near you
- a community e-waste event
- a SAFE Disposal event
- ecycleNYC, for apartment buildings with 10 or more units
- a manufacturer takeback program

**SAFE Disposal events:** DSNY's fall 2026 series ran one event per borough, from September 20 to October 24 [[dsny-safe]]. All the listed dates have passed, and we didn't find newer dates, so use the year-round sites or check nyc.gov/safedisposal or call 311 [[dsny-safe]].

## What these sites don't cover

Furniture, mattresses and bulky household junk aren't on DSNY's drop-off list [[dsny-sw]].
