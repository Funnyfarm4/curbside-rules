## The short version

New York City's Department of Sanitation (DSNY) will take renovation debris **only** from small **do-it-yourself** projects, and only **up to 6 large items per collection day** [[dsny-cd]]. If you hired a contractor, or the job is a major renovation, the debris is not residential garbage: you need a **private dumpster or hauler** [[dsny-cd]] [[311-rules]].

So the real question is who did the work and how much there is. A weekend bathroom refresh can usually go out over a few trash days. A gut renovation can't.

## Does your job count as DIY?

DSNY calls a project do-it-yourself if it is **small in scale and done without the help of professionals**, like hired workers or contractors [[dsny-cd]]. NYC311 puts it more narrowly: the curbside allowance is for repair work in **your own one- or two-family home** when you don't hire anyone to help [[311-rules]]. It also says work on **income properties (3-family homes and larger)**, fee-for-service jobs and contractor material must go to a **private carter or dumpster** [[311-rules]].

The same split applies to wood: debris from construction or home improvement is treated as **commercial waste** unless you did the work yourself and didn't hire anyone [[311-wood]].

## How to set out DIY debris for DSNY

Pickup is free, and there's no appointment; DSNY says large-item pickup appointments are no longer offered [[dsny-large]].

1. **Count your items.** You can set out **up to 6 large items** at the curb per collection day [[dsny-cd]]. NYC311 adds that each must be light enough for **two people to lift** and small enough to **fit in a garbage truck** [[311-rules]].
2. **Sort by material.** Items that are **mostly metal or plastic** go out with **recycling**. Everything else goes out with **trash** [[dsny-cd]], on your trash-only day, not your recycling day [[dsny-large]].
3. **Large pieces** that don't fit in a bin, like cabinetry, drywall, siding, sinks, toilets and tubs, follow the large-item rules above [[dsny-cd]].
4. **Wood and flooring:** treated wood (lumber, particle board, plywood and other composites) is trash, not compost, and so are vinyl and laminate flooring and tile [[dsny-cd]]. Bundle anything that won't fit in a bin in bales **no more than 2 feet high and 4 feet long**, tied with **twine or rope** (not nylon line or tape), with **nails removed** [[311-wood]]. Set it out between **6 PM and midnight** the night before your trash day [[311-wood]].
5. **Glass:** windows and mirrors **can't be recycled**; put them out with trash. Tape up large glass, double-bag broken glass, and pull exposed nails and other sharp objects [[dsny-cd]].

> **Needs special handling:** appliances with CFCs, harsh cleaners and paint, insulation, and thermostats [[dsny-cd]]. **Asbestos** is hazardous waste and must never go out with trash or recycling; DSNY says to use a hazardous-material disposal company [[dsny-cd]].

For appliances, see our [NYC refrigerator guide](/new-york-city/refrigerator/). For leftover paint and chemicals, see [NYC hazardous waste and electronics drop-off](/new-york-city/hazardous-waste-electronics/).

## Hired a contractor or doing a big renovation?

DSNY is clear: construction and demolition waste from professional contractors **cannot be discarded as residential garbage**, and the contractor or resident must arrange a **dumpster through a private trash removal service** [[dsny-cd]]. For major projects that generate a lot of waste, it gives the same advice even if you're doing the work yourself [[dsny-cd]].

Before you sign, ask your contractor whether debris removal is in the price. DSNY says private contractors should arrange disposal of debris like heaters, sinks and tubs [[dsny-cd]].

**Check the hauler is legit.** The Business Integrity Commission (BIC) licenses and registers waste haulers in NYC. Carters that only remove demolition, construction, alteration or excavation debris get a **BIC registration**, and anyone must have a license or registration before running a waste removal business [[bic]]. Ask for the hauler's BIC number.

## Putting a dumpster on the street

A container on the street needs a DOT permit, and in NYC it's the carting company that gets it. Our [NYC dumpster permit guide](/new-york-city/dumpster-permit/) covers which permit fits your job and where the container can't go.

## Reuse what's still good

DSNY points to reuse before disposal and lists the donateNYC Exchange, Big Reuse, the Rebuilding Together NYC Rebuild Center and Habitat for Humanity's NYC ReStore as places to reuse or exchange building materials [[dsny-cd]].

**Big Reuse (Gowanus, Brooklyn)** takes building materials, furniture and appliances at its **Gowanus Reuse Center, 1 12th St., Brooklyn**, daily **10 AM to 5 PM**; large furniture, appliances and building materials are accepted only at Gowanus, not its Bushwick store [[bigreuse]]. Its guidelines include [[bigreuse]]:

- **Cabinets:** complete sets only, sink base included, all hardware and shelving, doors attached; plywood or high-end brands, no particleboard
- **Lumber:** free of fasteners, in job-lot quantities over 4 feet long; sheet goods at least 4 by 4 feet
- **Tile** new in the box; salvaged hardwood flooring nail-free, at least 200 sq ft
- **Doors** without holes, cracks or rot (no flat-panel hollow-core doors); windows new in box
- **Plumbing fixtures** clean and working, with no cracks, chips, stains or rust

It **won't take** vinyl siding or sheetrock [[bigreuse]]. Questions go to donate@bigreuse.org or 718-725-8925 [[bigreuse]].

Furniture leftovers? See our [NYC furniture donation guide](/new-york-city/furniture-donation/).

## Don't dump it

NYC311 lists construction debris among the things it's illegal to dump on public or private property [[311-dump]]. The owner and driver of the vehicle are both legally responsible, fines for the owner run **$4,000 to $18,000**, and the vehicle may be **impounded** [[311-dump]] [[dsny-dump]]. Call **911** for dumping in progress, or report past dumping to **311** [[311-dump]].
