## The short version

Indianapolis doesn't collect remodeling or construction debris. **Construction waste can't go in your trash cart** and **construction debris isn't accepted as heavy trash** [[indy-101]]. Drywall, lumber, shingles, broken tile, old cabinets and demo rubble all need another route: haul them to a landfill yourself, rent a dumpster, hire a hauler, or donate whatever is still usable.

## Option 1: Haul it to South Side Landfill

South Side Landfill is a permitted municipal solid waste landfill that accepts **construction and demolition debris**, garage and basement cleanout debris, and clean fill [[ssl-faq]]. The City lists it as the place to go if none of its other options work [[indy-101]].

- **Address:** 2561 Kentucky Ave., Indianapolis, IN 46221, **317-247-6808** [[ssl-rates]]
- **Hours:** Monday to Friday 5 a.m. to 7 p.m., Saturday 5 a.m. to 3 p.m., closed Sunday. Holiday hours vary, so call ahead [[ssl-faq]].
- **Payment:** cash, check, and debit or credit cards, but **not American Express** [[ssl-faq]].

### What it costs

Cash customers pay by **volume** [[ssl-rates]]:

- **C&D and drywall:** $25.50 per cubic yard
- **Shingles:** $28.00 per cubic yard
- **Clean fill:** $13.00 per cubic yard
- **Open-top solid waste** (household junk): $25.50 per cubic yard
- **Car:** $21.00
- **Pickup truck:** priced from the landfill's chart by how full the bed is. SUVs and vans are charged like a similar pickup. Flat-bed, dump-bed and box trucks are measured and charged by the cubic yard.

Every load also pays a **fuel surcharge** that changes monthly, and solid waste carries a state fee of **$0.60 per ton**. Call 317-247-6808 for this month's surcharge [[ssl-rates]]. The landfill's online calculator estimates a load from its length, width and height [[ssl-rates]].

### Sort your load

A mixed load is charged at the rate of the **most restrictive material** in it [[ssl-rates]]. Clean fill means only uncontaminated rocks, brick, concrete, road demolition material or dirt. Wood, drywall, brush and painted concrete don't count [[ssl-faq]]. Keep broken concrete and brick separate from drywall and lumber, and the concrete goes at the cheaper clean fill rate.

If your load is too heavy to unload, the landfill offers a paid **pull-off** service: you attach a chain or rope to the load, their equipment pulls it, and you sign a liability release [[ssl-faq]].

### What the landfill won't take

No liquids, hazardous waste or infectious waste. Leftover paint has to be solidified first (sawdust, clay-based cat litter or cellulose insulation). Solvents, chemicals, used motor oil and car batteries are household hazardous waste and go to ToxDrop [[ssl-faq]]. See our [Indianapolis hazardous waste and electronics guide](/indianapolis/hazardous-waste-electronics/).

## What about the Citizens' Transfer Station?

The City's $2-to-$5 Saturday drop-off at 2324 S. Belmont Ave. is described as the place for **extra household trash, heavy trash and bags of leaves** [[indy-cts]], and construction debris isn't listed. Since construction debris is specifically excluded from heavy trash [[indy-101]], call DPW at **317-327-4000** before you bring remodeling debris there [[indy-101]]. Trailers, moving trucks and loads over 3/4 ton aren't allowed in any case [[indy-cts]].

## Option 2: Donate usable materials

Pulling out cabinets or fixtures that still work? Habitat for Humanity's ReStore accepts **kitchen and vanity cabinet sets with doors attached, countertops, bathtubs, sinks and toilets**, plus working ceiling fans with their hardware, lamps and light fixtures, and clean area rugs [[hab-donate]]. The City's donation list notes the ReStore takes new and reclaimed building materials [[indy-recycle]].

Pickup is free. Call the donation line at West Washington (317-969-8791), Avon (317-707-7530), Fishers (317-896-9423) or Greenfield (317-318-9458), or drop off Tuesday to Saturday, 10 a.m. to 7 p.m. Items must be in good working order, and the ReStore can refuse them because of condition or disposal costs [[hab-donate]]. More options are in our [Indianapolis furniture donation guide](/indianapolis/furniture-donation/).

## Option 3: Rent a dumpster or hire a hauler

For a kitchen, bathroom or roof job, the City's advice for loads you can't take yourself is to **rent a dumpster or hire a responsible trash hauler** [[indy-dumping]].

- **Dumpster on the street or sidewalk:** that needs a right-of-way permit from the City. Our [Indianapolis dumpster permit guide](/indianapolis/dumpster-permit/) covers who issues it, the fees and the timeline.
- **Check the hauler:** ask to see their **Waste Hauler License**, and don't pay without a **disposal receipt**. The City says disposing of a truckload properly costs contractors **at least $60**, so be suspicious of anything cheaper [[indy-dropoff]].
- **Ask where it's going.** Check background, references and whether they're licensed by the City. Some people offering cheap removal dump the load illegally [[indy-dumping]].

## Don't dump it

Leaving debris on public or private property without permission violates Sec. 601-2 of the municipal code, with fines up to **$2,500** per instance [[indy-dumping]]. Wood and debris left at City recycling drop-off sites count as illegal dumping too [[indy-dropoff]]. If someone dumps on your property, you're responsible for the cleanup, and if a City contractor does it you're billed at least **$418** [[indy-dumping]].
