## The short version

In Indianapolis, electronics and household chemicals can't go out with the trash. Indiana's e-waste law (IC 13-20.5) bans electronics from curbside and heavy trash pickup, and pouring or trashing hazardous waste creates real environmental and health hazards [[indy-101]]. The City's answer is **ToxDrop**, Saturday drop-offs for Marion County residents that take both household hazardous waste and electronics [[indy-tox]]. For electronics on a weekday, the nonprofit **RecycleForce** takes drop-offs on the near east side [[rf-services]].

## Option 1: ToxDrop (Saturdays)

ToxDrop is only for Marion County residents and doesn't accept commercial waste [[indy-tox]].

- **1st Saturday, 9 a.m. to 2 p.m.:** Traders Point Collection Facility, 7550 N. Lafayette Rd.
- **2nd and 4th Saturdays, 9 a.m. to 2 p.m.:** Perry Township Government Center, 4925 S. Shelby St.

The sites are **closed on the third and fifth Saturdays** and on all major holidays. Don't leave anything at a closed site: that's illegal dumping, with fines up to **$2,500** [[indy-tox]]. The City also runs occasional drive-thru disposal events when resources allow [[indy-tox]].

### Limits and packing

- Up to **20 gallons of liquid** or **75 pounds of solid** waste per visit [[indy-tox]].
- Up to **five computer systems** per visit [[indy-tox]].
- Bring it in plastic crates, cardboard boxes or storage bins you can see into. **Waste in trash bags may not be accepted** [[indy-tox]].

### What ToxDrop takes

**Paint and finishing:** paint, paint stripper, paint thinner, wood stains, wood varnishes, floor wax, furniture polish [[indy-tox]].

**Garage and yard:** gasoline, used motor oil, brake fluid, lighter fluid, lead acid batteries, propane tanks under 20 lbs, fertilizer, herbicides, insecticides, pesticides, pool chemicals, rock salt [[indy-tox]].

**Around the house:** AA and AAA batteries, aerosol cans, adhesives and glue, air fresheners, ammonia, bathroom and oven cleaners, carpet cleaners, chlorine bleach, detergents, stain removers, moth crystals, nail polish and remover, used cooking oil, fire extinguishers [[indy-tox]].

**Bulbs and mercury:** compact fluorescent bulbs, fluorescent tubes, LED bulbs, thermostats, thermometers, elemental mercury [[indy-tox]].

**Electronics:** computers and laptops, monitors, **TVs 27 inches or smaller** (including flat screens), printers, scanners, fax machines, keyboards, mice, hard drives and USB drives, modems, cable and satellite boxes, DVD and VHS players, gaming systems, e-readers, cameras, GPS units, MP3 players and UPS batteries [[indy-tox]].

## Option 2: RecycleForce (electronics, weekdays)

RecycleForce is a nonprofit electronics recycler at **816 N. Sherman Dr.** It takes consumer drop-offs **Monday to Friday 8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m.** and **Saturday 9 a.m. to 1 p.m.** Pull up at the southwest corner of the building and honk; staff help unload [[rf-services]]. Call **317-532-1367** with questions [[rf-services]].

- **What it takes:** basically anything with a cord, plus some metals and limited residential Styrofoam (one carload max) [[rf-services]] [[rf-materials]].
- **Cost:** free, except requested donations of **$25** per TV or monitor (CRT, flat screen or projection) and **$20** per refrigerator, freezer, air conditioner or dehumidifier [[rf-materials]] [[rf-services]].
- **What it won't take:** paint or other household hazardous waste, smoke detectors, vapes and e-cigarettes, or anything contaminated with chemicals or food [[rf-services]] [[rf-materials]].
- **Big TVs:** this is the place for a TV over 27 inches, since ToxDrop's limit is 27 inches [[indy-tox]] [[rf-materials]].

Data-bearing devices are taken apart and hard drives are physically destroyed [[rf-services]].

## What about fridges and other big appliances?

Appliances containing Freon can't go in the trash [[indy-101]]. The City will pick up a refrigerator, freezer or AC unit if you schedule it through the Mayor's Action Center at **317-327-4622**; allow ten business days [[indy-issue]]. See our [Indianapolis appliance and tire guide](/indianapolis/appliance-tire-disposal/) for the details.

## Hiring a hauler for the rest

A garage or basement cleanout usually turns up more than paint cans and old TVs. Furniture and bulky items can go out as heavy trash (two items a month) or to the Citizens' Transfer Station [[indy-dropoff]]. If you hire someone to haul it, ask to see their **Waste Hauler License** and don't pay without a disposal receipt [[indy-dropoff]].

## Don't leave it at a recycling bin site

Hazardous waste is on the City's list of items that count as illegal dumping when left at recycling drop-off sites, with fines up to **$2,500** [[indy-dropoff]].
