## The short version

There's no single "cheap rent" list for Indianapolis, but official data tells you what's normal and where rents run lowest. HUD's **Fair Market Rents** estimate the 40th-percentile gross rent (rent plus utilities) for a standard-quality unit, so about 40% of comparable rentals cost less [[hud-fmr]]. If an ad is far below these numbers, look at it closely, because a rent well below the local norm is one of the FTC's warning signs of a scam [[ftc-scams]].

For listings, use live searches sorted by price (below) instead of any list of prices, which goes stale within days.

## Indianapolis rents by bedroom count

HUD's FY 2027 Fair Market Rents for the Indianapolis-Carmel, IN HUD Metro FMR Area, which includes Marion County and eight surrounding counties [[hud-fmr]]:

- **Studio:** $1,187 a month
- **1 bedroom:** $1,336
- **2 bedrooms:** $1,536
- **3 bedrooms:** $1,991
- **4 bedrooms:** $2,425

For comparison, the Census Bureau's 2024 American Community Survey puts the **median gross rent** actually paid in Indianapolis at **$1,219** a month (±$33) [[acs-rent]]. That median covers every rented home in the city, all sizes, including people who've been in the same place for years.

## Income-based and subsidized housing

**HUD-assisted apartments.** HUD's map shows subsidized apartments near you: go to [HUD Resource Locator](https://resources.hud.gov/), choose "Find Affordable Housing Opportunities Near Me," enter a ZIP, then call the properties. HUD notes the map shows locations and contacts only. It doesn't list vacancies or waiting lists, and you apply with each property's management [[hud-help]].

**Indianapolis Housing Agency (IHA).** IHA serves Indianapolis and Marion County. It runs public housing, about 9,000 Housing Choice Vouchers and more than 1,600 affordable units [[iha-home]]:

- **Section 8 vouchers: waiting list closed.** IHA says it is processing applications selected in its 2016 lottery, and future openings will be announced in advance on indyhousing.org and in the media [[iha-faq]].
- **Applying:** IHA takes applications through its online [Applicant Portal](https://www.indyhousing.org/apply-now) (a RentCafe login) [[iha-apply]].
- **Contact:** 317-261-7200, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., 1919 N. Meridian St., Indianapolis, IN 46202 [[iha-home]].
- With a voucher, you rent from a private owner: an apartment, town home, house, duplex or mobile home [[iha-faq]].

**IHCDA (state housing agency).** IHCDA says it does **not** cover the city of Indianapolis or Marion County for vouchers. If you'd live in a surrounding county, use [its map of local providers](https://www.in.gov/ihcda/homeowners-and-renters/housing-choice-vouchers-hcv/new-hcv-applicant) and check its waiting-list notices [[ihcda-hcv]]. IHCDA also points renters to energy bill help (the Energy Assistance Program) and to local Community Action Agencies [[ihcda-renters]].

## Help lines

- **Indiana 2-1-1:** dial **2-1-1** for free, confidential help finding local resources, 24 hours a day [[ihcda-renters]]. HUD says to press 6 for homeless services [[hud-help]].
- **Tenant Information Hotline:** **317-327-2228**. Call right away if you think your landlord will file an eviction or you've received an eviction notice [[indy-edp]].
- **Housing discrimination:** report it to HUD's Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity at **1-800-669-9777** [[hud-help]].

## Avoid rental scams

The FTC's main warning signs [[ftc-scams]]:

- **Rent far below the area's rents,** especially with pressure to decide fast.
- **The "owner" can't show the place** and asks for money first.
- **Payment only by wire transfer, gift cards or crypto.** The FTC says that's a scam every time, and the money is usually gone for good.
- **Copied listings.** Search the address with the owner's or company's name. If the same address shows up with a different owner, or isn't on the management company's own site, be suspicious.
- **Requests for your SSN or credit score before you've agreed to rent.** Landlords normally pull credit themselves.

To check who owns a property, the FTC suggests looking up county property records and matching them to the landlord's ID [[ftc-scams]]. IHA also tells voucher holders to see a unit in person before signing anything and to ask neighbors about the area [[iha-faq]]. Report scams at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, to local police and to the site where the ad appeared [[ftc-scams]].

## Move-in costs and getting your deposit back

Budget for an application fee, a security deposit and the first month's rent, and get every amount in writing before you pay. When you move out, Indiana law gives the landlord **45 days** after the lease ends and you return the unit to send back your deposit, minus any itemized deductions for unpaid rent, damage beyond normal wear, or unpaid utilities. You have to give the landlord a **mailing address in writing** first. If the landlord doesn't follow these rules, you can recover the full deposit plus reasonable attorney's fees [[ic-deposit]].

## Free legal help for renters

If you're facing an eviction, a deposit dispute or a landlord problem, these groups offer **free or low-cost legal aid**. They are not paid partners, and we earn nothing if you contact them. Most have income limits, so check whether you qualify.

- [Indiana Legal Services](https://www.indianalegalservices.org/): Indiana's largest nonprofit law firm, giving free civil legal help, including housing cases, to eligible low-income Hoosiers [[ils]].
- [Indiana Legal Help](https://indianalegalhelp.org/): free plain-language guides, forms and a directory for finding legal help in Indiana [[ilh]].
- [Neighborhood Christian Legal Clinic](https://www.nclegalclinic.org/): an Indianapolis nonprofit offering free or low-cost legal services to people with limited incomes [[nclc]].
- [ABA Free Legal Answers](https://www.abafreelegalanswers.org/): ask a civil legal question online and a volunteer lawyer answers it for free, if you qualify [[aba-fla]].
- [LawHelp.org](https://www.lawhelp.org/): find nonprofit legal aid providers and free legal information in your state [[lawhelp]].
- **Already facing eviction in Indianapolis?** The City's [Tenant Advocacy Project / Eviction Diversion Program](https://www.indy.gov/activity/tenant-advocacy-project) and the Tenant Information Hotline (**317-327-2228**) can help right away [[indy-edp]].
