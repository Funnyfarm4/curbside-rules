## The short version

The City of Phoenix's clear path for an old hot tub is its **transfer stations**: **drain it, cut it into fourths**, bring the **registration or title**, and drop it off. If it isn't cut into fourths, you pay a **$50 penalty** [[phx-ts]]. Phoenix trash customers get their first ton each month free [[phx-ts]].

Bulk trash pickup isn't a good fit: its page doesn't mention spas, and its weight and length limits rule out a whole tub [[phx-bulk]]. Your other options are a **City dumpster rental** [[phx-dr]] or a **junk-removal crew** that does the tear-down and haul for you.

## Why bulk trash isn't the answer

Phoenix's appointment-based bulk trash pickup doesn't list hot tubs or spas as accepted or banned. But it tells you **not** to include [[phx-bulk]]:

- **Metal or car parts over 20 pounds or over 4 feet long**
- **Construction or demolition materials over 25 pounds or over 4 feet long**
- **Glass** such as windows, shower doors and mirrors
- **Household hazardous waste, including pool chemicals**
- More than **10 cubic yards** per residence

A full-size spa is far bigger and heavier than those limits. If you're thinking of setting cut-up panels or the wood surround out for bulk, call Public Works at **602-262-6251** first [[phx-bulk]]. Each household gets **4 bulk pickups a year**, booked at least 3 business days ahead [[phx-bulk]]. More: [Phoenix bulk trash pickup guide](/phoenix/bulk-trash/).

## Option 1: Cut it up and take it to a City transfer station

**The spa rules** [[phx-ts]]:

1. **Cut it into fourths.** Boats and spas that aren't cut into fourths get a **$50 penalty fee**.
2. **Bring proof of ownership:** the spa's **registration or title**.

**Free monthly load.** A Phoenix resident paying full-service trash fees can drop off **one load up to 1 ton (2,000 pounds) each calendar month at no charge** [[phx-ts]]. At the scale house, show:

- A copy of your **city services (water, sewer, trash) bill** showing an active, current account, and
- A **photo ID** with a name or address matching the bill. No substitutions [[phx-ts]].

**Fees beyond the free load** [[phx-ts]]:

- Over 1 ton: leave a deposit, weigh out, and pay **$55 a ton** for the extra.
- All other loads: **$55 a ton, $40 minimum.** Cash, credit and debit accepted; no checks or money orders.
- **Deposits are cash only.** They're required when a vehicle and trailer weigh more than 9,000 pounds for a resident with their water bill (6,000 pounds otherwise), and usually run **$65 to $180** for residential loads.
- Secure your load: the unsecured-load penalty is **$20**.

**Locations** [[phx-ts]]:

- **27th Avenue Transfer Station,** 3060 S. 27th Ave., Phoenix 85009 (south of Lower Buckeye Rd.)
- **North Gateway Transfer Station,** 30205 N. Black Canyon Hwy., Phoenix 85085 (half a mile northeast of the Dixileta Dr. exit, exit 220)

**Hours:** Monday to Friday 5:30 a.m. to 5 p.m., Saturday 6 a.m. to 3 p.m., closed Sunday [[phx-ts]]. Both close on major holidays, including **Thanksgiving and the day after**, Christmas and New Year's Day; Christmas Eve hours are 6 a.m. to noon [[phx-ts]].

## Option 2: Rent a dumpster from the City

If you're tearing out the tub along with a deck or patio, Phoenix Public Works rents roll-off dumpsters to residents with an **active city services account** that's current [[phx-dr]].

- **Price:** **$165 per pull** plus **$55 per ton** at pickup [[phx-dr]]
- **Sizes:** 10, 20, 30 or 40 yards; a customer service specialist confirms the size after you request [[phx-dr]]
- **Rental period:** up to **10 days**, with an extra fee beyond that [[phx-dr]]
- You sign a liability release and service contract before delivery, and **hazardous waste is prohibited** [[phx-dr]]

The rental page doesn't mention spas, so tell the specialist you're disposing of one when they call. Putting a private dumpster in the street instead? See [Do you need a dumpster permit in Phoenix?](/phoenix/dumpster-permit/)

## Leftover spa chemicals

Pool chemicals are household hazardous waste and can't go out with bulk trash [[phx-bulk]]. See [Phoenix hazardous waste and electronics disposal](/phoenix/hazardous-waste-electronics/) for the City's household hazardous waste options.

## Don't dump it

Phoenix asks residents to report illegal dumping in alleys, dumpsters, vacant lots and open desert. If it's happening now, call **911** for emergencies or Crime Stop at **602-262-6151**; otherwise call Public Works at **602-262-6251** [[phx-bulk]].
