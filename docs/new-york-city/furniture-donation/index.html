Free Furniture Donation Pickup in NYC (2026)

Habitat ReStore offers free NYC furniture pickup if you meet item minimums. Housing Works charges a small fee. What they refuse, plus the DSNY fallback.

Last checked against official sources: Nov 7, 2026 · Updated Nov 7, 2026

Quick answer

  • Start with donateNYC. DSNY's donateNYC program and directory help you find nearby places that take used goods, including several organizations that handle furniture. nyc.gov (DSNY)[1] nyc.gov (donateNYC)[2]
  • Free pickup with a minimum: Habitat for Humanity's NYC and Westchester ReStore offers a free pickup if you have enough large furniture or appliances (6 in Manhattan, the Bronx, Queens or Westchester; 10 in Brooklyn, Staten Island or Southeast Queens). Habitat NYC and Westchester[4]
  • Paid but flexible: Housing Works picks up furniture in excellent condition for a small fee based on your location and the items. They ask for at least five pieces and require photos. Housing Works[7]
  • Condition is everything: Habitat, Housing Works and Big Reuse all turn away stained, torn, damaged or soiled pieces, and Habitat's drivers can refuse items at the door. Habitat NYC and Westchester[5] Habitat NYC and Westchester[4] Housing Works[7] Big Reuse[8]
  • Turned down? DSNY will haul furniture from the curb for free, up to 6 items per collection day, with no appointment. See our NYC couch guide for set-out rules. nyc.gov (DSNY)[12] nyc.gov (DSNY)[13]

The short version

If your furniture is clean and in good shape, Habitat for Humanity's NYC and Westchester ReStore will pick it up for free, as long as you have enough large pieces and they pass review Habitat NYC and Westchester[4] Habitat NYC and Westchester[3]. Housing Works will also come to you, for a small fee based on your location and the items, if you have at least five pieces in excellent condition Housing Works[7].

DSNY's donateNYC program and directory are the City's official tools for finding nearby reuse organizations nyc.gov (DSNY)[1] nyc.gov (donateNYC)[2].

The catches are condition, quantity and timing. Charities turn away stained, torn or damaged pieces Habitat NYC and Westchester[5] Housing Works[7], Habitat needs several items at once Habitat NYC and Westchester[4], and Housing Works asks you to wait 2 to 3 business days just to hear back Housing Works[7]. If you're moving this week, the pieces are upstairs with no elevator, or a charity said no, skip to what to do if they won't take it.

Option 1: Habitat for Humanity ReStore (free pickup)

Habitat NYC and Westchester's ReStore accepts gently used furniture, appliances, cabinetry, building materials and other household items, and donations are tax-deductible Habitat NYC and Westchester[3]. They describe the pickup as a free service for people in their service area who have a large quantity they cannot transport themselves Habitat NYC and Westchester[4]. They also ask you to consider a monetary donation to help cover operating costs Habitat NYC and Westchester[3].

  • Item minimums: 6 large furniture or appliance items if the pickup is in Manhattan, the Bronx, Queens or Westchester; 10 if it is in Brooklyn, Staten Island or Southeast Queens Habitat NYC and Westchester[4].
  • Stairs: an elevator is required if items are more than one flight up (tell them about outdoor steps too). Otherwise you have to bring the pieces down to an accessible level Habitat NYC and Westchester[4].
  • What they take: clean, cosmetically attractive furniture with no rips, tears, stains or pet damage. That includes upholstered pieces in good condition and hardwood tables (dining tables should come with chairs), nightstands, dressers, bed frames, coffee and end tables, bookshelves and children's tables and chairs. They do not take cribs Habitat NYC and Westchester[5].
  • What they don't take: mattresses or box springs, clothing, pianos or organs, and a longer list of building materials and hazardous items in their guidelines Habitat NYC and Westchester[5].
  • They can still say no. Habitat may reject a request for oversized, overstocked, unmarketable or unsuitable items, and drivers can refuse pieces for wear, odor, staining, weight or a difficult removal Habitat NYC and Westchester[4].
  • How to schedule: use Habitat's online pickup form. They say they review requests and get back to you within 4 to 5 business days Habitat NYC and Westchester[3]. The scheduler sends a confirmation email within an hour, but that email is not an approval; the pickup stays tentative until they finish the review Habitat NYC and Westchester[4].
  • To cancel: email them or call (646) 808-0158 Habitat NYC and Westchester[4]. For business donations or questions, Habitat lists 646-876-9460 and restore@habitatnycwc.org Habitat NYC and Westchester[3].

Dropping off instead? Email photos to restore@habitatnycwc.org with DROP OFF in the subject line. They say someone will reply within 48 hours about whether the ReStore can take the items Habitat NYC and Westchester[3]. The store is at 470 Nepperhan Ave., Yonkers, NY 10701, (332) 777-5060, Tuesday to Saturday 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., plus the first and third Sunday of the month from noon to 4 p.m. It is closed Mondays Habitat NYC and Westchester[6].

Option 2: Housing Works (paid pickup)

Housing Works picks up furniture for a small fee based on your location and what you're donating Housing Works[7].

  • Minimum: at least five pieces of furniture, so this fits a room clear-out better than a single sofa Housing Works[7].
  • Photos are required with the request Housing Works[7].
  • Lead time: after you submit the form, allow 2 to 3 business days for a response, depending on volume. A team member then contacts you to schedule Housing Works[7].
  • What they take: furniture in excellent condition, plus clean bagged apparel, boxed or bagged accessories and shoes, antiques, art, decorative items, boxed housewares, jewelry, lighting, unusual finds, clean rugs up to 6 feet by 9 feet (with occasional exceptions), and up to 4 boxes of books (no larger than 24 by 24 inches) Housing Works[7].
  • What they will not take: furniture that isn't structurally sound or needs reupholstering; upholstery with multiple stains, fading, tears or pet hair; wood or other furniture with surface damage; pieces with missing parts (shelves, doors, casters, legs, cushions); office furniture; mattresses, box springs, futons and day beds; obsolete media stands or cabinets; large glass-top dining tables; Formica, wood-composite or plywood pieces; IKEA furniture (with occasional exceptions); electronics older than two years; used household appliances; large appliances; and medical equipment Housing Works[7].
  • Declined on pickup day: the most common reasons are worn, damaged or stained upholstery, a removal path that is too small, furniture that isn't structurally sound, pet hair and sun fading Housing Works[7].

You can also drop off gently used goods at any Housing Works thrift shop during store hours Housing Works[7].

Option 3: The Salvation Army (check your ZIP)

The Salvation Army's donation site describes a free furniture and household-goods pickup. Schedule it by entering your ZIP code at satruck.org or by calling 1-800-SA-TRUCK (1-800-728-7825) The Salvation Army (satruck.org)[10]. The Manhattan Adult Rehabilitation Center points donors to the same number and website The Salvation Army Manhattan Adult Rehabilitation Center[11].

Pickup availability is local, so confirm that trucks serve your address before you count on it The Salvation Army (satruck.org)[10].

In general, Salvation Army thrift stores accept unbroken furniture such as sofas, chairs, coffee and end tables, desks, dining furniture, cabinets, beds and mattresses, along with working (not built-in) appliances The Salvation Army (satruck.org)[10]. They generally will not take broken, rusty or dirty appliances, any gas appliance, built-in ovens, stoves, dishwashers or microwaves, TVs older than 5 years, large console TVs and stereos, auto parts, or paint and chemicals The Salvation Army (satruck.org)[10].

Option 4: Big Reuse (Brooklyn drop-off; large-volume pickups)

Big Reuse takes furniture at its Gowanus Reuse Center, 1 12th St., Brooklyn, NY 11215, daily from 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. Do not leave items after 5 p.m. Big Reuse[8]. Large furniture, appliances and building materials are accepted only at Gowanus, not at the Bushwick shop Big Reuse[8].

  • Furniture rules: pieces must be clean and undamaged. They do not take used couches or upholstered chairs unless those pieces are new, bed-related items (frames, headboards, bunk beds, mattresses, box springs, linens), breakable composite or particleboard, or unframed glass Big Reuse[8].
  • Pickup: they ask you to contact them for large-volume pickups. Email donate@bigreuse.org or call 718-725-8925 Big Reuse[8].
  • The Bushwick shop at 378 Troutman St. takes smaller items (clothing, home goods, books, accessories, kitchenware and media) daily from 11 a.m. to 7 p.m. Big Reuse[8].

Hour Children: call before you go

Hour Children's own thrift-shop page currently says it is not accepting donations while it works through inventory Hour Children[9]. When donations are open, they ask you to call 718-433-4724 to schedule a pickup of furniture and larger items, and they do not accept recliners, beds or mattresses, large appliances, medical equipment, electronics, books, exercise machines, cribs or several other baby items Hour Children[9]. Do not leave donations outside when they are closed; they say that creates a hazard and can bring heavy fines Hour Children[9]. Hour Children appears in the City's donateNYC directory nyc.gov (donateNYC)[2].

If no charity will take it

  1. DSNY curbside collection (free): NYC residents get free removal of large furniture such as bed frames, bookcases, couches and sofas, desks, dressers, and tables and chairs nyc.gov (DSNY)[12]. You can set out up to 6 items per collection day, and pick-up appointments are no longer offered nyc.gov (DSNY)[13]. Put mostly metal or rigid plastic furniture out with recycling, and everything else out with trash on your trash-only day, not your recycling day. Take pieces apart if you can, and bundle smaller parts with tape or twine nyc.gov (DSNY)[12] nyc.gov (DSNY)[13].
  2. Mattresses have extra rules (they must be sealed in a plastic bag) and separate fines. See our NYC mattress guide nyc.gov (DSNY)[12].
  3. Set-out timing, placement and the sofa-bed rule are covered in our NYC couch and furniture guide.

Charity said no, or can't come in time?

Charities turn down stained, worn or hard-to-reach pieces, and free pickups often need several matching items plus a few days' notice. A junk-removal crew can take everything, from any floor, usually within days. Check that they serve your ZIP and confirm the price before you book.

Frequently asked questions

Who picks up donated furniture for free in NYC?
Habitat for Humanity's NYC and Westchester ReStore offers a free pickup if you meet its item minimums and the pieces pass review. The Salvation Army's national donation site also describes a free furniture pickup you schedule by ZIP code or by calling 1-800-SA-TRUCK; check whether trucks serve your address. Habitat NYC and Westchester[4] The Salvation Army (satruck.org)[10] The Salvation Army Manhattan Adult Rehabilitation Center[11]
Does Housing Works pick up furniture in NYC?
Yes. Housing Works comes to you for a small fee based on your location and what you're donating. They want at least five pieces of furniture, photos with the request, and a reply typically takes 2 to 3 business days. Housing Works[7]
How many items do I need for a Habitat ReStore pickup?
At least 6 large furniture or appliance items if you live in Manhattan, the Bronx, Queens or Westchester, and at least 10 if you live in Brooklyn, Staten Island or Southeast Queens. Habitat NYC and Westchester[4]
Will a charity pick up furniture from upstairs in NYC?
Habitat requires an elevator if items are more than one flight of stairs up; otherwise you have to bring them down to an accessible level. Housing Works lists removal paths that are too small as a top reason pieces get declined on pickup day. Habitat NYC and Westchester[4] Housing Works[7]
Can I donate a mattress or a worn couch in NYC?
Usually no. Habitat does not accept mattresses or box springs, and Housing Works does not accept mattresses, box springs, futons or day beds. Housing Works also turns down upholstery with stains, fading, tears or pet hair. Big Reuse will not take used couches or upholstered chairs unless they are new, and it does not take beds or bedding. Use DSNY curbside collection instead; mattresses have extra bagging rules. Habitat NYC and Westchester[5] Housing Works[7] Big Reuse[8] nyc.gov (DSNY)[12]
What if no charity will take my furniture?
Set it out as a large item for DSNY. NYC residents get free curbside removal of furniture such as couches, desks, dressers, tables and chairs, up to 6 items per collection day, and you cannot book an appointment. nyc.gov (DSNY)[12] nyc.gov (DSNY)[13]
