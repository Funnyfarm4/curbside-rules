Where to Drop Off Electronics, Paint and Hazardous Waste in New York City (2026)

DSNY's five Special Waste Drop-Off sites take paint, batteries, e-waste, oil and more, Tuesday to Saturday. Addresses, per-visit limits and residency rules.

Quick answer

  • Five year-round sites, one in each borough, open Tuesday to Saturday, 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. They close on legal holidays and during severe weather. nyc.gov (DSNY)[1]
  • NYC residents only. No businesses or commercial vehicles, and you may be asked for proof of residency (NYS driver license or utility bill). nyc.gov (DSNY)[1]

The short version

New York City residents can drop off paint, batteries, electronics and other harmful household products at one of five year-round DSNY Special Waste Drop-Off sites, one in each borough. They're open Tuesday to Saturday, 9 a.m. to 3 p.m., and closed on legal holidays and during severe weather nyc.gov (DSNY)[1].

These items are "dangerous and illegal to dispose of with trash or recycling," in DSNY's words nyc.gov (DSNY)[1].

Where the sites are

Some sites may not be visible from the road nyc.gov (DSNY)[1].

  • Bronx, Hunts Point: Farragut Street and the East River, next to the Fulton Fish Market. Enter on Farragut Street, off Food Center Drive.
  • Brooklyn, Greenpoint: 459 North Henry Street (drop-off site near 530 Kingsland Avenue). The entrance is off Kingsland Avenue, north of Greenpoint Avenue.
  • Manhattan, Lower East Side/Two Bridges: 74 Pike Slip between Cherry Street and South Street, under the Manhattan Bridge.
  • Queens, College Point: 30th Avenue between 120th and 122nd Streets, at the northwest corner of DSNY's Queens District 7 garage.
  • Staten Island, Fresh Kills: off the Muldoon Avenue exit of the southbound West Shore Expressway, past the DSNY security booth.

What you can bring

From DSNY's list nyc.gov (DSNY)[1]:

  • Aerosols
  • Batteries: automotive, rechargeable (lead acid, Li-Ion, Ni-MH, Ni-ZN, Ni-Cd) and single-use (alkaline, lithium)
  • E-waste, including all cords, cables and wiring
  • E-cigarettes and vape pens
  • Fluorescent light bulbs and CFLs
  • Motor oil and transmission fluid (up to 10 quarts per visit)
  • Motor oil filters (up to two per visit)
  • Paint (up to five gallons per visit)
  • Passenger car tires (up to four per visit)
  • Mercury-containing devices (up to two per visit)
  • Skin lightening products

Rules at the site

  • Residents only. No businesses or commercial vehicles. You may be asked for proof of NYC residency nyc.gov (DSNY)[1].
  • Do it yourself. DSNY staff can give instructions and help, and the City recommends casual clothes and work gloves nyc.gov (DSNY)[1].
  • Batteries: bag them individually or cover the terminals with clear tape (never opaque tape). Alkaline batteries should not be bagged or taped nyc.gov (DSNY)[1].

What counts as e-waste

Only items covered by the NYS Electronic Equipment Recycling and Reuse Act count: computers and accessories, TVs and video equipment, phones, tablets and e-readers, game consoles and their cords. Most household appliances (microwaves, refrigerators), digital cameras, radios, calculators, GPS devices, power tools, medical equipment and batteries aren't covered e-waste, though they may still have special disposal rules nyc.gov (DSNY)[2].

Other ways to dispose

DSNY lists these options for e-waste nyc.gov (DSNY)[2]:

  • a retail or non-profit drop-off location near you
  • a community e-waste event
  • a SAFE Disposal event
  • ecycleNYC, for apartment buildings with 10 or more units
  • a manufacturer takeback program

SAFE Disposal events: DSNY's fall 2026 series ran one event per borough, from September 20 to October 24 nyc.gov (DSNY)[3]. All the listed dates have passed, and we didn't find newer dates, so use the year-round sites or check nyc.gov/safedisposal or call 311 nyc.gov (DSNY)[3].

What these sites don't cover

Furniture, mattresses and bulky household junk aren't on DSNY's drop-off list nyc.gov (DSNY)[1].

Frequently asked questions

Where can I drop off paint and batteries in NYC?
At one of DSNY's five Special Waste Drop-Off sites: Hunts Point in the Bronx, Greenpoint in Brooklyn, Lower East Side/Two Bridges in Manhattan, College Point in Queens, or Fresh Kills on Staten Island. They're open Tuesday to Saturday, 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. nyc.gov (DSNY)[1]
How much paint can I bring to a DSNY drop-off site?
Up to five gallons per visit. nyc.gov (DSNY)[1]
Do I need to prove I live in NYC?
You may be asked for proof of NYC residency, such as a NYS driver license or a utility bill. The sites are for NYC residents only. nyc.gov (DSNY)[1]
How do I prepare batteries for drop-off?
To prevent fires, bag batteries individually or cover the terminals with clear tape, not opaque tape. Alkaline batteries are accepted too and should not be bagged or taped. nyc.gov (DSNY)[1]
What counts as e-waste in NYC?
Under the NYS Electronic Equipment Recycling and Reuse Act: computers, monitors, keyboards, printers, TVs, DVD players and cable boxes, cell phones and tablets, game consoles and their cords. Most appliances, digital cameras, radios, calculators, GPS devices, power tools and batteries are not covered e-waste, though they may have their own rules. nyc.gov (DSNY)[2]
Are there other ways to get rid of electronics in NYC?
DSNY lists retail or non-profit drop-off locations, community e-waste events, SAFE Disposal events, ecycleNYC for buildings with 10 or more units, and manufacturer takeback programs. nyc.gov (DSNY)[2]
