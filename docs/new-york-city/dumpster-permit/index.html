Do You Need a Dumpster Permit in NYC? (2026)

In NYC the carting company, not you, gets the DOT permit for a dumpster on the street. Which permit fits your job, placement rules, and pods.

The short version

A dumpster on a New York City street or sidewalk needs a NYC DOT permit, and the container must be commercial grade NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[1]. The part that surprises most people: you don't apply for it. Permits must be obtained by a carting company or general contractor, not by the property or business owner NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[1].

So your job is to hire a carting company registered with DOT (or a registered general contractor) and confirm it's pulling the permit for your address.

Which permit fits your job?

Cleaning out a home or business: Commercial Refuse Container (up to 5 days)

  • Who it's for: homeowners or businesses clearing out bulk items NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[1].
  • Who applies: the carting company, which must be registered with DOT. It places the container NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[1].
  • How long: each permit covers one container at one location for up to 5 consecutive days nyc.gov (NYC DOT)[2] NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[1].
  • Swaps: if the container fills up and is exchanged within the 5 days, no new permit is needed. A second container at the same spot needs its own permit nyc.gov (NYC DOT)[2].
  • How fast: registered permittees can usually get one the same business day or the next nyc.gov (NYC DOT)[2].
  • What it can't hold: perishable waste, or construction material from a job that needs a Department of Buildings (DOB) or DOT construction permit NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[1] Rules of the City of New York (American Legal Publishing)[3].

Renovating with a DOB permit: Construction Debris Container (30 to 90 days)

  • Who it's for: worksites, such as a major building renovation, that require a DOB permit (or a DOT construction permit) NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[1] nyc.gov (NYC DOT)[2].
  • Who applies: only registered general contractors or carting companies registered with DOT NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[1] nyc.gov (NYC DOT)[2].
  • How long: typically 20- or 40-yard containers, or several mini containers, on the street for up to 90 days. The permit must be posted at the worksite for the whole project NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[1].

Moving: no storage pods on the street

Storage containers or "pods" used for non-construction activity, like temporary storage or shipping furniture and personal goods, are not permitted on City streets NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[1]. City rules also bar placing a storage box or a metal shipping container (a CONEX box) on any street unless the DOT Commissioner authorizes it Rules of the City of New York (American Legal Publishing)[3].

Do you even need a dumpster?

DSNY picks up up to 6 large items per collection day from the curb for free, with no appointment nyc.gov (DSNY)[5]. It also takes material from small do-it-yourself projects as bulk items. For a major construction or renovation project, DSNY says to arrange a dumpster through a private trash removal service, and construction waste from professional contractors can't go out as residential garbage nyc.gov (DSNY)[4]. Where the dumpster can't go

City rules for commercial refuse containers say they can't be placed Rules of the City of New York (American Legal Publishing)[3]:

  • in any No Stopping, No Standing, No Parking Anytime or Authorized Parking area
  • within 15 feet of a hydrant
  • in the corner area (the building line extended to the curb) or within 10 feet of either side of it
  • on, or blocking, a crosswalk or pedestrian ramp
  • within 5 feet of railroad tracks

They also can't block subway entrances, utility access points, fire alarms, traffic signals, street signs, bus stops or shelters, or water and gas shut-off valves without permission Rules of the City of New York (American Legal Publishing)[3].

The permit doesn't reserve a parking space or give any parking privileges, so the container still needs a legal spot at the curb nyc.gov (NYC DOT)[2].

What to check when it arrives

  • Placement: parallel to the curb, sticking out no more than 9 feet into the roadway Rules of the City of New York (American Legal Publishing)[3].
  • Street protection: planks, skids, plating or tires under each steel wheel or roller, in place the whole time Rules of the City of New York (American Legal Publishing)[3].
  • Markings: the owner's name, address and phone number in letters at least 3 inches high on both long sides, and reflective markings on all four sides Rules of the City of New York (American Legal Publishing)[3].
  • Clear paths: sidewalks, gutters, crosswalks, bike lanes and driveways must be kept clear, with spilled debris cleaned up promptly Rules of the City of New York (American Legal Publishing)[3].

DOT also checks requested dates against special events and will flag a conflict to the applicant nyc.gov (NYC DOT)[2].

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit for a dumpster in NYC?
If it goes on a City street or sidewalk, yes. NYC DOT requires a Construction Debris or Commercial Refuse Container permit, and the carting company or general contractor must get it, not the property owner. NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[1]
Can a homeowner put a dumpster in front of their house in NYC?
Yes, for cleaning out bulk items from a home, through a registered carting company. The company places a commercial-grade container and pulls a permit for up to 5 consecutive days. It can't hold perishable waste or construction material from a job that needs a DOB or DOT construction permit. NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[1]
How long can a dumpster stay on the street in NYC?
Up to 5 consecutive days on a Commercial Refuse Container permit. If it fills up, the carting company can swap it within those 5 days without a new permit. A Construction Debris Container permit for a DOB-permitted job runs 30 to 90 days. nyc.gov (NYC DOT)[2] NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[1]
Does a dumpster permit reserve a parking spot?
No. DOT says the Commercial Refuse Container permit carries no parking privileges and doesn't let the container owner reserve a space. nyc.gov (NYC DOT)[2]
Can I put a storage pod on the street in NYC?
No. NYC311 says storage containers or pods used for non-construction activity, like temporary storage or shipping personal goods or furniture, are not permitted on City streets. NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[1]
Where can't a dumpster go in NYC?
Not in No Stopping, No Standing, No Parking Anytime or Authorized Parking areas, within 15 feet of a hydrant, in the corner area, or on or blocking a crosswalk or pedestrian ramp. It must sit parallel to the curb and stick out no more than 9 feet. Rules of the City of New York (American Legal Publishing)[3]
