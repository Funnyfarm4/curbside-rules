How to Get Rid of Construction and Renovation Debris in NYC (2026)

DSNY takes up to 6 items of DIY renovation debris per collection day, but never contractor waste. The rules, prep, reuse options and dumping fines.

The short version

New York City's Department of Sanitation (DSNY) will take renovation debris only from small do-it-yourself projects, and only up to 6 large items per collection day nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[1]. If you hired a contractor, or the job is a major renovation, the debris is not residential garbage: you need a private dumpster or hauler nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[1] NYC311[3].

So the real question is who did the work and how much there is. A weekend bathroom refresh can usually go out over a few trash days. A gut renovation can't.

Does your job count as DIY?

DSNY calls a project do-it-yourself if it is small in scale and done without the help of professionals, like hired workers or contractors nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[1]. NYC311 puts it more narrowly: the curbside allowance is for repair work in your own one- or two-family home when you don't hire anyone to help NYC311[3]. It also says work on income properties (3-family homes and larger), fee-for-service jobs and contractor material must go to a private carter or dumpster NYC311[3].

The same split applies to wood: debris from construction or home improvement is treated as commercial waste unless you did the work yourself and didn't hire anyone NYC311[4].

How to set out DIY debris for DSNY

Pickup is free, and there's no appointment; DSNY says large-item pickup appointments are no longer offered nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[2].

  1. Count your items. You can set out up to 6 large items at the curb per collection day nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[1]. NYC311 adds that each must be light enough for two people to lift and small enough to fit in a garbage truck NYC311[3].
  2. Sort by material. Items that are mostly metal or plastic go out with recycling. Everything else goes out with trash nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[1], on your trash-only day, not your recycling day nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[2].
  3. Large pieces that don't fit in a bin, like cabinetry, drywall, siding, sinks, toilets and tubs, follow the large-item rules above nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[1].
  4. Wood and flooring: treated wood (lumber, particle board, plywood and other composites) is trash, not compost, and so are vinyl and laminate flooring and tile nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[1]. Bundle anything that won't fit in a bin in bales no more than 2 feet high and 4 feet long, tied with twine or rope (not nylon line or tape), with nails removed NYC311[4]. Set it out between 6 PM and midnight the night before your trash day NYC311[4].
  5. Glass: windows and mirrors can't be recycled; put them out with trash. Tape up large glass, double-bag broken glass, and pull exposed nails and other sharp objects nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[1].

Needs special handling: appliances with CFCs, harsh cleaners and paint, insulation, and thermostats nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[1]. Asbestos is hazardous waste and must never go out with trash or recycling; DSNY says to use a hazardous-material disposal company nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[1].

For appliances, see our NYC refrigerator guide. For leftover paint and chemicals, see NYC hazardous waste and electronics drop-off.

Hired a contractor or doing a big renovation?

DSNY is clear: construction and demolition waste from professional contractors cannot be discarded as residential garbage, and the contractor or resident must arrange a dumpster through a private trash removal service nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[1]. For major projects that generate a lot of waste, it gives the same advice even if you're doing the work yourself nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[1].

Before you sign, ask your contractor whether debris removal is in the price. DSNY says private contractors should arrange disposal of debris like heaters, sinks and tubs nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[1].

Check the hauler is legit. The Business Integrity Commission (BIC) licenses and registers waste haulers in NYC. Carters that only remove demolition, construction, alteration or excavation debris get a BIC registration, and anyone must have a license or registration before running a waste removal business nyc.gov (Business Integrity Commission)[5]. Ask for the hauler's BIC number.

Too much debris, or hired a contractor?

DSNY won't take renovation waste from a contractor or a big project, and the DIY limit is 6 items per collection day. A junk-removal crew can load and haul it in one trip. Confirm they serve your ZIP, what materials they take, and the price before you book.

Reuse what's still good

DSNY points to reuse before disposal and lists the donateNYC Exchange, Big Reuse, the Rebuilding Together NYC Rebuild Center and Habitat for Humanity's NYC ReStore as places to reuse or exchange building materials nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[1].

Big Reuse (Gowanus, Brooklyn) takes building materials, furniture and appliances at its Gowanus Reuse Center, 1 12th St., Brooklyn, daily 10 AM to 5 PM; large furniture, appliances and building materials are accepted only at Gowanus, not its Bushwick store Big Reuse[8]. Its guidelines include Big Reuse[8]:

  • Cabinets: complete sets only, sink base included, all hardware and shelving, doors attached; plywood or high-end brands, no particleboard
  • Lumber: free of fasteners, in job-lot quantities over 4 feet long; sheet goods at least 4 by 4 feet
  • Tile new in the box; salvaged hardwood flooring nail-free, at least 200 sq ft
  • Doors without holes, cracks or rot (no flat-panel hollow-core doors); windows new in box
  • Plumbing fixtures clean and working, with no cracks, chips, stains or rust

It won't take vinyl siding or sheetrock Big Reuse[8]. Questions go to donate@bigreuse.org or 718-725-8925 Big Reuse[8].

Don't dump it

NYC311 lists construction debris among the things it's illegal to dump on public or private property NYC311[6]. The owner and driver of the vehicle are both legally responsible, fines for the owner run $4,000 to $18,000, and the vehicle may be impounded NYC311[6] nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[7]. Call 911 for dumping in progress, or report past dumping to 311 NYC311[6].

Frequently asked questions

Will DSNY pick up construction debris?
Only from small do-it-yourself projects done without hired workers or contractors. You can set out up to 6 large items per collection day, with mostly metal or plastic items going out with recycling and the rest with trash. Waste from professional contractors can't be discarded as residential garbage. nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[1]
Can I put drywall, cabinets or a toilet out on the curb in NYC?
Yes, if you did the work yourself. DSNY lists cabinetry, drywall, siding, sinks, toilets and tubs as large items from DIY projects that follow the large-item rules, up to 6 items per collection day. nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[1]
What if my contractor did the work?
Then DSNY won't collect it. The contractor or resident must arrange a dumpster through a private trash removal service, and private contractors should handle disposal of things like heaters, sinks and tubs. nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[1] NYC311[3]
Does the DIY rule apply to apartment buildings?
NYC311 describes the 6-item allowance for repair work in your own one- or two-family home, and says work on income properties (3-family homes and larger), fee-for-service work and contractor material must be handled by a private carter or dumpster. NYC311[3]
How do I get rid of lumber and old flooring in NYC?
Treated wood such as lumber, plywood and particle board goes out as trash, not compost, as do vinyl and laminate flooring and tile. Bundle anything that doesn't fit in a bin in bales no more than 2 feet high and 4 feet long, tied with twine or rope, with nails removed. NYC311[4] nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[1]
What is the fine for dumping construction debris in NYC?
NYC311 says fines for the owner of a vehicle used for illegal dumping range from $4,000 to $18,000, and DSNY says the vehicle may be impounded. NYC311[6] nyc.gov (Department of Sanitation, DSNY)[7]
