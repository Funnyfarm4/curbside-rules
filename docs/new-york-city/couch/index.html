How to Get Rid of a Couch in NYC: DSNY Bulk Rules (2026)

DSNY hauls couches and furniture free from the curb, no appointment. Which night to set it out, the 6-item limit, sofa bed rules, and paid options.

Quick answer

  • Free curbside pickup, no appointment. DSNY removes couches, sofas and other large furniture from residential buildings at no charge. Appointments are no longer offered. nyc.gov (DSNY)[2] NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3]
  • Which night: a fabric or wood couch goes out the night before your trash (bulk) day, not your recycling day. Mostly metal or rigid plastic furniture goes out the night before your recycling day. nyc.gov (DSNY)[1] NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3]

The short version

New York City will haul away your old couch for free. There's nothing to book: DSNY no longer takes pick-up appointments for large items nyc.gov (DSNY)[2]. You just need to put it at the curb on the right night, in the right spot NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3].

For a regular fabric or wood couch, that means between 6 p.m. and midnight the night before your trash (bulk) collection day, not your recycling day NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3] nyc.gov (DSNY)[1].

Option 1: Free DSNY curbside collection

DSNY gives NYC residents free curbside removal of large furniture, including bed frames, bookcases, couches and sofas, desks, dressers, and tables and chairs nyc.gov (DSNY)[1]. The service covers residential buildings NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3].

  • Cost: free nyc.gov (DSNY)[1].
  • Appointment: none. Appointments are no longer offered and can't be scheduled nyc.gov (DSNY)[2] NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3].
  • Limit: up to 6 items at the curb per collection day nyc.gov (DSNY)[2]. With a lot of furniture, set it out over several collection days NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3].

Trash night or recycling night?

DSNY splits furniture by what it's made of nyc.gov (DSNY)[1] NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3]:

  • Trash night: couches and sofas, EZ chairs, wood tables, and anything made of wood or wood composites like particle board or fiberboard. These are not recyclable, so set them out before your trash-only day, not your recycling day nyc.gov (DSNY)[1] NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3].
  • Recycling night: furniture that's mostly metal or rigid plastic, like metal bed frames, cabinets, or aluminum lawn furniture with plastic webbing NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3].
  • Glass: glass furniture, windows and mirrors can't be recycled. Set them out with trash, tape up large glass, and double-bag broken glass nyc.gov (DSNY)[1].

Find your night

Look up the collection schedule for your address (DSNY's collection schedule tool) and find your bulk trash ("large item") day NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3]. The couch goes out the night before.

How to set it out

  1. Take it apart if you can. DSNY asks you to disassemble furniture before setting it out, and to bundle smaller pieces and tape or tie them with twine nyc.gov (DSNY)[1].
  2. Sofa bed or pullout? Remove the metal frame and put it out with metal recyclables. The rest of the sofa goes out with trash NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3].
  3. Time it. Put it at the curb between 6 p.m. and midnight the night before collection NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3].
  4. Right spot. Place it at the curb or in your alley if that's your designated collection area. It can't be in the street, extend in front of another property, or block people walking. Items left on private property are "unserviceable" and DSNY can't pick them up NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3].
  5. Bed bugs? Put a sign on the couch saying it has bed bugs, so no one takes it NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3].

If something isn't collected, NYC311 can help: call 311 or 212-NEW-YORK (212-639-9675) NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3].

Moving out or renovating?

  • Clearing out an apartment: the 6-item limit still applies, so a full move-out can take several collection days nyc.gov (DSNY)[2] NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3].
  • Renovation debris from a do-it-yourself project can go out as bulk items too, but for a major construction or renovation project DSNY says to arrange a dumpster through a private trash removal service nyc.gov (DSNY)[4].
  • A dumpster on the street needs a DOT permit, and the carting company gets it, not you. Storage pods for moving or storing furniture aren't permitted on City streets NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[5]. See our NYC dumpster permit guide.

Option 2: When DSNY pickup won't work

DSNY's free bulk collection is for residential buildings. NYC311 points non-residential buildings to its separate "Waste Disposal for Businesses" rules NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3]. And DSNY only takes what's at the curb. If you can't get a couch down the stairs, can't wait for your collection night, or have more than 6 pieces to move, a paid junk-removal crew can carry it out for you.

Frequently asked questions

Will NYC pick up my couch for free?
Yes. NYC residents get free curbside removal of large items like couches and sofas from residential buildings. There's no appointment; you set it out on the right night. nyc.gov (DSNY)[1] NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3] nyc.gov (DSNY)[2]
What night do I put a couch out in NYC?
A couch that isn't mostly metal or rigid plastic goes out between 6 p.m. and midnight the night before your bulk trash ("large item") collection day, not your recycling day. Look up your address in DSNY's collection schedule to find it. NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3] nyc.gov (DSNY)[1]
Do I need to schedule a bulk pickup in NYC?
No, and you can't. DSNY and NYC311 both say pick-up appointments for large items are no longer offered and cannot be scheduled. nyc.gov (DSNY)[2] NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3]
How many items can I put out at once?
Up to 6 large items per collection day. If you're clearing out a whole apartment, spread them over several collection days. nyc.gov (DSNY)[2] NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3]
Why didn't DSNY take my couch?
Common reasons: it went out on the recycling night instead of the trash night, it was left on private property instead of at the curb or alley, it was in the street or blocking the sidewalk, or it was over the 6-item limit. Items on private property are "unserviceable" and won't be collected. NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3]
What about a couch with bed bugs?
You must put a sign on it saying it has bed bugs, so nobody takes it. NYC311 (portal.311.nyc.gov)[3]
