Nashville fridge disposal: Sheriff's curbside pickup (615-880-3897), a free scrap-metal drop-off at Metro centers, or Habitat ReStore if 7 years old or newer.

The short version

You have three official ways to get rid of a refrigerator or freezer in Nashville:

  1. Sheriff's Office curbside pickup. Refrigerators and freezers are on the Davidson County Sheriff's Office list of items it removes Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1]. Call 615-880-3897 Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[2].
  2. Free drop-off at a Metro Convenience Center. One bulk scrap metal item, including a refrigerator, is accepted at no charge, and it gets recycled nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3].
  3. Donate it to Habitat's ReStore if it's 7 years old or newer and fully working. Free pickup is available for ground-floor items Habitat for Humanity of Greater Nashville (habitatnashville.org)[6].

Option 1: Sheriff's Office curbside pickup

  1. Call 615-880-3897. You don't need the Sheriff's website contact form for bulk item removal Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[2]. The homeowner or tenant has to make the request. You'll be asked to list the items, the service address, a phone number and the resident's name Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].
  2. Put it at the front curb by 6:30 a.m. on your date. Crews work from the front curb only Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].
  3. Give it time. Crews have up to seven business days from your date, not counting weekends and holidays Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].

The same call can cover other appliances on the list: washers and dryers, ranges, microwave ovens, water heaters, air conditioners and freezers Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].

Who can't use it: crews are prohibited on apartment, condo and townhome complexes, private roads, gated communities and business properties; residents of complexes are asked to put items at the front curb by the main entrance. Bulk items that resulted from an eviction can't be removed by law Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].

Recycling note: Metro says scrap metal isn't recycled through curbside pickup but is recycled at a Convenience Center nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3].

Option 2: Free drop-off at a Convenience Center

Residents can drop off one bulk scrap metal item, or up to three trash bags of scrap metal, at no charge. Metro's scrap metal list includes refrigerators, dishwashers, ovens and ranges, washers and dryers and other large household appliances nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3].

Locations, all open Tuesday to Saturday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. and closed on Metro holidays nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[5]:

  • Anderson Lane: 939A Anderson Ln, Madison, TN 37115
  • East: 943A Doctor Richard G. Adams Dr, Nashville, TN 37207
  • Ezell Pike: 3254 Ezell Pk, Nashville, TN 37211
  • Omohundro: 1019 Omohundro Pl, Nashville, TN 37210

Before you go nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4]:

  • Bring a Tennessee driver's license showing you live in Davidson County.
  • You unload it yourself, so bring a helper and a dolly.
  • No commercial vehicles (including any with company advertising or commercial tags) and no commercial waste.
  • Not sure about an item? Use the Waste Wizard in Nashville's Waste and Recycling App or call 615-924-2633.

Empty it first. Take out all food and tape the doors shut before you move it.

Option 3: Donate a newer, working fridge

Habitat for Humanity of Greater Nashville's ReStore accepts refrigerators, freezers and other large appliances if they are Habitat for Humanity of Greater Nashville (habitatnashville.org)[6]:

  • 7 years old or younger
  • Clean, in 100% working order, with all parts

It can't accept gas appliances or dishwashers, and final approval is at the discretion of its donation staff Habitat for Humanity of Greater Nashville (habitatnashville.org)[6].

  • Drop off: 414 Harding Place, Ste 110, Nashville, TN 37211, Tuesday to Saturday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Habitat for Humanity of Greater Nashville (habitatnashville.org)[6]
  • Free pickup for large or heavy items located on the ground floor; items must be disassembled. Scheduling depends on demand, but pickups generally happen within two weeks. Call the Nashville donation hotline at (615) 942-1290 Habitat for Humanity of Greater Nashville (habitatnashville.org)[6].
  • Need it faster, or it's upstairs? Habitat offers a priority paid pickup through its partner ReSupply for items that need pickup within 48 hours, need disassembly, or aren't on the first floor. ReSupply charges a fee based on the number of items Habitat for Humanity of Greater Nashville (habitatnashville.org)[6].

What about the refrigerant?

Household refrigerators and freezers usually go to disposal with their refrigerant still inside. Under federal rules, the final person in the disposal chain, such as a scrap metal recycler or landfill, is responsible for making sure the refrigerant is recovered. If a fridge arrives already emptied of refrigerant, that facility needs a signed statement with the name and address of whoever recovered it and the date U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (epa.gov)[7]. Don't cut refrigerant lines yourself; ask the facility or hauler how they handle it.

Frequently asked questions

Will Nashville pick up an old refrigerator?
Yes, through the Davidson County Sheriff's Office bulk item removal. Refrigerators and freezers are on its list of authorized items. Call 615-880-3897 to get a date, then put it at the front curb by 6:30 a.m. Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1] Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[2]
Where can I drop off a refrigerator for free in Nashville?
At any of Metro's four Convenience Centers. Residents can drop off one bulk scrap metal item, such as a refrigerator, or up to three bags of scrap metal at no charge. Bring a Tennessee driver's license showing a Davidson County address. nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3] nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4]
Does Nashville recycle refrigerators picked up at the curb?
Metro says scrap metal isn't recycled through curbside pickup but is recycled at a Convenience Center, so dropping a fridge off there is the way to make sure it's recycled. nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3]
Do I need to remove the Freon before getting rid of a fridge?
Federal rules put the duty to make sure refrigerant is recovered on the final person in the disposal chain, such as the scrap metal recycler or landfill. If the fridge no longer holds refrigerant, that facility needs a signed statement saying who recovered it and when. Ask the facility or hauler how they handle it. U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (epa.gov)[7]
Will Habitat for Humanity pick up a refrigerator in Nashville?
Habitat's ReStore accepts refrigerators and freezers that are 7 years old or newer, clean, complete and in 100% working order, and offers free pickup of large items on the ground floor, generally within two weeks. Call the Nashville donation hotline at (615) 942-1290. Final approval is up to its donation staff. Habitat for Humanity of Greater Nashville (habitatnashville.org)[6]
Can I put a refrigerator out after an eviction?
No. The Sheriff's Office says it's prohibited by law from removing bulk items that resulted from an eviction. Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1]
