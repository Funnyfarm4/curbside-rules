Do You Need a Dumpster Permit in Nashville? (2026)

A dumpster or storage container in Nashville's right-of-way needs an NDOT permit: $10 a day (max $2,000), applied for on ePermits 2 business days ahead.

The short version

If you're putting a dumpster, roll-off, trailer or storage container in the Metro Nashville right-of-way, you need a permit from the Nashville Department of Transportation and Multimodal Infrastructure (NDOT). It costs $10 a day, up to $2,000, and putting one out without a permit carries a $30-a-day violation fee nashville.gov (NDOT)[1].

You apply on NDOT's ePermits site at least 2 business days before the container arrives nashville.gov (NDOT)[2], and every application needs a traffic control plan nashville.gov (NDOT)[3].

Which permit applies

NDOT lists a Trailer / Dumpster fee for "placing a trailer, dumpster, or storage unit in the Metro right-of-way (ROW)" nashville.gov (NDOT)[1]:

  • Permit fee: $10 per day, not to exceed $2,000
  • Violation fee: $30 per day

If the container also closes a traffic lane, sidewalk or bike lane, NDOT's closure permits apply. Each is $160 for up to 5 days, plus $10 for each day after the 5th. Closing without a permit triggers a triple fee of $480 (plus $30 a day after 5 days), and permit holders who break the rules can be charged $50 per violation per day nashville.gov (NDOT)[1].

If your work needs more than one type of right-of-way permit, NDOT says you may qualify for a single unified right-of-way permit; email the NDOT permits office to ask nashville.gov (NDOT)[1].

How to apply

  1. Apply on ePermits. NDOT says all right-of-way permits must be applied for through ePermits (ePermits.nashville.gov) nashville.gov (NDOT)[2].
  2. Apply early. Permits must be applied for at least 2 business days before the actual start date nashville.gov (NDOT)[2].
  3. Attach a traffic control plan. NDOT requires one with every permit submittal, even when no lane is closed, plus pedestrian and cyclist control wherever a sidewalk, crosswalk or bike lane is affected nashville.gov (NDOT)[3]. NDOT's list of standard layouts includes a Layout for a Dumpster, showing a container in a parking lane and in a travel lane nashville.gov (NDOT)[3] nashville.gov (NDOT)[4]. A site-specific, signed and sealed plan may be required if the standard layout doesn't match your street nashville.gov (NDOT)[3].
  4. Pay once approved. Payment is due in full after the application is approved, and no permit is issued until it's paid. Fees are non-refundable. NDOT takes cash, check (payable to "Metropolitan Government"), Visa or MasterCard nashville.gov (NDOT)[2], with a 2.3% convenience fee on card payments nashville.gov (NDOT)[2].

What the layout requires on the street

NDOT's dumpster layout carries general notes that apply to all its standard plans nashville.gov (NDOT)[4]:

  • Channelizing devices (cones, drums and the like) must follow the federal Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices.
  • Traffic control devices must have working, visible warning lights.
  • The contractor must provide a sidewalk or crosswalk closure, or a walkway bypass, wherever pedestrians are affected.
  • The City Traffic Engineer or inspectors may require more devices.

Contact

  • NDOT permits office: 615-862-8782, or use the email link on NDOT's permits page nashville.gov (NDOT)[2]
  • NDOT main line: 615-862-5000, Monday to Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. nashville.gov (NDOT)[1]

Remodeling? Debris can't go in your Metro trash cart

Metro says residential construction waste must be collected by a private hauler or contractor, which is the usual reason people rent a dumpster. Small do-it-yourself amounts can go to the Omohundro Convenience Center nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[5], at $20 for up to 2 cubic yards of construction and demolition material nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[6].

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit for a dumpster in Nashville?
You do if the dumpster, trailer or storage unit sits in the Metro right-of-way. NDOT charges a Trailer / Dumpster permit fee for that. nashville.gov (NDOT)[1]
How much is a dumpster permit in Nashville?
NDOT's Trailer / Dumpster permit fee is $10 per day, not to exceed $2,000. The violation fee is $30 per day. nashville.gov (NDOT)[1]
How do I apply for a Nashville dumpster permit?
All right-of-way permits go through NDOT's ePermits system, at least two business days before the start date, with a traffic control plan. Payment is due once the application is approved. nashville.gov (NDOT)[2] nashville.gov (NDOT)[3]
What if the dumpster blocks a traffic lane or the sidewalk?
NDOT has separate lane, sidewalk and bike lane closure permits at $160 for up to 5 days plus $10 a day after that, or a $480 triple fee if you close it without a permit. Closures longer than 7 continuous days need a stamped traffic control plan from a licensed engineer. nashville.gov (NDOT)[1]
Can I put remodeling debris in my Metro trash cart?
No. Metro says residential construction waste must be collected by a private hauler or contractor. Small do-it-yourself amounts can go to the Omohundro Convenience Center for $20, up to 2 cubic yards. nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[5] nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[6]
