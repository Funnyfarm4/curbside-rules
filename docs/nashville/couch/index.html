How to Get Rid of a Couch in Nashville (2026)

Nashville's Sheriff's Office picks up couches free at the curb (615-880-3897), or drop one free at a Metro Convenience Center. Rules, hours and limits.

The short version

You have two free official ways to get rid of a couch in Nashville:

  1. Free curbside pickup from the Davidson County Sheriff's Office. Sofas, couches and old furniture are on its list Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1]. Call 615-880-3897 to schedule Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[2].
  2. Drop it off at a Metro Convenience Center. Your first bulk item each day is free nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4].

Metro asks residents to consider donating usable furniture first, through secondhand stores or online marketplaces nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3].

Option 1: Free Sheriff's Office pickup

  1. Call 615-880-3897. You don't need the Sheriff's online contact form for bulk removal Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[2]. You'll be asked what you're setting out, the address, a phone number and the resident's name. The homeowner or tenant has to make the request Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].
  2. Put it at the front curb by 6:30 a.m. on your date. Front curb only Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].
  3. Nothing in bags. Take cushions out of trash bags, and keep glass or mirrored pieces (like a glass coffee table top or a mirror) out of the pile, since crews won't take them Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].
  4. Give it time. Crews have up to seven business days from your date, not counting weekends and holidays Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].

The same pickup also takes lawn furniture, baby furniture, mattresses, rugs and carpet, so you can clear out a room in one call Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].

Apartment, condo or townhome? Crews can't come onto private complexes, private roads, gated communities or business property. Put the couch at the front curb by the main entrance instead Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].

After an eviction: the Sheriff's Office is prohibited by law from removing bulk items that resulted from an eviction Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].

For the full list of what the Sheriff's crews take and don't take, see our Nashville bulk trash pickup guide.

Option 2: Drop it off at a Convenience Center

Metro lists couches, chairs and sofas, dressers, mirrors and tables as bulk items it accepts for disposal nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3]. All four Convenience Centers take bulk items and are open Tuesday to Saturday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., closed on Metro holidays nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[5]:

  • Anderson Lane: 939A Anderson Ln, Madison, TN 37115
  • East: 943A Doctor Richard G. Adams Dr, Nashville, TN 37207
  • Ezell Pike: 3254 Ezell Pk, Nashville, TN 37211
  • Omohundro: 1019 Omohundro Pl, Nashville, TN 37210

What it costs

nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4]

  • Free: one bulk item plus up to three bags of trash (30 gallons max each), once a day.
  • Extra furniture is charged by volume: under 1 cubic yard (no higher than the truck bed) is $10; between 1 and 8 cubic yards is $20 per cubic yard. No trailers longer than 12 feet, and no cargo vans or trailers at Anderson Lane.
  • Cash (not at Omohundro), check or card. Cards add 2.8%, minimum $1.95. Metro says prices are non-negotiable.

Before you go

nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4]

  • Bring a Tennessee driver's license showing you live in Davidson County.
  • You unload it yourself.
  • No commercial vehicles, including any vehicle with company advertising or commercial tags, and no commercial waste.

Mattress going too?

Mattresses aren't covered by the free daily bulk item at Convenience Centers; they're $12 each nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4]. The Sheriff's curbside pickup takes them free Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1]. See our Nashville mattress guide.

Frequently asked questions

Will Nashville pick up an old couch?
Yes. Sofas and couches are on the Davidson County Sheriff's Office list of items it removes free from the front curb. Call 615-880-3897 to get a date. Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1] Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[2]
How long does the Sheriff's couch pickup take?
Put the couch at the front curb by 6:30 a.m. on your date. Crews have up to seven business days from that date, not counting weekends and holidays. Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1]
Can I drop off a couch for free in Nashville?
Yes. Metro Convenience Centers take one bulk item, such as a couch, chair or sofa, plus three bags of trash free per visit per day. Additional items are charged by volume. nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4] nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3]
How much does it cost to dump furniture at a Nashville Convenience Center?
Beyond the free daily bulk item, loads under 1 cubic yard are $10 and loads between 1 and 8 cubic yards are $20 per cubic yard. Card payments add 2.8%, with a $1.95 minimum. nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4]
I live in an apartment. Can the Sheriff's Office take my couch?
Crews can't go onto apartment, condo or townhome complexes, private roads, gated communities or business property. Residents of those places are asked to put items at the front curb by the main entrance. Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1]
Can I put a couch out for pickup after an eviction?
No. The Sheriff's Office says it's prohibited by law from removing bulk items that resulted from an eviction. Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1]
