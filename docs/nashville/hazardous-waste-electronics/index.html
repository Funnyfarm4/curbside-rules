Where to Drop Off Electronics, Paint and Hazardous Waste in Nashville (2026)

Nashville takes household hazardous waste free at East and Ezell Pike, and electronics at three centers. Hours, ID rules, limits and what's not accepted.

  • Electronics: free at East, Ezell Pike and Omohundro, up to 100 pounds a day. Electronics are banned from Metro trash carts. nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2] nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3]

The short version

Davidson County residents can drop off household chemicals free at two Metro Convenience Centers, East and Ezell Pike, up to 15 gallons or 100 pounds per household per month nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[1]. Old electronics go free to East, Ezell Pike or Omohundro, up to 100 pounds a day nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2].

All four Convenience Centers are open Tuesday to Saturday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., and closed on Metro holidays nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4].

Where to go

  • East Convenience Center: 943A Doctor Richard G. Adams Dr, Nashville, TN 37207 nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[1]
  • Ezell Pike Convenience Center: 3254 Ezell Pk, Nashville, TN 37211 nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[1]
  • Omohundro Convenience Center: 1019 Omohundro Pl, Nashville, TN 37210 (electronics and cooking grease) nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2] nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[1]

The Anderson Lane center in Madison doesn't take hazardous waste or electronics nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3].

Rules at every center

  • Show a Tennessee driver's license with a Davidson County address nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3].
  • Unload it yourself nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3].
  • No commercial vehicles, including any vehicle with commercial advertising or commercial tags, and no commercial waste nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3].
  • One visit a day for each of hazardous waste and electronics nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3].

Hazardous waste: what you can bring

Metro's accepted list includes nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[1]:

  • Paint and finishes: oil-based paint, stain, varnish, paint thinner, adhesives and caulk
  • Car fluids: motor oil, antifreeze, brake fluid, transmission fluid, gasoline and windshield wiper fluid
  • Batteries: rechargeable, automobile and lead-acid
  • Bulbs: fluorescent tubes and CFLs
  • Yard and pest products: pesticides, insecticide, weed killer, rat and mouse poison, flea collars, pet spray and dip
  • Household cleaners: bleach, drain opener, oven cleaner, toilet bowl and tub cleaners, detergent, spot remover
  • Other: pool chemicals, lighter fluid, mercury thermometers, mothballs, shoe polish, car wax and cooking grease

Limit: 15 gallons or 100 pounds per household per month nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[1].

What they won't take

  • Latex or acrylic paint and water-based stains
  • Propane tanks
  • Unused or expired medications
  • Medical waste (Metro says needles can go in a red sharps container or a lidded two-liter bottle in your trash)
  • Ammunition and explosives
  • Business or industrial hazardous waste, or anything in a commercial or work vehicle
  • Waste from outside Davidson County nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[1]

How to transport it

Keep products in their original containers when you can. Otherwise use a plastic jug with a secure lid and label what's in it. Don't mix liquids. Pack containers in sturdy boxes or flat-bottom tote bags lined with newspaper to stop spills nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[1].

Latex paint goes in the trash (once it's dry)

Leave the lid off or mix in cat litter until the paint is dry, then put the container in your trash. Containers with liquid in them must be emptied first nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[5].

Electronics: what you can bring

Up to 100 pounds a day of household electronics, free, with valid Davidson County ID nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2]. Per visit that can include nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2]:

  • 1 computer system (computer and monitor), limited to one a month
  • 1 TV, no wider than 80 inches
  • 1 microwave, coffee machine or other kitchen appliance
  • 1 desktop copier, printer or scanner
  • 1 radio, CD player, DVD or VCR player, or stereo (not a cabinet system)
  • Phones, tablets, e-readers, keyboards, mice, cables, external drives, string lights, hair dryers and other small electronics

Electronics from businesses, truck or trailer loads, and electronics from out-of-county residents aren't accepted. Businesses should contact local e-waste recyclers, and some retailers run drop-off or take-back programs nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2].

Other things these centers handle

East and Ezell also take tires (free without rims, $5 each with rims, four a month), and East, Ezell and Omohundro take mattresses and box springs ($12 each, four a month) nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3]. If you have more than one carload

Every center lets you drop off 3 bags of trash and 1 bulk item free per day. Beyond that, loads are charged by volume: $10 for less than a cubic yard, or $20 per cubic yard for 1 to 8 cubic yards. Card payments carry a 2.8% fee (minimum $1.95) nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3]. You still have to load, drive and unload everything yourself nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3].

Clearing out a garage or whole house?

Metro's centers take chemicals and electronics you unload yourself, one visit a day, with weight and volume caps. If you're clearing out furniture, appliances and general junk too, a junk-removal crew can haul the rest in one trip. Confirm they serve your ZIP and check the price before you book.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I drop off household hazardous waste in Nashville?
At the Ezell Pike Convenience Center (3254 Ezell Pike, 37211) or the East Convenience Center (943A Doctor Richard G. Adams Drive, 37207). There's no fee. nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[1]
How do I get rid of latex paint in Nashville?
Latex and acrylic paint aren't accepted at the hazardous waste sites. Metro says to dry the paint out by leaving the lid off or using cat litter, then put the container in the trash. nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[1] nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[5]
Where can I recycle a TV or computer in Nashville?
At the East, Ezell Pike or Omohundro Convenience Center, free, up to 100 pounds a day. TVs can't be larger than 80 inches wide, and there's a limit of one computer system per month. nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2]
Can I put electronics in my trash cart in Nashville?
No. Electronic waste isn't allowed in waste containers under Metro Code 10.20.095 and should be recycled. nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2]
Do I need to live in Davidson County?
Yes. Convenience Centers ask for a Tennessee driver's license showing a Davidson County address, and they don't accept hazardous waste or electronics from out-of-county residents. nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3] nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[1]
Who do I call with questions?
Call 615-862-5000 for household hazardous waste questions. You can also use the Waste Wizard in Nashville's Waste and Recycling App or call 615-924-2633. nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[1] nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3]
