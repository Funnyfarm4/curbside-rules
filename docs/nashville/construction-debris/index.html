How to Get Rid of Construction and Remodeling Debris in Nashville (2026)

The short version

Construction debris is banned from Metro Nashville trash carts. Metro says residential construction waste must be collected by a private hauler or contractor, and only small do-it-yourself amounts can go to the Omohundro Convenience Center nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[1].

Your realistic options are: drop a small DIY load at Omohundro nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2], set out the few remodel items the Sheriff's curbside pickup takes Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[6], take bigger loads to a private C&D facility nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4], donate reusable materials Habitat for Humanity of Greater Nashville[9], or hire a crew (or rent a dumpster) to load and haul it. If a dumpster will sit on the street, see Do you need a dumpster permit in Nashville?.

Option 1: Omohundro Convenience Center (small DIY loads)

Of Metro's four convenience centers, only Omohundro takes construction and demolition material nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2] nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4].

  • Where: Omohundro Convenience Center, 1019 Omohundro Pl, Nashville, TN 37210 nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3]
  • When: Tuesday to Saturday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.; closed on Metro holidays nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3]
  • Fee: $20, one visit per day nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2]
  • Size limit: the bulky item page limits residents to 1 cubic yard nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4]; the convenience center fee table says loads can't exceed 2 cubic yards nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2].
  • Payment: check or any major credit or debit card (cards carry a 2.8% fee, minimum $1.95). Cash isn't accepted at Omohundro nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2].
  • Who can use it: Davidson County residents with a Tennessee driver's license showing they live in the county. You unload your own material, and commercial vehicles (including any vehicle with business advertising or commercial tags) and commercial waste aren't allowed nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2].

Heads-up on the limit: Metro's two pages disagree (1 vs. 2 cubic yards). We show the stricter 1 cubic yard; call Metro at 615-862-5000 before hauling more nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4] nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2].

Not accepted at any convenience center nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2]: asbestos (shingles, flooring and so on), brick, concrete, masonry, rock, rebar and dirt, roofing shingles, siding, gutters, plumbing, pallets, posts, poles, chain-link and railroad ties.

The free daily allowance (3 bags of trash and 1 bulk item) excludes construction and demolition material, and the per-yard volume pricing for 1 to 8 cubic yards says no construction or demolition material nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2].

Option 2: Davidson County Sheriff's curbside pickup (some items)

The Davidson County Sheriff's Office removes bulk items from the front curb, and its authorized list includes construction debris, lumber, fencing, pallets, shipping crates, toilets and water heaters, plus a mailbox or fence post with its concrete footing Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[6].

It's not authorized to take Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[6]:

  • Sheet rock/drywall
  • Roofing
  • Glass or mirrored items
  • Sand, dirt, rocks or concrete
  • Paint cans (even empty), household chemicals and tires
  • Anything in bags

How to book: call 615-880-3897; you don't need the online contact form Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[7]. The homeowner or tenant must call and give the items, address, phone number and resident's name. Set items at the front curb by 6:30 a.m.; crews have up to seven business days from your date Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[6]. Crews can't go onto apartment, condo or townhome complexes, private roads, gated communities or business property Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[6].

Option 3: Larger loads to a private C&D facility

For more than a DIY load, Metro lists these sites and says to contact each for fees nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4]:

  • Triune Residuals Management (Hermitage), 3516 Central Pike, Hermitage, TN 37076, at the MS-COT landfill, 615-573-8004. Takes construction and demolition debris from building, remodeling, repair and demolition, including roofing shingles, glass, lumber, brick, concrete and masonry, scrap metal, soil, rock and rebar nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4].
  • American Farms, 2132 Smith Springs Rd, Nashville, TN 37217, 615-573-0293. Takes dirt, clay, topsoil, rock, gravel, concrete (with or without rebar), brick and block, plus trees, stumps, brush and other green waste nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4].
  • Middle Tennessee Environmental Center (MTEC), 4651 Amy Lynn Drive, Nashville, TN 37218, at WM's Southern Services Landfill nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4].

Metro also links a list of other recycling processors for source-separated loads such as metal or concrete; some may take commercial loads only nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4].

Need a crew to load and haul it?

Metro's own drop-off takes only about a truck bed of DIY debris, one visit a day, at a single center, and the Sheriff's curbside pickup won't take drywall, roofing or concrete. A junk-removal crew can load a whole remodel in one trip. Confirm they serve your ZIP, what materials they take, and the price before you book.

Option 4: Donate reusable materials to Habitat ReStore

Habitat for Humanity of Greater Nashville's ReStore takes building materials in good condition Habitat for Humanity of Greater Nashville[9]:

  • Lumber 6 feet or longer and trim 4 feet or longer
  • Plywood and sheetrock/drywall in full or half sheets
  • Rolled roofing, metal roofing and bundled shingles in their original packaging only
  • Doors in good condition, and kitchen cabinets and vanities with doors and shelving and no damage
  • New tile, hardwood, vinyl or ceramic flooring in original packaging (at least 100 square feet of one style)
  • Full, unused cans of water-based paint less than a year old

It won't take building materials with nails, screws, staples or hooks, damaged or water-damaged doors, or countertops without their base cabinets Habitat for Humanity of Greater Nashville[9].

Drop off at the Nashville ReStore, 414 Harding Place, Ste 110, Nashville, TN 37211 (615-942-1291), Tuesday to Saturday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Habitat for Humanity of Greater Nashville[8]. Free pickup is offered for large or heavy items on the ground floor (disassembled); in Davidson County, book online or call the donation hotline at 615-942-1290. Pickups generally happen within two weeks Habitat for Humanity of Greater Nashville[9].

Remodeling a kitchen? Habitat's DeConstruct crews remove cabinets, claw-foot tubs, doors and other donated building materials at no charge in its five-county area. Email deconstruction@habitatnashville.org to schedule Habitat for Humanity of Greater Nashville[8]. Metro's waste department also encourages deconstruction and reuse over demolition, and flags lead and asbestos as common hazards to identify first nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[5].

Paint and other leftovers

  • Latex or water-based paint: dry it out (leave the lid off or add cat litter) before it goes in the trash nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[1].
  • Oil-based paint, stains, solvents and gasoline: free at Metro's two household hazardous waste sites (East and Ezell Pike), up to 15 gallons or 100 pounds per visit nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[1] nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2]. See Nashville hazardous waste and electronics drop-off.
  • Scrap metal and old appliances: one bulk scrap metal item free at a convenience center nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4].

Frequently asked questions

Can I put construction debris in my trash cart in Nashville?
No. Construction debris is on Metro's list of materials banned from the brown trash cart. Residential construction waste has to be collected by a private hauler or contractor, and small do-it-yourself amounts can go to the Omohundro Convenience Center. nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[1]
Where can I dump construction debris in Nashville?
For small DIY loads, the Omohundro Convenience Center at 1019 Omohundro Pl takes construction and demolition material for $20 per visit, Tuesday to Saturday from 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., with a Tennessee driver's license showing a Davidson County address. For larger loads, Metro lists Triune Residuals Management at 3516 Central Pike in Hermitage and the Middle Tennessee Environmental Center at 4651 Amy Lynn Drive. nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2] nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3] nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4]
How much construction debris can I take to a Nashville convenience center?
Metro's bulky item page limits residents to 1 cubic yard of DIY construction and demolition waste, at Omohundro only. The convenience center fee table lists $20 per visit and says loads can't exceed 2 cubic yards, so the two pages disagree; plan on 1 cubic yard. The other centers' per-yard volume pricing excludes construction material. nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4] nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2]
Will the Davidson County Sheriff pick up construction debris?
Its curbside bulk item list includes construction debris, lumber, fencing and pallets, but it won't take sheet rock or drywall, roofing, glass, sand, dirt, rocks or concrete, paint cans, or anything in bags. Call 615-880-3897 to schedule; items go at the front curb by 6:30 a.m. Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[6] Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[7]
Where can I get rid of concrete and brick in Nashville?
Metro's convenience centers don't accept concrete, brick, rock, masonry or dirt. Metro lists American Farms at 2132 Smith Springs Rd (615-573-0293) for dirt, rock, gravel, concrete (including with rebar), brick and block. Contact them for fees. nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[2] nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4]
Can I donate leftover building materials in Nashville?
Yes. Habitat for Humanity of Greater Nashville's ReStore takes lumber 6 feet or longer, trim 4 feet or longer, full and half sheets of plywood and drywall, packaged roofing, doors and complete kitchen cabinets, with no nails or screws. Drop off at 414 Harding Place, Tuesday to Saturday 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., or call 615-942-1290 about a free pickup. Habitat for Humanity of Greater Nashville[9] Habitat for Humanity of Greater Nashville[8]
