How to Get Rid of a Hot Tub in Nashville (2026)

Nashville's Sheriff's Office takes hot tubs from the curb if cut in two pieces (615-880-3897). Set-out rules, drop-off fees, and what to do with spa chemicals.

The short version

Nashville's main official option for an old hot tub is the Davidson County Sheriff's Office bulk item removal. It's on the list of items crews remove, with one condition: the hot tub must be in two pieces Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1]. Call 615-880-3897 to get a date Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[2], and have both pieces at the front curb by 6:30 a.m. Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].

If you can't cut it up, can't get it to the front curb, or live somewhere crews can't go, a junk-removal crew is the usual answer. Metro's Convenience Centers charge for bulky loads by volume, but their pages don't mention spas, so call before you haul one there nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4].

Option 1: Sheriff's Office curbside pickup

  1. Drain it and cut it into two pieces. The Sheriff's Office says a hot tub must be in two pieces for pickup Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].
  2. Call 615-880-3897. You don't need the Sheriff's website contact form for bulk item removal Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[2]. The homeowner or tenant has to make the request. You'll be asked to list the items, the service address, a phone number and the resident's name Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1]. Mention the hot tub by name.
  3. Front curb by 6:30 a.m. Crews remove items from the front curb only Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].
  4. Give it time. Once you have a date, crews have up to seven business days from it to remove the items, not counting weekends and holidays Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].

What the crews won't take with it

Keep these out of the pile Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1]:

  • Household or hazardous chemicals, including leftover spa chemicals
  • Glass or mirrored items
  • Sheet rock/drywall and roofing
  • Sand, dirt, rocks or concrete, such as a concrete pad under the tub
  • Batteries and propane or gas tanks
  • Anything in bags

Wood from a deck around the tub may qualify: lumber, fencing and construction debris are on the accepted list Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1]. List everything when you call.

Who can't use it

  • Apartment, condo and townhome complexes, private roads, gated communities and business properties: crews are prohibited there. Residents of complexes are asked to put items at the front curb by the main entrance Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].
  • After an eviction: the Sheriff's Office is prohibited by law from removing bulk items that resulted from an eviction Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].

Option 2: Haul it to a Convenience Center (call first)

Metro says items too bulky for your curbside cart should go to a Convenience Center nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3]. Its fee table doesn't list hot tubs. Items not listed by material type are charged by volume nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4]:

  • Less than 1 cubic yard: $10, no higher than the level of the truck bed
  • Between 1 and 8 cubic yards: $20 per cubic yard. No construction or demolition material, no trailers longer than 12 feet, and no cargo vans or trailers at Anderson Lane

Because spas aren't named, call Metro's Waste Wizard line at 615-924-2633 before you load one nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4].

Before you go nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4]:

  • Bring a Tennessee driver's license showing you live in Davidson County.
  • You unload everything yourself.
  • No commercial vehicles (including any with company advertising or commercial tags) and no commercial waste.
  • Cash (not at Omohundro), check or card. Cards add 2.8%, minimum $1.95.

Locations, all open Tuesday to Saturday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. and closed on Metro holidays nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[5]:

  • Anderson Lane: 939A Anderson Ln, Madison, TN 37115
  • East: 943A Doctor Richard G. Adams Dr, Nashville, TN 37207
  • Ezell Pike: 3254 Ezell Pk, Nashville, TN 37211
  • Omohundro: 1019 Omohundro Pl, Nashville, TN 37210

Tearing out a deck or patio along with the tub? Metro points larger amounts of construction and demolition material to private disposal sites. See How to get rid of construction debris in Nashville nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3].

Leftover spa chemicals

Pool chemicals are on Metro's household hazardous waste list. Drop them off at no charge at the East or Ezell Pike Convenience Center, up to 15 gallons or 100 pounds per household per month. Questions: 615-862-5000 nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[6]. Full details: Nashville hazardous waste and electronics drop-off.

Can I donate it?

Not to Goodwill: Goodwill Industries of Middle Tennessee lists outdoor playground and pool equipment, including hot tubs, among items it can't accept Goodwill Industries of Middle Tennessee (giveit2goodwill.org)[7]. A working spa may be easier to sell or give away locally to someone who'll move it.

Frequently asked questions

Will Nashville pick up an old hot tub?
Yes, through the Davidson County Sheriff's Office bulk item removal, not Metro's trash trucks. Its list says a hot tub must be in two pieces for pickup. Call 615-880-3897 to get a date. Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1] Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[2]
Does the hot tub have to be cut up for the Sheriff's pickup?
Yes. The Sheriff's Office says the hot tub must be in two pieces. Both pieces go at the front curb by 6:30 a.m. on your date. Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1]
Can I take a hot tub to a Nashville Convenience Center?
Metro's pages don't list hot tubs either way. Items that aren't listed by material type are charged by volume ($10 under 1 cubic yard, $20 per cubic yard from 1 to 8), and loads of 1 to 8 cubic yards can't include construction or demolition material. Call Metro's Waste Wizard line at 615-924-2633 before you load it. nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4]
Will Goodwill take a hot tub in Nashville?
No. Goodwill Industries of Middle Tennessee lists outdoor playground and pool equipment, including hot tubs, among items it can't accept. Goodwill Industries of Middle Tennessee (giveit2goodwill.org)[7]
What do I do with leftover hot tub chemicals in Nashville?
Pool chemicals are on Metro's household hazardous waste list. Drop them off free at the East or Ezell Pike Convenience Center, up to 15 gallons or 100 pounds per household per month. The Sheriff's crews won't take household chemicals. nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[6] Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1]
