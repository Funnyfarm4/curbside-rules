How to Get Bulk Trash Picked Up in Nashville (2026)

Nashville's free bulk pickup is run by the Davidson County Sheriff's Office: call 615-880-3897, set items at the curb by 6:30 a.m. Or use a Metro drop-off.

The short version

Nashville's curbside bulk pickup isn't run by the trash department. It's a free service of the Davidson County Sheriff's Office (DCSO). Call 615-880-3897 to get a date Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[2], put the items at your front curb by 6:30 a.m. that day, and crews have up to seven business days from that date to take them away Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].

If you'd rather not wait, Metro Nashville's Department of Waste Services runs four Convenience Centers where Davidson County residents can drop off one bulk item free each day nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4] nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3].

Option 1: Sheriff's Office curbside pickup (free)

How to book

  1. Call 615-880-3897. The Sheriff's Office says you don't need to fill out its website contact form for bulk item removal; just call Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[2].
  2. Have this ready: a list of the items, the service address, a phone number, and the name of the resident whose home it is. The request has to come from the homeowner or tenant of that property Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].
  3. Note your date. Items must be at the curb by 6:30 a.m. on it. Crews then have up to seven business days to come; weekends and holidays don't count. The Sheriff's own example: a date of Jan. 3 means pickup by Jan. 12 Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].

Where to put it

  • Front curb only. The service removes items from the front curbside only Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].
  • Apartments, condos and townhomes: crews aren't allowed on private property such as apartment, condominium or townhome complexes, private roads, gated communities or business properties. Residents of those places can put items at the front curb by the main entrance Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].
  • Nothing in bags. Bagged items aren't taken Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].
  • Eviction items: by law, the Sheriff's Office can't remove bulk items that resulted from an eviction Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1].

What they take

From the Sheriff's list of authorized items Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1]:

  • Furniture: old furniture, sofas and couches, lawn furniture, baby furniture
  • Mattresses and box springs
  • Appliances: refrigerators, freezers, water heaters, ranges, washers and dryers, air conditioners, microwaves
  • Electronics: computers and monitors, TVs, copy machines
  • Carpet, carpet pads and rugs
  • Exercise equipment, bicycles, kids' toys, toilets
  • Lawn mowers, emptied of gas and oil
  • Lumber, fencing, pallets, shipping crates, and mailbox or fence posts with concrete footings
  • Construction debris (but see the exclusions below)
  • Car parts such as fenders, hoods, bumpers, transmissions, radiators and brake rotors
  • Swing sets, basketball goals and storage sheds, taken apart first
  • Hot tubs, cut into two pieces

What they won't take

Davidson County Sheriff's Office (sheriff.nashville.gov)[1]

  • Tires and motor oil
  • Household or hazardous chemicals, paint cans (even empty), batteries
  • Propane, gas or compressed air tanks
  • Glass or mirrored items
  • Sheetrock (drywall) and roofing
  • Sand, dirt, rocks or concrete
  • Trash cans, shopping carts or other businesses' products
  • Boats, cars, motorcycles and jet skis
  • Brush and tree limbs, which Metro picks up separately on a quarterly schedule

Option 2: Drop it at a Convenience Center

Metro says items too bulky for your curbside cart should go to a Convenience Center nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3].

Locations (all open Tuesday to Saturday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., closed Metro holidays) nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[5]:

  • Anderson Lane: 939A Anderson Ln, Madison, TN 37115
  • East: 943A Doctor Richard G. Adams Dr, Nashville, TN 37207
  • Ezell Pike: 3254 Ezell Pk, Nashville, TN 37211
  • Omohundro: 1019 Omohundro Pl, Nashville, TN 37210

What it costs nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4]:

  • Free: 1 bulk item plus 3 bags of trash (30 gallons max per bag), once a day. This free allowance excludes mattresses, tires on rims, and construction and demolition material.
  • Mattresses and box springs: $12 each, up to 4 a month (East, Ezell Pike and Omohundro only).
  • Everything else by volume: $10 for under 1 cubic yard (up to the level of the truck bed), or $20 per cubic yard for 1 to 8 cubic yards.
  • Appliances and scrap metal: one bulk scrap metal item, such as a refrigerator, washer, dryer or stove, is free nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3].
  • Paying: cash (not at Omohundro), check, or card, with a 2.8% card fee, $1.95 minimum.

Rules nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4]:

  • Bring a Tennessee driver's license showing you live in Davidson County.
  • You unload your own load.
  • No commercial vehicles, including any vehicle with company advertising or commercial tags, and no commercial waste.
  • Brush, leaves, concrete, dirt, brick, rock, roofing shingles, siding, pallets and car parts other than tires aren't accepted.

Construction and remodeling debris

Metro bans construction debris from the trash cart and says residential construction waste must be collected by a private hauler or contractor. Small do-it-yourself amounts can go to the Omohundro Convenience Center nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[6], for $20 for up to 2 cubic yards nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[4]. For bigger jobs, Metro's bulky item page lists private disposal sites; call them for fees nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3].

Donate first if you can

Before sending furniture to the landfill, Metro asks residents to consider donating it to secondhand stores or offering it on online marketplaces nashville.gov (Nashville Department of Waste Services)[3].

Want it hauled away?

The Sheriff's crews only take items from the front curb, can take up to seven business days after your date, and won't come onto apartment, condo or business property. If you can't get a couch or appliance to the curb, or you need it gone today, a junk-removal crew can carry it out from inside. Confirm they serve your ZIP and check the price before you book.
