How to Get Rid of Construction and Remodeling Debris in Austin (2026)

Austin bulk pickup won't take tile, sheet glass or remodeling debris. What it does take, TDS landfill rates, free concrete recycling, and ReStore donation.

The short version

Austin's curbside bulk collection is not a way to clear out a remodel: tile, mirrors, sheet glass, construction carpet and "other construction and remodeling debris" are all on its not-accepted list austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]. The City's Recycle and Reuse Center doesn't list construction debris among what it takes either austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[2].

Your realistic options are: put out the few items bulk pickup does take austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1], haul the rest to a landfill such as Texas Disposal Systems in Creedmoor Texas Disposal Systems[3], recycle clean concrete free Texas Disposal Systems[5], donate what's reusable Austin Habitat for Humanity[7], or hire a crew to load and haul it.

What Austin bulk pickup will and won't take

Bulk collection is for Austin Resource Recovery residential customers (single-family homes up to fourplexes), by appointment, up to three times a calendar year austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]. Book in the Austin Recycles app, online, or by calling Austin 3-1-1 at 512-974-2000 austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1].

Accepted remodel-type items include austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]:

  • Doors
  • Nail-free lumber
  • Pallets
  • Carpet (household)
  • Appliances (remove doors)

Not accepted austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]:

  • Tile (whole or broken)
  • Mirrors and sheet glass and other construction and remodeling debris
  • Carpet from construction or demolition
  • Hazardous materials, household trash, cardboard boxes and brush

Set-out rules: put items at the curb by 5:30 a.m. on your appointment date, in separate piles for metal and rigid plastic items and for non-metal items such as carpet and nail-free lumber. Don't bag or box anything (bags are treated as extra trash and may cost extra), and keep piles 5 feet from carts, mailboxes, fences, meters, hydrants and parked cars austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]. Option 1: Haul it to the TDS Landfill in Creedmoor

The Texas Disposal Systems (TDS) Landfill is a private facility at 3016 FM 1327, Creedmoor, TX 78610, in southeast Travis County just outside Austin Texas Disposal Systems[4] Texas Disposal Systems[5].

Hours: Monday to Saturday, 7 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. or sunset, whichever comes first; closed Sunday, Thanksgiving, Christmas Day and New Year's Day Texas Disposal Systems[4]. Office: 512-421-1363; customer care 800-375-8375 Texas Disposal Systems[4].

Prices on the TDS gate-rate page Texas Disposal Systems[3]:

  • Citizen drop-off (car, pickup or SUV): $66 minimum plus $12 per yard over 5.5 yards, plus fees and charges
  • Construction & demolition plant, small personal loads: $12.50 per cubic yard (businesses and large loads: $90 a ton, $375 minimum)
  • Untreated, unpainted lumber and brush (no metal or trash): $10 per cubic yard, $20 minimum, for loads under 5.5 yards
  • Scrap metal, water heaters and appliances without freon: no charge
  • Unsecured load: $35 (free tarp for pickup trucks), so tie down and cover your load
  • Landfilled loads also carry a state fee, fuel surcharge, environmental charge and other percentage charges

TDS says to call for pricing on asbestos and other special waste, and on reusable items and construction materials Texas Disposal Systems[3].

Heads-up on hours: the TDS concrete FAQ lists hours to 7 p.m., but its contact and gate-rate pages say 5:30 p.m. We show the earlier time; call 512-421-1363 before a late trip Texas Disposal Systems[5] Texas Disposal Systems[4].

Option 2: Recycle concrete, brick and rock for free

TDS accepts clean concrete (with or without rebar) and clean rock base such as brick, granite, tile and asphalt for recycling, and says it takes concrete and approved rock base at no charge at this time Texas Disposal Systems[5]. The gate-rate page lists concrete, rock, gravel, pavers, asphalt and road base without trash or contamination as no charge Texas Disposal Systems[3].

  • Keep it free of trash, wood, glass, plastic and hazardous waste, and tell the gatehouse attendant what you're bringing Texas Disposal Systems[5].
  • TDS may charge a fee if materials aren't properly prepared; for prep instructions call 800-375-8375 Texas Disposal Systems[5].
  • Concrete mixed with trash is landfilled at $60 a ton, 2-ton minimum, plus fees Texas Disposal Systems[3].

Option 3: Donate reusable materials to Habitat ReStore

Austin Habitat for Humanity's ReStores take new or gently used building materials Austin Habitat for Humanity[7], including:

  • Drywall, insulation, shingles, roofing tin, plywood, siding and trim Austin Habitat for Humanity[7]
  • Lumber over 36 inches (not painted, not under 36 inches, and no nails, screws or holes) Austin Habitat for Humanity[7]
  • Cabinets with drawers and doors intact, and countertops in good condition (no laminate or cultured marble) Austin Habitat for Humanity[7]
  • Interior and exterior doors in good condition (no painted doors, 8-foot doors or water damage) Austin Habitat for Humanity[7]
  • Unopened paint (less than a year old) and unopened Quikrete Austin Habitat for Humanity[7]

Free pickup: the ReStore offers free donation pickup if you can't drop off Austin Habitat for Humanity[6]. Drop-off at any of three stores Austin Habitat for Humanity[6]:

  • South Austin, 500 W Ben White Blvd, 512-478-2165
  • Northwest Austin, 13804 N US-183, 512-367-5795
  • San Marcos, 2521 S Interstate 35, 512-738-8383

Habitat's page says the donation drive closes one hour before the store closes in one place and thirty minutes in another, so plan to arrive at least an hour before closing Austin Habitat for Humanity[6].

Frequently asked questions

Will Austin bulk pickup take construction debris?
No. Austin Resource Recovery's not-accepted list includes tile, mirrors, sheet glass and other construction and remodeling debris, and carpet from construction or demolition. It does accept doors, nail-free lumber, pallets and household carpet, so some remodel leftovers can go out if you book an appointment. austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]
Where can I dump construction debris in Austin?
The City's Recycle and Reuse Center doesn't list construction debris among its accepted items. One option open to the public is the Texas Disposal Systems landfill at 3016 FM 1327 in Creedmoor, Monday to Saturday from 7 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. or sunset, whichever comes first. Car, pickup and SUV loads cost a $66 minimum plus fees. austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[2] Texas Disposal Systems[4] Texas Disposal Systems[3]
Where can I get rid of concrete for free in Austin?
Texas Disposal Systems recycles clean concrete (with or without rebar), rock, gravel, pavers, asphalt and road base at no charge, as long as there's no trash or other contamination mixed in. Tell the gatehouse attendant what you're bringing. Texas Disposal Systems[3] Texas Disposal Systems[5]
Can I donate leftover building materials in Austin?
Yes. Austin Habitat for Humanity's ReStores accept new or gently used building materials such as drywall, insulation, shingles, plywood, siding, lumber over 36 inches, intact cabinets and unpainted doors. They offer free donation pickup, or you can drop off at the South Austin, Northwest Austin or San Marcos store. Austin Habitat for Humanity[6] Austin Habitat for Humanity[7]
What do I do with leftover paint from a remodel in Austin?
Austin and Travis County residents can drop off paint and thinners free at the City's Recycle and Reuse Center by appointment. Curbside customers can also book a household hazardous waste pickup. austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[2] austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]
