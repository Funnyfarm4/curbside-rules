Where to Drop Off Electronics, Paint and Hazardous Waste in Austin (2026)

Austin takes paint, chemicals, batteries and electronics free at the Recycle and Reuse Center by appointment, or picks up HHW at home. Rules and limits.

The short version

Austin's main drop-off for paint, chemicals, batteries and electronics is Austin Resource Recovery's Recycle and Reuse Center. It's free for Austin and Travis County residents (except tires), but every visit needs an appointment, booked in the Austin Recycles app or online austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1].

If you're a City curbside customer, you can skip the trip: Austin will collect household hazardous waste at your home up to three times a year austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[2].

Option 1: Drop it off at the Recycle and Reuse Center

Booking

  • Appointment required. Austin and Travis County residents schedule through the Austin Recycles app or the City's website austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1].
  • Can't change a booking. To move it, cancel and book again, using the same method you booked with (app, the My Schedule web page, or the link in your confirmation email). Call Austin 3-1-1 if you have trouble canceling austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1].
  • Where: the City's driving-directions map for the center points to 2514 Business Center Dr, Austin, TX 78744 austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1].

Who can use it and what it costs

  • Free for Austin and Travis County residents, except tires, which are $6 per tire austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1].
  • Non-residents pay fees under Austin Resource Recovery's fee schedule austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1].
  • Businesses can't drop off hazardous waste. For business disposal, call 512-974-4335 austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1].

Household hazardous waste it takes

austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]

  • Aerosol cans
  • Automotive fluids and oil filters
  • Batteries (car and household)
  • Cleaning products
  • Cooking oil
  • Fluorescent light bulbs
  • Mercury
  • Paint and thinners
  • Pesticides and herbicides
  • Pool chemicals
  • Propane cylinders

Not accepted: any waste generated by a business, explosives (including ammunition), radioactive materials, and syringes or medical waste austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1].

How to pack it

austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]

  • Keep products in their original containers and don't mix them.
  • Use containers of 5 gallons or smaller.
  • Set small containers upright in sturdy boxes.
  • If something leaks, put it in another container and soak up the spill with cat litter.
  • No trash bags. Containers can break and mix, causing a reaction.

Electronics and appliances it takes

austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]

  • Televisions, computers, printers and fax machines
  • Cell phones and chargers
  • CDs, DVDs and floppy disks (throw away the jewel cases)
  • Microwaves and other kitchen appliances, stoves
  • Washers and dryers, water heaters, air conditioners
  • Lamps, exercise equipment, lawn mowers and weed eaters

The center also takes solar panels and battery storage systems, scrap metal, lawn furniture, clothing and housewares, and regular recyclables austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1].

What the center won't take

Furniture, mattresses, non-recyclable trash, Styrofoam packing peanuts, toilets, and brush or yard trimmings. Brush can go to Austin Water's Hornsby Bend Biosolids Management Plant, with no appointment needed austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1].

Free stuff while you're there

The center's ReUse Store gives away usable items other people dropped off, such as cleaning products, automotive fluids and art supplies. Austin ReBlend, a recycled flat paint made from paint collected at the center, is free too (appointment needed), and you can load free mulch yourself austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1].

Option 2: Have it picked up at home

Austin Resource Recovery's residential customers, meaning single-family homes and buildings of up to four units, can schedule up to three household hazardous waste collections a year. Book through the Austin Recycles app, online, or by calling Austin 3-1-1 (512-974-2000) austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[2].

What crews collect: aerosol cans, automotive fluids and oil filters, car and household batteries (lithium-ion only from laptops or smaller devices), cleaning products, cooking oil and grease, fluorescent bulbs, mercury, paint and thinners, pesticides and herbicides, pool chemicals, and propane cylinders up to 1 pound. Anything not on that list can't go through this service austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[2].

Set-out rules austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[2]:

  • Don't put it at the curb. Leave it where crews can reach it, out of rain and direct sunlight and away from heat, sparks or flame.
  • Containers must be sealed and clearly labeled; bag leaking containers separately in zip-top bags.
  • If you can, put everything in a recyclable box or tub (it won't be returned).
  • No single container over 5 gallons, and 30 gallons total per collection. Have more? Book another pickup or use the center.
  • Restrain your pets.
  • No commercial or industrial materials.

Electronics: for TVs, computers and other electronics, curbside customers can schedule an on-demand bulk collection instead austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[3]. See our Austin bulk collection guide for the set-out rules.

Batteries

Batteries should never go in a dumpster or any curbside cart. Take them to the Recycle and Reuse Center, book an at-home HHW pickup, or drop them at your nearest Austin library branch; the City says many branches accept them austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[3].

Clearing out a garage, closet or whole house?

The Recycle and Reuse Center takes chemicals and electronics, but not furniture, mattresses, toilets or general junk, and every visit needs an appointment. A junk-removal crew can haul the rest in one trip. Confirm they serve your ZIP and check the price before you book.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I drop off household hazardous waste in Austin?
At Austin Resource Recovery's Recycle and Reuse Center. An appointment is required; Austin and Travis County residents book through the Austin Recycles app or online. austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]
Is it free to drop off paint and chemicals in Austin?
Yes for Austin and Travis County residents. The center is free except for tires, which cost $6 each. Non-residents pay fees under the Austin Resource Recovery fee schedule. austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]
Will Austin pick up hazardous waste from my house?
Yes, if you're an Austin Resource Recovery curbside customer. You can schedule up to three household hazardous waste collections a year, with a 30-gallon limit per collection, through the Austin Recycles app, online or by calling 3-1-1. Don't set HHW at the curb; leave it somewhere crews can reach, out of rain and sun. austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[2]
How do I get rid of an old TV or computer in Austin?
The Recycle and Reuse Center accepts TVs, computers, printers, cell phones and other electronics by appointment. Curbside customers can also schedule an on-demand bulk collection for electronics. austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1] austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[3]
Can I take furniture or a mattress to the Recycle and Reuse Center?
No. The center doesn't accept furniture, mattresses, toilets, non-recyclable trash or brush. Brush can go to Austin Water's Hornsby Bend plant instead. austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]
