How to Get Rid of a Refrigerator in Austin (2026)

Austin bulk pickup takes fridges with the doors off, by appointment. Or drop one at the Recycle and Reuse Center, or donate a working one. Rules and limits.

The short version

Austin makes refrigerator disposal fairly easy if you live in a house or small building with City trash service:

  1. Book a bulk pickup. Appliances are accepted with the doors removed, by appointment, up to three times a year austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]. There's no extra charge; it's part of your utility bill's base fee austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[4].
  2. Drop it off at the City's Recycle and Reuse Center, by appointment, free for Austin and Travis County residents austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[2] austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[3].
  3. Donate it if it still cools: Austin Habitat ReStore takes working fridges and will pick them up free Austin Habitat for Humanity[6] Austin Habitat for Humanity[7].

The City's What Do I Do With? tool actually lists reuse first: its "best option" for a refrigerator is the Austin Reuse Directory, which helps you find places to donate, sell or repair used items austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[2].

Option 1: City bulk pickup

Austin Resource Recovery's on-demand bulk collection takes appliances (remove doors) austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]. The City recycles every appliance it collects this way, and says the recycling revenue helps keep customer fees down austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1].

Who qualifies

  • Single-family homes and buildings of up to four units served by Austin Resource Recovery, plus homes in certain Limited Districts austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]
  • Items set out in an alley, in front of a vacant lot, or in front of a business won't be collected austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]

If you're in a building with five or more units, this service doesn't cover you; ask your property manager how the building handles appliances, or see the hauling option below.

How to book

  1. Schedule in the Austin Recycles app, online through the City's My Schedule page, or by calling Austin 3-1-1 (512-974-2000) austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1].
  2. Select every item you'll set out. You'll get an email confirmation and, later, a reminder austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1].
  3. No appointment, no pickup austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1].

You get up to three bulk collections per calendar year austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]. Appointments can't be changed: cancel (using the same method you booked with, or the link in your confirmation email) and book again austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1].

What it costs

Nothing extra per pickup. The $24.90 monthly base fee on the solid waste section of your City of Austin utility bill pays for bulk collection, along with recycling, composting, brush and household hazardous waste pickup, and you're charged it whether or not you use those services austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[4].

How to set it out

austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]

  • Take the doors off the refrigerator or freezer.
  • Put it at the curb by 5:30 a.m. on your appointment date.
  • Sort your pile: the fridge goes with metal items and rigid plastics (appliances, electronics), separate from non-metal items and tires.
  • Keep it 5 feet away from carts, mailboxes, fences or walls, water meters, utility boxes, fire hydrants and parked cars, and not under low tree limbs or power lines.
  • Don't bag or box anything.

Missed? Different trucks may come at different times. If everything isn't collected by 4 p.m., call 3-1-1 or email Austin Resource Recovery after 4 p.m. that day and within 2 business days austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1].

Option 2: Drop it at the Recycle and Reuse Center

The City's What Do I Do With? tool lists the Recycle and Reuse Center as an option for refrigerators austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[2].

  • Address: 2514 Business Center Drive, Austin, TX 78744 austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[2]
  • Phone: 512-974-4373 (recycling and reuse) austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[2]
  • Appointment required. Austin and Travis County residents book online, in the Austin Recycles app, or by calling 3-1-1 austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[2] austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[3].
  • Cost: free for Austin and Travis County residents, except tires. Non-residents pay fees under the City's fee schedule austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[3].

The center's accepted electronics and appliances list also includes air conditioners, microwaves and other kitchen appliances, stoves, washers and dryers, and water heaters austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[3]. It doesn't take furniture, mattresses or toilets austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[3].

Option 3: Donate a working fridge

  • Austin Habitat for Humanity ReStore accepts used appliances in good working condition, clean and complete, but not appliances with cooling components that don't work, such as refrigerators and air conditioners Austin Habitat for Humanity[6]. Free donation pickup needs 3-4 days' lead time Austin Habitat for Humanity[7], or drop off at South Austin (500 W. Ben White Blvd., 512-478-2165) or Northwest Austin (13804 N. US-183, 512-367-5795) Austin Habitat for Humanity[7].
  • Goodwill Central Texas lists large appliances (refrigerators, freezers, air conditioners) among items it can't accept Goodwill Central Texas[8].
  • The Austin Reuse Directory is the City's top suggestion for finding other places to donate or sell a fridge austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[2].

Want it hauled away?

City bulk pickup is curbside only: you have to take the doors off and get the fridge to the curb by 5:30 a.m. yourself, and it's limited to homes of up to four units and three bookings a year. If you can't move it, live in a bigger building, or need it gone from inside the house, a junk-removal crew can carry it out for you. Confirm they serve your ZIP and check the price before you book.

Is there a utility buyback?

Not right now, as far as Austin Energy's website shows. Its residential Appliances and Equipment rebate page lists rebates for heat pump water heaters, variable-speed pool pumps and Power Partner backup batteries, and no refrigerator recycling offer austinenergy.com (Austin Energy)[5].

What about the Freon?

Under federal rules, the final person in the disposal chain, such as a scrap metal recycler or landfill, is responsible for making sure refrigerant is recovered before the appliance is disposed of. If an appliance arrives with no refrigerant left, that facility must keep a signed statement from whoever dropped it off U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (epa.gov)[9].

Frequently asked questions

Will Austin bulk pickup take a refrigerator?
Yes. Appliances are on Austin Resource Recovery's accepted bulk items list, with the doors removed. You need an appointment, and the fridge goes in the metal and rigid plastics pile at the curb by 5:30 a.m. on your date. austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]
Does Austin charge to pick up a refrigerator?
There's no separate fee per appointment. Bulk collection is paid for by the $24.90 base fee on the solid waste part of your City utility bill, which you're charged whether or not you use it. austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[4]
How many bulk pickups do I get in Austin?
Up to three bulk collections per calendar year for each address served by City curbside collection (single-family homes and up to fourplexes). austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[1]
Can I drop off a refrigerator at the Austin Recycle and Reuse Center?
Yes. The City's What Do I Do With? tool lists the Recycle and Reuse Center at 2514 Business Center Drive as an option for refrigerators. Every visit needs an appointment, booked online, in the Austin Recycles app or through 3-1-1, and it's free for Austin and Travis County residents. austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[2] austintexas.gov (Austin Resource Recovery)[3]
Does Austin Energy pick up old refrigerators?
Austin Energy's current appliance rebate page lists rebates for heat pump water heaters, pool pumps and backup batteries only. It doesn't list a refrigerator recycling or pickup offer. austinenergy.com (Austin Energy)[5]
Will a charity take a used fridge in Austin?
Austin Habitat for Humanity ReStore accepts used appliances in good working condition, but not appliances whose cooling doesn't work, and it offers free donation pickup with 3-4 days' lead time. Goodwill Central Texas lists large appliances such as refrigerators among items it can't accept. Austin Habitat for Humanity[6] Austin Habitat for Humanity[7] Goodwill Central Texas[8]
