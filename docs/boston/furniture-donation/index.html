Furniture Donation Pickup in Boston (2026)

Where to donate furniture in Boston: Habitat ReStore drop-off or paid pickup, New Life Furniture Bank, Salvation Army pickup, plus the free City fallback.

The short version

Boston has good places to donate furniture, but free pickup is limited. Habitat for Humanity's ReStore in West Roxbury and New Life Furniture Bank in Walpole both take free drop-offs. Their pickups either cost money or don't list Boston in the service area Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston[1] New Life Furniture Bank of MA[2]. The Salvation Army offers a free pickup you book by ZIP code The Salvation Army (satruck.org)[5].

If nobody will take a piece, the City lets you put furniture out with your regular curbside trash on collection day boston.gov[6].

Option 1: Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston ReStore

The ReStore resells donated furniture, appliances and building materials to fund Habitat's affordable homes, and donations are tax-deductible Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston[1].

  • Drop-off (free): 1580 VFW Parkway, West Roxbury. Donations are accepted Tuesday through Saturday, 9:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. Phone 617-327-1170 Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston[1].
  • Pickup (paid): Habitat books in-home pickups through ReSupply, a Boston scheduling company that works with local movers "at a lower cost to you." You still get a tax receipt. You can also email ReStore@habitatboston.org about a pickup for qualifying items Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston[1].
  • Furniture they want: bed frames, dressers and armoires, coffee and end tables, desks (3 by 5 feet or smaller), file cabinets, headboards and footboards, outdoor furniture, tables and chairs (not conference tables or dated pieces), and upholstered furniture that isn't ripped, worn, broken, dated or faded Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston[1]. Its wish list asks for appliances less than eight years old Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston[1].
  • Take elsewhere: mattresses and box springs, futons, sofa beds, sofas with slipcovers, hutches, sideboards and china cabinets, TV armoires and entertainment centers, platform and trundle beds, and baby furniture Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston[1].
  • Staff can refuse anything based on condition and store need, and all donations must work Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston[1].

Habitat says a second Greater Boston ReStore is opening at 44 Green River Way in Watertown; check its page for the opening date before you go Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston[1].

Option 2: New Life Furniture Bank of MA

New Life gives furniture to families moving out of homelessness and serves Greater Boston from its Walpole distribution center New Life Furniture Bank of MA[2].

  • Drop-off (free): Walpole, Saturdays 9:30 to 11:30 a.m., no appointment. It doesn't take resident drop-offs at other times. Enter on West Street. It's closed on Memorial Day, Independence Day and Labor Day weekends and during the December holidays New Life Furniture Bank of MA[4].
  • Standard pickup: New Life's own truck, on a sliding-scale fee from $95 (up to 10 items outside or in a garage) to $400 (more than 10 items from inside), with a 2 to 4 week lead time. The pickup area is Canton, Dedham, Dover, Easton, Foxboro, Franklin, Mansfield, Medfield, Medway, Millis, Natick, Needham, Norfolk, Norwood, Sharon, Sherborn, Walpole, Wellesley and Westwood. Boston isn't on the list New Life Furniture Bank of MA[2].
  • Priority pickup (anywhere): handled by Limitless Moving, an independent for-profit company, at market rates, with extra charges for items New Life can't accept New Life Furniture Bank of MA[2].
  • What they take: pieces that fit a small apartment and a narrow staircase, with no stains, wear, damage or missing parts. That includes dressers up to 6 feet wide, couches and sectionals up to 7 feet per piece, upholstered chairs and recliners, dining tables up to 5 feet, small desks and bookcases up to 6 feet tall New Life Furniture Bank of MA[3].
  • What they don't take: mattresses, box springs and bed frames of any size, futons, headboards, sleeper and recliner sofas, wardrobes, entertainment centers, hutches, outdoor furniture, and children's furniture New Life Furniture Bank of MA[3].

Option 3: The Salvation Army

The Salvation Army's site describes a free furniture and household-goods pickup. Schedule it by entering your ZIP code at satruck.org or by calling 1-800-SA-TRUCK (1-800-728-7825) The Salvation Army (satruck.org)[5]. Confirm that trucks serve your address before you count on it.

Its thrift stores generally take unbroken furniture, such as beds, chairs, coffee and end tables, desks, dining furniture, cabinets, mattresses and sofas The Salvation Army (satruck.org)[5]. They generally won't take appliances that are broken, rusty, dirty or missing a cord, any gas appliance, built-in appliances, TVs older than 5 years, large console TVs and stereos, auto parts, or paint and chemicals The Salvation Army (satruck.org)[5].

If no charity will take it

  1. Furniture goes out with regular trash. Boston Public Works says furniture can be put out with your normal curbside trash and recycling on your scheduled collection day, with no appointment boston.gov[6].
  2. Mattresses must be scheduled for collection. See our Boston mattress guide boston.gov[6].
  3. Appliances like fridges and air conditioners must be requested through 311. See our Boston refrigerator guide boston.gov[6].
  4. Big building? Boston treats buildings with more than six units as large residential buildings with their own rules boston.gov[7]. Ask your property manager how bulky items are handled.

Charity said no, or can't come in time?

Boston charities turn down worn, stained, oversized or hard-to-reach pieces, and some pickups take weeks or don't cover the city at all. A junk-removal crew can take everything at once, from any floor. Check that they serve your ZIP and confirm the price before you book.

Frequently asked questions

Who picks up donated furniture for free in Boston?
The Salvation Army describes a free furniture and household-goods pickup that you schedule by ZIP code at satruck.org or 1-800-SA-TRUCK (1-800-728-7825). Confirm trucks serve your address. Habitat's ReStore pickup and New Life's out-of-area option go through movers and cost money. The Salvation Army (satruck.org)[5] Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston[1] New Life Furniture Bank of MA[2]
Does Habitat for Humanity pick up furniture in Boston?
Habitat Greater Boston's ReStore schedules in-home pickups through ReSupply, which works with local movers for a fee. You can also drop items off free at 1580 VFW Parkway in West Roxbury, Tuesday to Saturday, 9:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston[1]
Will a Boston charity take my couch?
Often, if it's in good shape. New Life accepts couches and sectionals up to 7 feet per piece but not sleeper or recliner sofas. Habitat ReStore takes upholstered furniture that isn't ripped, worn, broken, dated or faded, but not sofa beds or sofas with slipcovers. New Life Furniture Bank of MA[3] Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston[1]
Can I donate a mattress in Boston?
Habitat ReStore and New Life Furniture Bank both list mattresses and box springs as items they don't accept. Salvation Army thrift stores list mattresses among commonly donated items, so ask when you schedule. Otherwise Boston requires mattresses to be scheduled for City collection. Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston[1] New Life Furniture Bank of MA[3] The Salvation Army (satruck.org)[5] boston.gov[6]
What if no charity will take my furniture in Boston?
Boston Public Works says furniture can go out with your normal curbside trash and recycling on your scheduled collection day. Mattresses and appliances need a scheduled pickup instead. boston.gov[6]
