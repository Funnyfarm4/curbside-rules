How to Get Rid of a Refrigerator in Boston (2026)

Boston only takes fridges, freezers and ACs by appointment. How to book special item pickup, the door and food rules, limits, and washer or stove rules.

Quick answer

  • Appointment only. Boston won't collect a fridge, freezer, air conditioner, dehumidifier, water cooler or ice maker without a special item appointment. boston.gov[1]
  • Book it: use the scheduler on boston.gov's Special Collection Items page, or call 311 / 617-635-4500. boston.gov[1]
  • Doors off, food out. Fridges and freezers (mini fridges too) won't be collected with a door attached or food inside. boston.gov[1]

The short version

A refrigerator can't just go out with the trash in Boston. Massachusetts bans "white goods" (large appliances) from disposal mass.gov[6], and appliances with Freons, like fridges and freezers, need a special item appointment with the City boston.gov[1]. Boston doesn't collect any special item without one boston.gov[1].

The catch: you have to take the doors off and empty it first, and you're capped at 10 special items a year boston.gov[1].

Option 1: City special item pickup (by appointment)

Boston Public Works collects appliances and electronics that contain Freons or cathode ray tubes at the curb, by appointment boston.gov[1].

Which items need an appointment

  • Items with Freons: fridges and freezers, water coolers, ice makers, air conditioners and dehumidifiers boston.gov[1].
  • Items with cathode ray tubes (CRTs): televisions, laptops and computer monitors boston.gov[1].

How to schedule

  1. Use the Schedule a Special Item Pickup tool on the City's Special Collection Items page boston.gov[1].
  2. Or call 311 or 617-635-4500. You can also file a request through Boston 311 boston.gov[1] boston.gov[2].
  3. Can't find your address in the tool? Fill out the City's Special Collection Form, linked in the FAQ on the same page boston.gov[1].

Limits

  • 10 items per calendar year, and no more than 5 per appointment boston.gov[1].
  • No commercial-grade items boston.gov[1].
  • Past your limit? The City says to contact a private hauler boston.gov[1].

Cost

The City's Special Collection Items page doesn't list a fee boston.gov[1]. Ask 311 if you're unsure.

How to prepare a fridge or freezer

Follow these City rules, or it won't be collected boston.gov[1]:

  1. Remove every door, including on mini fridges. Set the doors next to your trash bin for regular trash collection.
  2. Remove all food.
  3. Timing: put it out by 6 a.m. on your appointment day or after 5 p.m. the night before.
  4. Location: where trash is normally placed (curbside, public alley, or dumpster area), in a publicly accessible space. Crews can't collect from private property.
  5. Keep it clear: don't let vehicles or other objects block it, and don't put anything on top of it.
  6. Keep it intact: don't smash or take apart the appliance.

Cancelling or a missed pickup

  • Cancel before 12 p.m. the day before your appointment, using the link in your confirmation or reminder email. Call 311 for help boston.gov[1].
  • Not collected? Call 311 within 24 hours of your scheduled date, or you'll have to reschedule boston.gov[1].

Option 2: You live in a large building (7+ units)

If your building gets trash and recycling collection from the City, Boston will still collect special items like fridges and air conditioners by appointment; call 311 to schedule boston.gov[3]. If your building uses a private hauler instead, ask your property manager how appliances are handled.

Want it hauled away?

If you've hit the City's limit, have a commercial-grade unit, a dishwasher or water heater the City won't take, or just can't get the fridge down the stairs and doors off yourself, a junk-removal crew can carry it out for you. Confirm they serve your ZIP and check the price before you book.

Washers, dryers, stoves and dishwashers

Not every appliance needs an appointment. If an item isn't on the special item list, it doesn't need one boston.gov[1]. The City's Waste Sorting Tool gives these instructions boston.gov[4]:

  • Washing machine: regular trash, bulky item. It must be empty and the water drained. Put it next to your trash container on the curb on your regular trash day.
  • Dryer: regular trash, bulky item. Must be empty.
  • Stove: regular trash, bulky item. Must be empty.
  • For all three: set it out neatly by 6 a.m. on collection day or after 5 p.m. the night before, without blocking the sidewalk. The tool lists reuse (donation) as the best option if it still works.

Dishwashers, water heaters and plumbing fixtures are different: the City says to donate them or hire a private hauler boston.gov[1], and it doesn't collect water heaters or plumbing fixtures boston.gov[2].

Option 3: Donate it or let the store take it

If the appliance still works, the City suggests donating it to a local organization that will accept it. It also notes that many retailers accept certain electronics and appliances for recycling boston.gov[1]. To find a place, use Boston's Zero Waste Directory boston.gov[5].

Frequently asked questions

Can I put a refrigerator out with the trash in Boston?
No. Refrigerators and freezers contain Freons, and Boston only collects them by special item appointment. Items left out without an appointment aren't collected. boston.gov[1] boston.gov[2]
How do I schedule a refrigerator pickup in Boston?
Use the Schedule a Special Item Pickup tool on boston.gov's Special Collection Items page, or call 311 (617-635-4500). If the tool can't find your address, the City has a Special Collection Form linked in the page's FAQ. boston.gov[1]
Why do I have to take the doors off my fridge?
The City won't collect a fridge or freezer with the doors still attached, and that includes mini fridges. Put the removed doors next to your trash bin for regular trash collection. boston.gov[1]
How many appliances can Boston pick up?
Up to 10 special items per calendar year, with a maximum of 5 per appointment. Over the limit, the City says to contact a private hauler. boston.gov[1]
Does Boston pick up washers, dryers and stoves?
Yes, without an appointment. The City's Waste Sorting Tool lists them as regular trash bulky items: set them out empty (drain washers) next to your trash container by 6 a.m. on trash day or after 5 p.m. the night before. boston.gov[4]
Will Boston take my dishwasher or water heater?
No. The City lists water heaters, plumbing fixtures and dishwashers as items to donate or hand to a private hauler, and it doesn't collect water heaters or plumbing fixtures with trash. boston.gov[1] boston.gov[2]
My fridge wasn't picked up. What do I do?
Call 311 within 24 hours of your scheduled date. After that you'll have to reschedule the pickup. boston.gov[1]
