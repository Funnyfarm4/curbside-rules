How to Get Rid of a Couch in Boston (2026)

Boston takes couches with regular curbside trash, no appointment. Set-out times, who qualifies, sofa bed and futon rules, and where to donate one instead.

The short version

Getting rid of a couch in Boston is easier than in most big cities:

  1. Put it out with your trash. Boston Public Works says furniture can go out with your normal curbside trash and recycling on your scheduled collection day. No appointment is needed boston.gov (Public Works)[1] boston.gov (Public Works)[2].
  2. Or donate it, if it's clean and in good shape. Habitat's ReStore, New Life Furniture Bank and the Salvation Army all take sofas, with limits Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston[6] New Life Furniture Bank of MA[7] The Salvation Army (satruck.org)[9].
  3. Or pay to have it carried out, if you can't get it to the curb yourself.

The one catch is anything with a mattress in it. Boston won't collect mattresses with regular trash; they need a scheduled pickup boston.gov (Public Works)[4]. More on sofa beds below.

Option 1: Curbside trash pickup (no appointment)

boston.gov (Public Works)[1]

  • What goes out: couches, chairs, tables and other furniture. The City's list of items that don't need special collection includes "Furniture (donate or curbside trash)" boston.gov (Public Works)[2].
  • When: wait until after 5 p.m. the evening before your collection day, and have it out no later than 6 a.m. on collection day.
  • Which day: check the Trash Day app or the City's waste collection schedule search. Enter only your street number and name, with no unit number or ZIP code boston.gov (Public Works)[5].
  • Where: at the curb with your trash. For larger buildings on City service, the City asks that furniture be neatly placed next to the trash receptacles boston.gov (Public Works)[3].
  • Cost: Boston's trash pages list no fee or item limit for furniture. The 10-items-a-year limit applies to special collection items (fridges, TVs, air conditioners and the like) and to mattresses, not to furniture boston.gov (Public Works)[2] boston.gov (Public Works)[4].

If you don't follow the trash rules, you may get a ticket from Boston's Code Enforcement Division boston.gov (Public Works)[1].

What Boston won't take at the curb

The City doesn't collect construction debris, automotive parts, plumbing fixtures, fences, water heaters or fire debris. You'll need a private hauler for those boston.gov (Public Works)[1]. If you're tearing out built-in furniture or cabinets as part of a remodel, see our Boston construction debris guide.

Sofa beds, futons and recliners

  • Futons: the City's mattress program does not accept futons, mattress toppers or similar non-mattress items. Boston says those may be placed out for curbside trash boston.gov (Public Works)[4].
  • Mattresses: Boston won't collect mattresses or box springs with curbside trash; Massachusetts bans them from disposal. You must schedule a mattress pickup, up to 10 items per calendar year, for most buildings with 6 units or fewer boston.gov (Public Works)[4]. See our Boston mattress guide.
  • Sleeper sofas: Boston's pages don't say how to handle the fold-out mattress inside a sofa bed. Call 311 (617-635-4500) before you put one out boston.gov (Public Works)[1].

Who gets City curbside service

  • Homes and buildings with 6 units or fewer are covered by Boston's resident and small-landlord trash rules boston.gov (Public Works)[1].
  • Buildings with more than 6 units count as large residential buildings. The City offers trash and recycling collection to qualifying large buildings that buy approved containers, and the same furniture rule applies there boston.gov (Public Works)[3].
  • Buildings whose trash receptacles are larger than 2 cubic yards must use a private hauling service boston.gov (Public Works)[3]. If that's your building, ask your property manager before you put a couch out.

Option 2: Donate it

If your couch is clean and undamaged, a charity may take it. Each one has its own rules:

  • Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston ReStore: free drop-off at 1580 VFW Parkway, West Roxbury, Tuesday to Saturday, 9:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. In-home pickup is booked through ReSupply, which works with local movers for a fee. Habitat takes upholstered furniture that isn't ripped, worn, broken, dated or faded. It won't take sofa beds, sofas with slipcovers or futons Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston[6].
  • New Life Furniture Bank of MA: accepts couches and sectionals up to 7 feet per piece, but not sleeper sofas, recliner sofas or futons, and nothing stained, worn or damaged New Life Furniture Bank of MA[7]. Its own low-cost truck serves a list of towns south and west of Boston that doesn't include Boston. For Boston addresses it points to Limitless Moving, an independent mover that charges market rates New Life Furniture Bank of MA[8].
  • The Salvation Army: schedule a free furniture pickup by ZIP code at satruck.org or 1-800-SA-TRUCK (1-800-728-7825), and confirm trucks serve your address The Salvation Army (satruck.org)[9].

Our Boston furniture donation guide has more detail on each.

Want it hauled away?

Curbside pickup only works if you can get the couch down the stairs and out to the curb yourself, on the right night. If it's stuck on an upper floor, your building uses a private hauler, or you need it gone before trash day, a junk-removal crew can carry it out for you. Check that they serve your ZIP and confirm the price before you book.

Couch didn't get picked up?

Boston's trash pages don't spell out a missed-pickup process for furniture. File a request with Boston 311 online, by calling 617-635-4500, or by emailing 311@boston.gov boston.gov (Public Works)[1].

Frequently asked questions

Does Boston pick up couches?
Yes. Boston Public Works says furniture can be put out with your normal curbside trash and recycling on your scheduled collection day. Furniture doesn't need a special collection appointment. boston.gov (Public Works)[1] boston.gov (Public Works)[2]
Do I need to schedule a bulk pickup for a couch in Boston?
No. Boston's special collection appointments are for items with Freon or cathode ray tubes, such as fridges, air conditioners, TVs and monitors. The City lists furniture as donate or curbside trash. Mattresses are different and must be scheduled. boston.gov (Public Works)[2] boston.gov (Public Works)[4]
When can I put a couch on the curb in Boston?
Wait until after 5 p.m. the evening before your collection day, and have it out by 6 a.m. on collection day. Find your day in the Trash Day app or on the City's schedule search page. boston.gov (Public Works)[1] boston.gov (Public Works)[5]
Can I put a futon out with the trash in Boston?
Yes. Boston's mattress program doesn't accept futons, mattress toppers or similar non-mattress items, and says those may be placed out for curbside trash collection. boston.gov (Public Works)[4]
Will a Boston charity take my couch?
Often, if it's in good shape. New Life Furniture Bank accepts couches and sectionals up to 7 feet per piece, but not sleeper or recliner sofas. Habitat's ReStore takes upholstered furniture that isn't ripped, worn, broken, dated or faded, but not sofa beds or sofas with slipcovers. New Life Furniture Bank of MA[7] Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston[6]
I live in a big apartment building. Can I leave my couch at the curb?
Only if the City collects your building's trash. Buildings with more than six units can get City trash service if they meet its container rules, and furniture then goes neatly next to the trash receptacles. Buildings with trash receptacles larger than 2 cubic yards must use a private hauler, so ask your property manager. boston.gov (Public Works)[3]
