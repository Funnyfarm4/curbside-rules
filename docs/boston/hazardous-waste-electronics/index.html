The short version

Boston doesn't have a permanent drop-off center for household hazardous waste. Instead, Public Works runs Zero Waste Days, drop-off events for hazardous chemicals, electronics, tires and a few other waste-ban items boston.gov[1].

The next full Zero Waste Day is Saturday, November 21, 2026, from 8:30 a.m. to 12 p.m. at Millennium Park, 315 Gardner Street, West Roxbury. It's free boston.gov[2]. It replaces the September 26 event, which was postponed and moved because of an expected nor'easter boston.gov[1].

Before you load the car, check whether you need to go at all: latex paint and regular alkaline batteries go in the trash boston.gov[1].

Option 1: Zero Waste Day drop-off

Rules for attending

  • Boston residents only. Show a photo ID, utility bill or lease as proof of residency boston.gov[1].
  • No business waste boston.gov[1].
  • Arrive between 8:30 a.m. and 12 p.m. The City asks you not to come before 8:30 and stops accepting waste promptly at noon boston.gov[1].
  • 20-gallon limit on paint, motor oil, gasoline and all chemicals combined, and no container over 5 gallons boston.gov[1].
  • Labels must be readable on all paint and hazardous liquids boston.gov[1].
  • Pack by station. Group items in your car the way the City's list groups them, so you can unload at the right station boston.gov[1].
  • Staff can reject anything that doesn't meet the City's rules boston.gov[1].

Three kinds of events

  • Zero Waste Days take everything on the accepted list below.
  • Zero Waste Pop-Ups take at least two types of material; check each Pop-Up's event page for its list.
  • Yard Waste Drop-Off events take only leaves and yard waste.

All three are listed on the City's Zero Waste Days page boston.gov[1].

What you can bring

Electronics

Cell phones, computers, computer monitors and TVs, cords, chargers and wires, keyboards, microwaves, printers, power supplies, tablets and VCR/DVD players. The City links a full list on its page boston.gov[1].

Hazardous chemicals

Examples from the City's list: oil-based paint, motor oil, pesticides, aerosol cans, acids, caulk, pool and chemistry-set chemicals, fire starters, flammable liquids, floor-care products, furniture polish, and lithium and nickel-cadmium batteries boston.gov[1].

Mercury items

Fluorescent bulbs of all sizes, thermometers, thermostats, blood pressure cuffs, barometers and mercury switches. Everything must be intact; cracked items aren't accepted. Liquid mercury must stay in its original sealed container, placed inside a second sealed bag or bucket boston.gov[1].

Car-related items

  • Car tires and bike tires, up to 8 of each per person
  • Car batteries
  • Fire extinguishers
  • Propane tanks, empty or full boston.gov[1]

Paper shredding and textiles

  • Up to 10 copy-paper-size boxes of personal documents shredded on site. Remove binder clips and binders; staples, rubber bands and paper clips are fine. You can't watch the shredding because of traffic boston.gov[1].
  • Clothing, shoes (in pairs), linens, bags and accessories, plus small household items like coffee makers, dishware, pots and pans, toys and musical instruments boston.gov[1].

What Zero Waste Days won't take

No asbestos, explosives, radioactive material, commercial or industrial waste, construction debris, alkaline batteries (those go in the trash) or medical waste boston.gov[1]. The City doesn't collect construction debris at the curb either; it says to use a private hauler boston.gov[3]. See how to get rid of construction debris in Boston.

Paint and batteries: trash or drop-off?

  • Latex and acrylic paint isn't hazardous. Let it dry out in the can and put it out on your trash day boston.gov[2] boston.gov[1].
  • Oil-based paint goes to a drop-off event boston.gov[1].
  • Alkaline batteries (AAA, AA, C, D, 9-volt, labeled "no added mercury" or with a green tree logo) go in your regular trash boston.gov[1].
  • Bring to an event: lithium-ion rechargeables (phones, laptops, small power tools), rechargeable NiCad AA to 9-volt, lithium primary, lead-acid automotive or sealed, and button or coin cells boston.gov[1].

Can't make it on November 21?

  • TVs and monitors: schedule a curbside special item pickup instead of waiting for an event. Televisions and computer monitors need an appointment, booked on the City's Special Collection Items page or through 311 at 617-635-4500. The limit is 10 special items a year and 5 per appointment boston.gov[4] boston.gov[1].
  • Electronics, batteries, bulbs and more: the City says many retailers and other locations take them year-round. Search Boston's Zero Waste Directory boston.gov[1] boston.gov[5].
  • Questions: call 311 at 617-635-4500 or email zerowaste@boston.gov boston.gov[1].

Clearing out a basement or garage?

Zero Waste Days take the chemicals, electronics and tires, but not the furniture, boxes, construction debris and other junk around them. A junk-removal crew can haul the rest in one trip. Confirm they serve your ZIP and check the price before you book.

Frequently asked questions

When is the next hazardous waste drop-off in Boston?
Saturday, November 21, 2026, from 8:30 a.m. to 12 p.m. at Millennium Park, 315 Gardner Street in West Roxbury. It's the rescheduled September 26 Zero Waste Day and it's free. boston.gov[2]
Can I throw latex paint in the trash in Boston?
Yes, once it's dry. The City says latex and acrylic (water-based) paint isn't hazardous: let it dry out in the can and put it out on trash day. Only oil-based paint has to go to a drop-off event. boston.gov[1] boston.gov[2]
Which batteries can go in the trash?
Regular alkaline batteries (non-rechargeable AAA, AA, C, D and 9-volt, often labeled "no added mercury") go in your regular trash. Bring lithium-ion, rechargeable, lithium primary, button-cell and lead-acid batteries to a drop-off event. boston.gov[1]
How much paint or chemicals can I bring?
Up to 20 gallons in total of paint, motor oil, gasoline and chemicals, in containers no bigger than 5 gallons, with labels the staff can read. boston.gov[1]
Do I need to prove I live in Boston?
Yes. Zero Waste Days are only for Boston residents, so bring a photo ID, utility bill or lease. The City won't take waste from businesses. boston.gov[1]
What about an old TV or computer monitor?
You can bring it to a Zero Waste Day, or skip the wait and schedule a special item curbside pickup online. TVs and monitors need an appointment for curbside collection. boston.gov[1] boston.gov[4]
