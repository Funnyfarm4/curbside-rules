Why you can't just leave it on the curb

Since November 1, 2022, Massachusetts has banned mattresses from disposal or transport for disposal mass.gov[5]. Boston follows that rule: the City will not collect mattresses or box springs with your curbside trash, and it won't collect any mattress without an appointment boston.gov[1].

The good news: for most smaller buildings, the City picks it up for free boston.gov[2].

Option 1: Free City curbside pickup (buildings with 6 units or fewer)

Boston Public Works collects mattresses and box springs at the curb by appointment for residents of most buildings with six units or fewer boston.gov[1]. The City describes this as a free curbside pickup boston.gov[2].

How to schedule

  1. Go to the City's Mattress Recycling page and use the scheduling tool in the "Schedule Pickup" section boston.gov[1].
  2. When you book, include where your mattress will be: at the curb where your trash and recycling are normally collected boston.gov[1].
  3. Watch for your confirmation and reminder emails. They include the link you'd use to cancel boston.gov[1].

Can't find your address in the scheduler? The City's mattress page has an address-help form in its FAQ. If you live in a building with six or fewer units but still can't book, there's a separate eligibility form on the same page boston.gov[1].

Need help? Contact Boston 311 by dialing 311, calling 617-635-4500, or emailing 311@boston.gov boston.gov[1].

What the City accepts

  • Accepted: memory foam, box springs, innerspring, hybrid and latex mattresses boston.gov[1].
  • Not accepted: futons, mattress toppers and similar non-mattress items. Put those out with your regular curbside trash instead boston.gov[1].
  • Limit: 10 items per calendar year boston.gov[1].

Cost

Free for eligible residents boston.gov[2].

How to prepare your mattress for pickup

Follow these City rules, or your pickup may be missed or need rescheduling boston.gov[1]:

  1. Timing: put the mattress at the curb by 6 a.m. on your appointment day, or after 5 p.m. the night before.
  2. Location: curbside, where your trash and recycling usually go. Crews can't collect from private property.
  3. Keep it clear: don't let cars or bins block it, and don't put anything on top of it.
  4. Keep it whole: don't crush or cut the mattress or box spring.
  5. Bag it if needed: mattresses that are soiled, dirty or have bed bugs must be bagged and fully sealed. Mattress bags are sold at hardware stores and online.
  6. Bad weather: the City suggests bagging the mattress so it can still be fully recycled.

Cancelling or a missed pickup

  • Cancel before 12 p.m. the day before your appointment, using the link in your confirmation or reminder email. For help, call 311 boston.gov[1].
  • Not collected? Call 311 within 24 hours of your scheduled date. After that you'll need to book a new appointment boston.gov[1].

Option 2: You live in a building with 7 or more units

The City's mattress program doesn't serve buildings with more than six units boston.gov[1]. Here's what the City says to do instead boston.gov[3]:

  • Ask your property manager how your building handles mattress recycling. The City notes there are many local mattress haulers that serve large residential buildings.
  • Hire a mattress recycler. The City keeps a list of local mattress recyclers on its large residential buildings page, with phone numbers and notes on each.
  • Search MassDEP's directory. The City also points to MassDEP's Beyond the Bin search tool, which has a "Mattresses & Box Springs" category. MassDEP notes that listings are self-reported and recommends calling ahead MassDEP Recycle Smart[7].

Prices for private recyclers vary widely. On the City's list they range from $25 per item (drop-off, with proof of Boston residency) to $245 (one hauler's king-size price), as posted on boston.gov boston.gov[3]. Confirm the price with the company before you book.

The same options work if you've already used your 10 City pickups for the year boston.gov[1].

Option 3: Drop-off and recycling

The City's own program is curbside pickup by appointment. Its mattress page doesn't list a City drop-off site boston.gov[1]. If you'd rather drive it somewhere yourself, several recyclers on the City's list accept drop-offs boston.gov[3]:

  • Tough Stuff Recycling, 145 Authority Drive, Fitchburg. 978-307-4118. Drop-off listed at $25 per mattress or box spring with proof of Boston residency or business. Pickup listed at $75.
  • HandUp Mattress Recycling & Upcycling, 127 Rodney French Blvd, New Bedford. 774-271-8744. Drop-off listed at $35 per mattress or box spring.
  • Green Mattress, 144 Field Street, Brockton. 781-720-9855. Drop-off at their facility; curbside pickup also offered.
  • Raw Material Recovery Corp., 461 West Broadway, Gardner. 978-730-8266. Drop-off center open to the public, with a monthly price sheet.

These details are as listed by the City of Boston boston.gov[3]. Call ahead to confirm hours and prices.

Option 4: Donate it or let the store take it

  • Buying a new mattress? Ask the seller to haul away the old one when they deliver the new one. Boston and MassDEP both note that many mattress sellers do this boston.gov[1] mass.gov[6].
  • Still in good shape? MassDEP suggests donating it. Use the Beyond the Bin directory to find charities and organizations that accept mattresses mass.gov[6] MassDEP Recycle Smart[7].

Other bulky items in Boston

Boston handles other large items differently boston.gov[4]:

  • Furniture can go out with your normal curbside trash and recycling on your collection day.
  • Refrigerators, TVs and air conditioners need a pickup request through 311.
  • Textiles must also be scheduled for collection.

Frequently asked questions

Can I put a mattress out with my regular trash in Boston?
No. Mattresses and box springs are banned from disposal in Massachusetts, and Boston will not collect them with curbside trash. You must schedule a mattress pickup appointment. mass.gov[5] boston.gov[1]
Does Boston charge for mattress pickup?
No. The City describes it as a free curbside pickup by appointment for eligible residents. boston.gov[2]
How many mattresses can I get picked up?
Curbside mattress pickup is limited to 10 items per calendar year. After that, the City says to use a private mattress recycling hauler. boston.gov[1]
Does a box spring count?
Yes. The City collects box springs as well as memory foam, innerspring, hybrid and latex mattresses. boston.gov[1]
Will the City take my futon or mattress topper?
Not through the mattress program. Futons, mattress toppers and similar non-mattress items can go out with your regular curbside trash. boston.gov[1]
What if my mattress has bed bugs or is dirty?
It must be bagged and fully sealed in a mattress bag, or it may not be collected. The City says bags are sold at hardware stores and online. boston.gov[1]
My mattress wasn't picked up. What now?
Call 311 (617-635-4500). Missed pickups must be reported within 24 hours of your scheduled date, or you'll have to book a new appointment. boston.gov[1]
I live in a large apartment building. Who takes my mattress?
The City doesn't collect mattresses from buildings with more than six units. Ask your property manager how the building handles mattress recycling, or book a private mattress recycler. boston.gov[3]
