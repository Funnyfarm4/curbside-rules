How to Get Rid of a Mattress in Chicago (2026)

Chicago Streets and Sanitation can pick up a mattress free for homes of 4 units or fewer. Call before you set it out, who qualifies, and 5+ unit options.

The short version

If you live in a single-family home or a building with four units or fewer, Chicago's Department of Streets and Sanitation (DSS) can pick up a mattress as a bulk item chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1] chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2].

Call first. Arrange the pickup with your ward Streets and Sanitation superintendent (or through 311) before you put the mattress out chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1] chicago.gov (18th Ward)[6]. Putting large items out without arranging pickup can get you a ticket chicago.gov (18th Ward)[6].

There's no extra charge for bulk waste beyond the City's monthly garbage fee chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[3].

Who qualifies for City pickup

City refuse collection covers Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[4] chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1] chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2]:

  • A single dwelling
  • A multiple dwelling with four or fewer dwelling units
  • Certain townhouses and small bed-and-breakfasts that meet the code's rules

DSS says it collects from about 600,000 households citywide under the grid garbage system chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2].

Five or more units: City service doesn't apply. The owner or agent of a multiple dwelling with five or more units must have refuse removed at least once a week at their own cost by a private licensed scavenger Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[5] chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1]. Ask your property manager how the building handles mattresses and other bulk items.

How to schedule mattress pickup

  1. Find your ward. Use the City's ward and alderman lookup, then open your ward's chicago.gov page for the Streets and Sanitation / ward yard phone number chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2].
  2. Call the ward yard or 311. Tell them you need a bulk pickup for a mattress (and any other large items). Ward pages list refuse and bulk pickup as a Streets and Sanitation service; for example the 44th Ward Yard is at 1501 W. School St., 312-744-5028, open 7 a.m. to 3 p.m. weekdays chicago.gov (44th Ward)[7]. The 18th Ward Sanitation Office similarly asks residents to contact them before putting bulk items out chicago.gov (18th Ward)[6].
  3. Wait for instructions on when to set the mattress out. Don't put it at the curb until the ward has arranged the pickup chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1] chicago.gov (18th Ward)[6].

DSS district offices are also listed on the City's Grid Garbage Collection page if you need a starting point for your area chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2].

What it costs

Residents on City garbage service pay $9.50 per dwelling unit per month (for example $9.50 for a single-family home, $19 for a two-flat) chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[3] Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[4]. Eligible seniors can get a 50% discount ($4.75 per unit) chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[3] Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[4].

The City's Garbage Fee FAQ says that fee covers garbage collection and that there is no additional cost for lawn waste or bulk waste collection chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[3]. You can't opt out of City garbage service for a four-unit-or-fewer property; DSS is the exclusive provider under the Municipal Code chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[3] Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[4].

Prep tips

Official Chicago pages don't publish a mattress-bagging rule the way some other cities do. What they do say:

  • Arrange the pickup before set-out for any large item or extra load of trash chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1].
  • Keep regular garbage in carts with lids closed; don't leave raw garbage outside the container chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1].
  • Remodeling debris is your responsibility to remove (dumpsters can be rented for that) chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1]. For dumpster rules on the public way, see our Chicago dumpster permit guide.

If your building or address doesn't qualify

  • 5+ unit buildings and commercial properties must use a private licensed scavenger Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[5] chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1].
  • For chemicals, oil paint and electronics (not mattresses), Chicago's Household Chemicals & Computer Recycling Facility on Goose Island is the City drop-off — see our Chicago hazardous waste and electronics guide.

Want it hauled away?

If you live in a building with five or more units, can't get the mattress downstairs yourself, or need it gone before Streets and Sanitation can schedule a bulk pickup, a junk-removal crew can carry it out for you. Confirm they serve your ZIP and check the price before you book.

Frequently asked questions

Will Chicago pick up an old mattress?
Yes, for homes that get City garbage collection (single-family and buildings of four units or fewer). Contact your ward Streets and Sanitation office or 311 to arrange a bulk pickup before you put the mattress out. chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1] chicago.gov (18th Ward)[6] chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2]
Does Chicago charge for mattress pickup?
There is no additional cost for bulk waste collection. It is covered by the City's $9.50 per dwelling unit per month garbage fee. chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[3]
Can I just leave a mattress at the curb on garbage day in Chicago?
No. The City tells residents to arrange a bulk pickup with their Streets and Sanitation ward superintendent before putting out any large item, and ward offices warn that putting items out without arranging pickup can lead to a ticket. chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1] chicago.gov (18th Ward)[6]
I live in a 5+ unit building. Who takes my mattress?
City refuse collection doesn't cover buildings with five or more dwelling units. The owner (or their agent) must have refuse removed at least once a week by a private licensed scavenger at their own expense. Ask your property manager how the building handles bulk items. Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[5] chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1]
How do I find my Chicago ward sanitation office?
Look up your ward and alderman on the City's ward lookup page, then check your ward's chicago.gov page for the Streets and Sanitation / ward yard phone number. You can also call 311. chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2] chicago.gov (44th Ward)[7]
Is mattress pickup included in Chicago's garbage fee?
Yes. The City's Garbage Fee FAQ says there is no additional cost for lawn waste or bulk waste collection beyond the monthly garbage fee. chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[3]
