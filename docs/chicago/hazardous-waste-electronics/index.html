The short version

Chicago has one permanent drop-off for household chemicals and old electronics: the Household Chemicals & Computer Recycling Facility (HCCRF) at 1150 N. North Branch Street on Goose Island, two blocks east of the Kennedy Expressway at Division Street chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[1].

It's open Tuesdays 7 a.m. to 12 p.m., Thursdays 2 to 7 p.m., and the first Saturday of every month 8 a.m. to 3 p.m., year-round chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[1] chicago.gov (Chicago Recycles)[2]. The next Saturday opening is November 7, 2026, then December 5.

The City's page currently warns that repairs are under way at the facility and asks visitors to watch for schedule changes, so check the HCCRF page the day you go chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[1].

Who can use it

  • All Chicagoans can drop off their own household chemicals and electronics chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[1].
  • Businesses and large groups can't. They must hire a private disposal company chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[1].
  • The Illinois EPA lists the facility's phone number as 312-744-3060 Illinois EPA[3].

What happens when you arrive

There are two drop-off buildings. The blue building is for household chemicals and the yellow building is for electronics chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[1].

At the chemical door an attendant meets you, you take your items out of the car, and the attendant checks everything and hands back anything that isn't accepted. Then you move on to the electronics building or leave chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[1].

Never leave items outside the gate after hours chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[1].

Household chemicals it takes

From the City's accepted list chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[1]:

  • Household chemicals such as bleach, harsh cleaners and drain cleaners
  • Oil-based paint, aerosol paint and paint thinner
  • Solvents: mineral spirits, stains, paint strippers, preservatives and sealers
  • Batteries: rechargeable, lithium, small lead-acid and car batteries
  • Compact fluorescent bulbs and fluorescent tubes
  • Antifreeze, motor oil, gasoline and other auto fluids
  • Herbicides, pesticides and insecticides
  • All aerosol cans, such as spray paint and hair spray
  • Lawn, pool and hobby chemicals
  • Mercury thermometers, switches and thermostats
  • BBQ propane tanks, small fire extinguishers and small non-refillable cylinders

Electronics it takes

The City's electronics program lists computers, monitors, TVs, printers, fax machines and scanners, keyboards and mice, VCRs and DVD players, video game consoles, portable music players, digital converter boxes, cable and satellite receivers, and small-scale servers chicago.gov (Chicago Recycles)[2]. The HCCRF page also names cell phones chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[1].

Only the listed items are accepted, commercial electronics are refused, and once the truck is full no more electronics are taken that day chicago.gov (Chicago Recycles)[2]. The City's monthly neighborhood e-waste drop-off sites are on hold until further notice, so the HCCRF is the City option for now chicago.gov (Chicago Recycles)[2].

What the HCCRF won't take

  • Business or commercial waste, and agricultural or institutional waste
  • Latex, acrylic and water-based paint
  • Alkaline batteries
  • Explosives, ammunition and fireworks
  • Smoke and carbon monoxide detectors
  • Household appliances, including microwaves, toasters, coffee makers, stoves, refrigerators, washers, dryers, humidifiers and dehumidifiers
  • Air conditioners, HVAC equipment and refrigerants
  • Tires, cement and concrete
  • Medical waste such as sharps and needles, and pharmaceuticals
  • Radioactive materials, farm machinery oil and large business copiers chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[1]

What to do with latex paint

The HCCRF refuses latex paint because of its high disposal cost and low environmental impact. The City suggests you chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[1]:

  • Use it up on another project or on scrap wood or cardboard, then let the empty can dry and throw it away.
  • Dry it out by mixing in kitty litter, shredded paper or sawdust, or pouring it into a bag. Once dry, throw the paint and can in the trash.
  • Give it away to neighbors, or to schools and theater groups that sometimes want paint for sets and art.
  • Store it for touch-ups, with plastic wrap under the lid so it doesn't dry out.

Free stuff while you're there

Usable leftovers such as cleaning products and auto fluids may be available free in the HCCRF's material exchange room. Ask a staff member to let you in; it's open to anyone during regular hours chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[1].

Can't get to Goose Island?

  • The Illinois EPA runs one-day household hazardous waste collections around the state that are open to all Illinois residents, and lists other long-term drop-off sites Illinois EPA[3].
  • For bulbs, the City notes you can also take used CFLs to your local Home Depot chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[1].

Frequently asked questions

Where can I drop off household hazardous waste in Chicago?
At the City's permanent Household Chemicals & Computer Recycling Facility, 1150 N. North Branch Street on Goose Island. It's open Tuesdays 7 a.m. to noon, Thursdays 2 to 7 p.m., and the first Saturday of each month 8 a.m. to 3 p.m. chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[1]
Does Chicago take latex paint at the drop-off?
No. The HCCRF doesn't accept latex, acrylic or water-based paint. The City says to use it up, give it away, or dry it out with kitty litter, shredded paper or sawdust and put the dried paint and can in the trash. chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[1]
Where do I recycle an old TV or computer in Chicago?
Bring it to the yellow electronics building at the HCCRF during its regular hours. The City's monthly neighborhood e-waste drop-offs are on hold until further notice, and once the electronics truck is full no more items are accepted that day. chicago.gov (Chicago Recycles)[2] chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[1]
Can I throw regular batteries in the trash in Chicago?
The HCCRF doesn't accept alkaline batteries, so it isn't the place for them. It does take rechargeable, lithium, small lead-acid and car batteries. chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[1]
Can I leave items at the facility after hours?
No. The City says materials must not be left outside the HCCRF and can only be dropped off during scheduled hours. chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[1]
