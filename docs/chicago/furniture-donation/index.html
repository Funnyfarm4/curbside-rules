The short version

Chicago has several charities that will come get good furniture: ReStore Chicago offers a free pickup, Brown Elephant runs a curbside pickup, and the Salvation Army schedules free pickups by ZIP code ReStore Chicago (Habitat for Humanity Chicago)[2] Howard Brown Health (Brown Elephant)[4] The Salvation Army (satruck.org)[8]. Almost all of them need the furniture clean, undamaged and already outside or at ground level.

If no charity wants it, the City will haul it as a bulk pickup at no extra charge for homes it serves, but you have to arrange it first chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[9] chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[10].

ReStore Chicago (Habitat for Humanity Chicago)

ReStore Chicago takes new or gently used furniture, appliances, housewares and building materials. You can drop off or request a free pickup ReStore Chicago (Habitat for Humanity Chicago)[2].

  • ReStore Chicago North, 6040 N. Pulaski Rd., Chicago 60646
  • ReStore Chicago South, 6057 S. Western Ave., Chicago 60636
  • Donation drop-off: Wednesday to Sunday, 11 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., at both stores ReStore Chicago (Habitat for Humanity Chicago)[1].
  • Questions: 312-563-0296 or restore@habitatchicago.org ReStore Chicago (Habitat for Humanity Chicago)[1].

What it takes: furniture with no rips, scratches, stains or pet hair, such as bookshelves, buffets, chairs, coffee tables, dressers, nightstands, side tables, sofas and sectionals, and TV stands Habitat for Humanity Chicago[3]. Appliances must work and have all their parts, with age limits by type (for example, refrigerators, stoves and washers up to 10 years old) Habitat for Humanity Chicago[3].

What it won't take: clothing, mattresses, televisions, used doors and windows, paint, sleeper sofas and china cabinets. Because of inventory, bed frames and dining tables aren't being accepted right now ReStore Chicago (Habitat for Humanity Chicago)[2]. Its guide also turns away armoires, baby furniture, box springs, exercise machines, pianos and TV cabinets Habitat for Humanity Chicago[3]. Staff review every item and can refuse it Habitat for Humanity Chicago[3].

Brown Elephant (Howard Brown Health)

Brown Elephant resale shops raise money for Howard Brown Health Howard Brown Health (Brown Elephant)[5].

  • Curbside pickup: fill out the online request form and upload photos. They reply with available dates and typically book a few days to a week ahead Howard Brown Health (Brown Elephant)[4].
  • It's curbside only. They don't do move-outs. Items must be outside and ready, at ground level outdoors, in a garage or on a loading dock Howard Brown Health (Brown Elephant)[4].
  • Movers can decline anything with stains, rips, excessive pet hair, mold, missing hardware, discoloration or other damage Howard Brown Health (Brown Elephant)[4].
  • To change or cancel, email BEdonate@HowardBrown.org at least 24 hours ahead Howard Brown Health (Brown Elephant)[4]. Brown Elephant says it can't book pickups by phone Howard Brown Health (Brown Elephant)[5].

Drop-off: the Northalsted store (812 W. Belmont Ave., 773-549-5943) and the Andersonville store (5404 N. Clark St., 773-271-9382) are open 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. every day Howard Brown Health (Brown Elephant)[6]. The Oak Park store has closed Howard Brown Health (Brown Elephant)[5].

Accepted: upholstered furniture in sound condition with no rips, stains or pet hair, sofa beds with clean mattresses, tables, dining sets, dressers, bookcases, small desks and working electronics Howard Brown Health (Brown Elephant)[5]. Not accepted: mattresses, box springs, headboards and bed frames, children's furniture, large appliances, large entertainment centers, armoires, china cabinets, pianos and exercise equipment Howard Brown Health (Brown Elephant)[5].

Salvation Army

The Salvation Army describes a free furniture pickup. Enter your ZIP code on satruck.org to book, or call 1-800-SA-TRUCK (1-800-728-7825) The Salvation Army (satruck.org)[8].

Its stores generally take unbroken furniture, such as beds, chairs, coffee and end tables, desks, dining furniture, cabinets, mattresses and sofas The Salvation Army (satruck.org)[8]. They generally won't take broken, rusty or dirty appliances, any gas appliance, built-in appliances, TVs older than 5 years, or paint and chemicals. Rules vary by area, so call to confirm The Salvation Army (satruck.org)[8].

Chicago Furniture Bank

Chicago Furniture Bank gives furniture free to Chicago families in need, so it accepts some things others don't, including mattresses and box springs and couches Chicago Furniture Bank[7].

  • Pickup isn't free. It asks a suggested donation from $100 (one large item plus a few small ones) up to $400 to $600 for a full estate, and needs at least a week to schedule. Pickups run Monday to Saturday, 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. Chicago Furniture Bank[7]
  • Drop-off: 4801 S. Whipple St., Chicago 60632, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. Call 312-752-0211 before you head over Chicago Furniture Bank[7].
  • Won't pick up from the third floor or higher without an elevator, and items must be clean and pest-free Chicago Furniture Bank[7].
  • Doesn't take large sectionals, sleeper sofas, California king beds, wooden or platform bed frames, headboards, armoires, large TV stands, outdoor furniture, pianos or large appliances Chicago Furniture Bank[7].

If no charity will take it

  1. Arrange a City bulk pickup first. If the City collects your garbage, call your ward Streets and Sanitation superintendent or 311 (312-744-5000 from outside Chicago, or the CHI 311 app) and ask for a bulk pickup before you put anything out chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[9] chicago.gov (311 City Services)[11]. There's no extra charge beyond the monthly garbage fee chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[10]. Our Chicago bulk pickup guide explains who qualifies.
  2. Fridges and freezers: remove the doors or lids before set-out chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[9]. See our Chicago refrigerator guide.
  3. Five or more units? The City only collects from residential properties with four units or fewer, so larger buildings aren't eligible chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[10]. Ask your property manager how bulky items are handled.

Charity said no, or can't come in time?

Chicago charities turn down stained, worn, oversized or upstairs pieces, and most won't carry anything out of your home. A junk-removal crew can take everything at once, from any floor. Check that they serve your ZIP and confirm the price before you book.

Frequently asked questions

Who picks up donated furniture for free in Chicago?
ReStore Chicago (Habitat for Humanity Chicago) says you can request a free pickup, and the Salvation Army describes a free furniture pickup you schedule by ZIP code at satruck.org or 1-800-728-7825. Brown Elephant also runs a curbside pickup you request online with photos. ReStore Chicago (Habitat for Humanity Chicago)[2] The Salvation Army (satruck.org)[8] Howard Brown Health (Brown Elephant)[4]
Where can I donate a mattress in Chicago?
ReStore Chicago and Brown Elephant both refuse mattresses. Chicago Furniture Bank lists mattresses and box springs (twin to king) among items it accepts, with a suggested donation for pickup or free weekday drop-off at its warehouse. The Salvation Army's general list includes mattresses, but it says to call 1-800-SA-TRUCK to confirm what your area takes. ReStore Chicago (Habitat for Humanity Chicago)[2] Howard Brown Health (Brown Elephant)[5] Chicago Furniture Bank[7] The Salvation Army (satruck.org)[8]
Will Chicago charities pick up furniture from inside my apartment?
Usually not. Brown Elephant asks that all items be outside and ready, picking up only at ground level outdoors, in garages or on a loading dock. Chicago Furniture Bank won't pick up from the third floor or higher without an elevator. For an inside or upstairs carry-out, a paid junk-removal crew is the usual option. Howard Brown Health (Brown Elephant)[4] Chicago Furniture Bank[7]
Does ReStore Chicago take couches?
Yes, if they're in good shape. Its accepted-donations guide lists sofas and sectionals with no rips, scratches, stains or pet hair. Sleeper sofas are not accepted. Habitat for Humanity Chicago[3] ReStore Chicago (Habitat for Humanity Chicago)[2]
What if no charity will take my furniture in Chicago?
If the City collects your garbage (homes and buildings with four or fewer units), arrange a bulk pickup with your ward Streets and Sanitation superintendent or through 311 before putting the furniture out. There's no extra charge beyond the monthly garbage fee. chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[9] chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[10] chicago.gov (311 City Services)[11]
