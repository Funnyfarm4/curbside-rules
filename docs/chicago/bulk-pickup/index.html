How to Get Bulk Items Picked Up in Chicago Through 311 (2026)

Chicago bulk pickup has no extra charge for homes of 4 units or fewer. Call your ward or 311 before set-out, who qualifies, and 5+ unit options.

The short version

If you live in a single-family home or a building with four units or fewer, Chicago's Department of Streets and Sanitation (DSS) can pick up large items and extra loads of trash as a bulk collection chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1] chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2].

Call first. Arrange the pickup with your ward Streets and Sanitation superintendent (or through 311 / the CHI 311 app) before you put anything out chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1] chicago.gov (18th Ward)[6] chicago.gov (311 City Services)[8]. Putting bulk items out without arranging pickup can get you a ticket chicago.gov (18th Ward)[6].

There's no extra charge for bulk waste beyond the City's monthly garbage fee chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[3].

Who qualifies for City pickup

City refuse collection covers Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[4] chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1] chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2]:

  • A single dwelling
  • A multiple dwelling with four or fewer dwelling units
  • Certain townhouses and small bed-and-breakfasts that meet the code's rules

DSS says it collects from about 600,000 households citywide under the grid garbage system, with weekly pickups Monday through Friday chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2].

Five or more units: City service doesn't apply. The owner or agent of a multiple dwelling with five or more units must have refuse removed at least once a week at their own cost by a private licensed scavenger Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[5] chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1]. Ask your property manager how the building handles furniture, mattresses and other bulk items.

How to schedule a bulk pickup

  1. Find your ward. Use the City's ward and alderman lookup, then open your ward's chicago.gov page for the Streets and Sanitation / ward yard phone number chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2].
  2. Call the ward yard or 311. Tell them you need a bulk pickup for large items or an extra load of trash. Ward pages list refuse and bulk pickup as a Streets and Sanitation service; for example the 44th Ward Yard is at 1501 W. School St., 312-744-5028, open 7 a.m. to 3 p.m. weekdays chicago.gov (44th Ward)[7]. The 18th Ward Sanitation Office is at 1756 W. 74th St., 312-747-3655, open 6 a.m. to 2:30 p.m. weekdays, and asks residents to contact them before putting bulk items out chicago.gov (18th Ward)[6]. Outside Chicago you can reach 311 at 312-744-5000 chicago.gov (311 City Services)[8].
  3. Wait for instructions on when to set the items out. Don't put them at the curb until the ward has arranged the pickup chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1] chicago.gov (18th Ward)[6].

DSS district offices are also listed on the City's Grid Garbage Collection page if you need a starting point for your area chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2].

What it costs

Residents on City garbage service pay $9.50 per dwelling unit per month (for example $9.50 for a single-family home, $19 for a two-flat) chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[3] Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[4]. Eligible seniors can get a 50% discount ($4.75 per unit) chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[3] Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[4].

The City's Garbage Fee FAQ says that fee covers garbage collection and that there is no additional cost for lawn waste or bulk waste collection chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[3]. You can't opt out of City garbage service for a four-unit-or-fewer property; DSS is the exclusive provider under the Municipal Code chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[3] Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[4].

What you can put out

Official Chicago pages do not publish a single citywide list of every item DSS will take as bulk. What they do say:

  • Arrange a bulk pickup before setting out any large item or an extra load of trash, such as after cleaning a basement or attic chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1].
  • Refrigerators and freezers need doors or lids removed, and you still have to arrange the bulk pickup before set-out chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1].
  • Keep regular garbage in carts with lids closed; don't leave raw garbage outside the container chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1].
  • Remodeling debris is your responsibility to remove, and dumpsters can be rented for that chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1]. For dumpster rules on the public way, see our Chicago dumpster permit guide.

If your building or address doesn't qualify

  • 5+ unit buildings and commercial properties must use a private licensed scavenger Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[5] chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1].
  • For chemicals, oil paint and electronics (not furniture), Chicago's Household Chemicals & Computer Recycling Facility on Goose Island is the City drop-off — see our Chicago hazardous waste and electronics guide.

Frequently asked questions

How do I request a bulk item pickup in Chicago?
Contact your ward Streets and Sanitation office or call 311 (or use the CHI 311 app) and ask for a bulk pickup. Do this before you put large items or an extra load of trash at the curb. Putting items out without arranging pickup can lead to a ticket. chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1] chicago.gov (18th Ward)[6] chicago.gov (311 City Services)[8]
Does Chicago charge extra for bulk pickup?
There is no additional cost for bulk waste collection. It is covered by the City's $9.50 per dwelling unit per month garbage fee. chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[3]
Who qualifies for Chicago bulk pickup?
The City collects from single-family homes and buildings with four units or fewer (plus certain townhouses and small bed-and-breakfasts that meet the code). Buildings with five or more units must use a private licensed scavenger. Can I just leave bulky items at the curb on garbage day in Chicago?
No. The City tells residents to arrange a bulk pickup with their Streets and Sanitation ward superintendent before putting out any large item or extra load of trash, and ward offices warn that putting items out without arranging pickup can lead to a ticket. chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1] chicago.gov (18th Ward)[6]
