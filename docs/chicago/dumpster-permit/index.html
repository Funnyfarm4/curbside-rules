Do You Need a Dumpster Permit in Chicago? (2026)

In Chicago the dumpster company, not you, gets the CDOT permit for a dumpster on the street or alley. Fees from $50, placement rules, and fines.

The short version

If a dumpster or roll-off box will sit on a Chicago street, alley, parkway or sidewalk, it needs a dumpster permit from the Chicago Department of Transportation (CDOT) Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[1] chicago.gov[3].

The good news if you're the customer: the permit is the dumpster company's job. City code makes it unlawful for any provider, meaning the company that leases you the dumpster, to place one on the public way without first getting the permit Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[1]. If the dumpster goes on private property, like your driveway, no dumpster permit is needed, although other permits may apply City of Chicago (CDOT)[2].

Do you need one?

  • Street, curb lane, alley, parkway or sidewalk: yes. These are all public way placements with a set fee City of Chicago (CDOT)[2].
  • Your own driveway, yard or private lot: no dumpster permit, but other permits may apply City of Chicago (CDOT)[2].
  • Downtown: yes, at double the rate. The Central Business District runs roughly from Division Street south to Roosevelt Road, and from Lake Michigan west to Halsted Street (west only to LaSalle Street between Division Street and Chicago Avenue) Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[5] Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[1].

What it costs

These are the usage fees in City code and CDOT's dumpster application guide Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[1] City of Chicago (CDOT)[2]. They're the same for an alley, curb lane, parkway or sidewalk.

  • Class A (30 cubic yards or less), outside downtown: $50 for up to 3 days, or $100 per month
  • Class B (over 30 cubic yards), outside downtown: $100 for up to 3 days, or $200 per month
  • Inside the Central Business District: double those amounts, so $100 or $200 for Class A, and $200 or $400 for Class B

On top of that:

  • An application fee. CDOT sets it, and it can rise each January with inflation (up to 2.5% a year) Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[1]. CDOT posts the current amount in its yearly Permit Fee Schedule, and says fees may change from year to year chicago.gov[4] chicago.gov[3].
  • Parking meters, if any are blocked. There's a monthly surcharge for each disabled meter or metered space. If a meter has to be pulled out, the fee is $150 to remove and reinstall it, or $1,000 for a meter that serves several spaces City of Chicago (CDOT)[2].

Ask your dumpster company whether its quote includes the permit and any meter charges. Either way, the provider files and pays CDOT online City of Chicago (CDOT)[2].

Where the dumpster can't go

City code bans dumpster placement Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[1]:

  • within 40 feet of a bus stop
  • within 20 feet of a fire hydrant
  • within 10 feet of an intersection, a crosswalk, or a parking space for people with disabilities
  • on a parkway or sidewalk, unless the provider shows CDOT there's no workable alternative and it won't create a hazard or block traffic

Winter warning: from November 1 to April 1, a dumpster on an arterial street has to come off once CDOT gives the provider notice of a Phase 3 snow emergency, with 12 hours to remove it Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[1].

The dumpster also has to sit immediately next to the address on the permit Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[1]. If a hydrant or another obstacle blocks that spot, the provider can amend the permit to change the location for no extra fee City of Chicago (CDOT)[2].

How the permit works

  1. The provider is licensed first. Before CDOT issues any dumpster permit, the provider needs $1,000,000 of liability insurance naming the City as additional insured, plus a $5,000 letter of credit Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[1] City of Chicago (CDOT)[2]. This one-time license part stays on file with CDOT City of Chicago (CDOT)[2].
  2. It applies for each job online. Dumpster permit applications and payments are only accepted online, through the City's permit portal at ipi.cityofchicago.org City of Chicago (CDOT)[2] chicago.gov[3]. The application lists the provider, how many dumpsters, the class, the street address, what it's for and how long it'll stay Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[1].
  3. Changes need an amendment. A bigger dumpster or a longer stay needs an amended permit and an adjusted fee. A different number of dumpsters, a different address (other than a hydrant or obstacle move) or a different use needs a new permit Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[1].
  4. Canceled job? If you cancel before delivery, the provider can return the permit with a cancellation request and get a credit for the permit fee by emailing CDOT's permit office City of Chicago (CDOT)[2].

What to check before you book

  • Ask if they hold a CDOT dumpster license and will pull the permit for your address. Unpermitted dumpsters can be fined $250 to $1,500 per offense, or $500 to $3,000 downtown, with each day a separate offense, and the City can haul one away at the provider's cost Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[1].
  • Look at the dumpster when it arrives. The provider's name and phone number must be on both long sides in letters at least 3 inches tall, along with an ID number, and there must be reflective strips on all four corners Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[1].
  • Know who to ask. CDOT's right-of-way permit questions go to CDOTPermitsupport@cityofchicago.org, and CDOT publishes permit data on ChiStreetWork and the Chicago Data Portal chicago.gov[3].

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit for a dumpster in Chicago?
If the dumpster sits on the public way (street, alley, parkway or sidewalk), yes, but under City code it's the dumpster provider that must get the permit, not the customer. No dumpster permit is needed when it's on private property, though other permits may apply. Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[1] City of Chicago (CDOT)[2]
How much is a dumpster permit in Chicago?
For a Class A dumpster (30 cubic yards or less) outside the Central Business District, the code sets $50 for up to three days or $100 per month. A Class B dumpster (over 30 cubic yards) is $100 or $200, and fees double inside the Central Business District. An application fee set by CDOT each year is added on top. Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[1] City of Chicago (CDOT)[2]
Do I need a permit if the dumpster is in my driveway in Chicago?
Not a CDOT dumpster permit. CDOT's dumpster guide says the permit isn't necessary if the dumpster is placed on private property, but that other permits may apply. City of Chicago (CDOT)[2]
Can a homeowner get a dumpster permit in Chicago?
The permit is issued to the dumpster provider, which must have $1,000,000 in liability insurance naming the City and a $5,000 letter of credit on file. In practice, rent from a company that already holds a CDOT dumpster license and ask it to pull the permit. Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[1] City of Chicago (CDOT)[2]
What is the fine for a dumpster without a permit in Chicago?
$250 to $1,500 per offense, or $500 to $3,000 in the Central Business District. Each day is a separate offense, and the City can remove an unpermitted dumpster at the provider's cost. Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[1]
Where can't a dumpster go in Chicago?
Not within 40 feet of a bus stop, 20 feet of a fire hydrant, or 10 feet of an intersection, crosswalk or disabled parking space. Parkways and sidewalks are off limits unless CDOT agrees there's no other workable spot. Municipal Code of Chicago (American Legal Publishing)[1]
