How to Get Rid of a Refrigerator in Chicago (2026)

The short version

If you live in a single-family home or a building with four units or fewer, Chicago's Department of Streets and Sanitation (DSS) can take an old refrigerator or freezer as a bulk pickup chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1] chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2]. Two rules come first:

  1. Remove the doors or lids from any discarded refrigerator or freezer chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1].
  2. Arrange the bulk pickup with your ward Streets and Sanitation superintendent (or through 311) before you put it out chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1].

There's no extra charge for bulk waste beyond the City's monthly garbage fee chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[3].

Who qualifies for City pickup

DSS crews service all single-family homes and apartment buildings of four units or less, about 600,000 households, with weekly pickups Monday through Friday chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2]. Everyone else must arrange private garbage collection chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2].

Five or more units: the City says owners of apartment buildings must arrange private or licensed scavenger pickup chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1]. Ask your property manager how the building handles appliances.

Businesses must use a licensed scavenger service chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1].

How to schedule a fridge pickup

  1. Find your ward. Use the City's ward and alderman lookup, then open your ward's chicago.gov page for its Streets and Sanitation office.
  2. Call the ward office or 311 and ask for a bulk pickup of a refrigerator or freezer. For example, the 18th Ward Sanitation Office (312-747-3655) asks residents to contact it before putting bulk items out so a ticket isn't issued chicago.gov (18th Ward)[4]. The 44th Ward lists its ward yard at 312-744-5028 chicago.gov (44th Ward)[5]. From outside Chicago, 311 is 312-744-5000 chicago.gov (311 City Services)[6].
  3. Take the doors or lid off before the appliance goes outside chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1].
  4. Set it out when the ward tells you to. Don't leave it in the alley or at the curb before the pickup is arranged chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1] chicago.gov (18th Ward)[4].

What it costs

City-serviced residents pay $9.50 per dwelling unit per month for garbage collection, and the City says there is no additional cost for bulk waste collection chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[3].

Freon and the Illinois white goods law

You don't need to drain the refrigerant yourself, and you shouldn't try. Here's what the official rules say:

  • Illinois bans offering or collecting white goods for landfilling until their "white good components" are removed. White goods include refrigerators, freezers, ranges, water heaters, air conditioners and humidifiers. The components include CFC refrigerant gas, mercury switches and PCB-containing parts Illinois General Assembly[8].
  • Federal rules put the job of recovering refrigerant from household fridges, freezers and window air conditioners on the final person in the disposal chain, such as a scrap metal recycler or landfill U.S. EPA[9]. If an appliance arrives with its refrigerant already removed, that final processor must keep a signed statement naming who recovered it and when U.S. EPA[9].

In practice, that means whoever hauls your fridge to a recycler has to deal with the refrigerant properly. If you hire a private hauler, it's fair to ask how they handle it.

What the City drop-off won't take

Chicago's Household Chemicals & Computer Recycling Facility (HCCRF) on Goose Island does not accept household appliances, including microwaves, stoves, refrigerators, washers, dryers, humidifiers and dehumidifiers. It also turns away air conditioners, HVAC equipment and refrigerants chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[7]. For what it does take, see our Chicago hazardous waste and electronics guide.

Frequently asked questions

Can I put a refrigerator out with the trash in Chicago?
Not without calling first. The City says to remove the doors or lids from discarded refrigerators and freezers and to arrange a bulk pickup with your Streets and Sanitation ward superintendent before putting out any large item. chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1]
Does Chicago charge to pick up a refrigerator?
City-serviced homes pay a $9.50 per dwelling unit monthly garbage fee, and the City's Garbage Fee FAQ says there is no additional cost for bulk waste collection. chicago.gov (Department of Finance)[3]
Why do I have to take the doors off my fridge?
The City's sanitation guidelines tell residents to always remove doors or lids from discarded refrigerators and freezers before arranging a bulk pickup. chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1]
Can I drop off a refrigerator at the Goose Island recycling facility?
No. The Household Chemicals & Computer Recycling Facility lists household appliances, including refrigerators, stoves, washers, dryers and dehumidifiers, plus air conditioners and refrigerants, as not accepted. chicago.gov (Department of Environment)[7]
Can I just take my old fridge to a landfill in Illinois?
Illinois law bans landfilling white goods, including refrigerators, freezers, ranges, water heaters and air conditioners, unless parts like CFC refrigerant and mercury switches have been removed first. Illinois General Assembly[8]
I live in a 5+ unit building. Who takes my fridge?
City garbage service covers single-family homes and buildings of four units or less. Owners of larger apartment buildings must arrange private or licensed scavenger service, so ask your property manager. chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1] chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2]
