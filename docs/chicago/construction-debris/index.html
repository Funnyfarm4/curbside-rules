How to Get Rid of Construction and Remodeling Debris in Chicago (2026)

Chicago won't collect remodeling debris: you must remove it yourself. Dumpster permit rules, what ReStore Chicago reuses, and the fines for dumping it.

The short version

Chicago's garbage service does not take remodeling waste. The Department of Streets and Sanitation says that if you're remodeling, you're responsible for removing discarded building materials, and that dumpsters can be rented for the job chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1].

That leaves three realistic routes: reuse what's still good (ReStore Chicago takes some building materials), rent a dumpster, or hire a hauler to load it and take it away. Whatever you do, don't leave it in the alley; Chicago fines illegal dumping heavily chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[5].

What counts as construction debris

The City describes construction and demolition (C&D) debris as non-hazardous waste from construction, remodeling, repair or demolition, including chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2]:

  • Bricks, concrete, rock and other masonry
  • Wood, including non-hazardous painted, treated and coated wood
  • Scrap metal
  • Plaster and gypsum drywall
  • Plumbing fixtures and piping
  • Non-asbestos insulation
  • Roofing shingles and other roof coverings
  • Glass and plastics

Why you can't put it out for bulk pickup

City-serviced homes (single-family homes and buildings of four units or fewer) can arrange a bulk pickup with their ward Streets and Sanitation superintendent before putting out a large item or an extra load of trash chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1]. Remodeling debris is called out separately: the homeowner removes it chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1].

If you're getting rid of a couch, mattress or appliance during a renovation, those can still go through the City's bulk process. See our Chicago 311 bulk pickup guide.

Option 1: Reuse what's still good (ReStore Chicago)

ReStore Chicago accepts new or gently used building materials along with furniture and appliances, and you can drop off or request a free pickup ReStore Chicago (Habitat for Humanity Chicago)[6]. Items are subject to staff review and must be clean and in good condition Habitat for Humanity Chicago[7].

Building materials it lists as accepted Habitat for Humanity Chicago[7]:

  • Complete kitchen cabinet sets with working doors and drawers (no scratches, nails, mold or mildew)
  • Lumber and trim more than 4 feet long (not warped, splintered or with nails)
  • New siding and drywall, packaged insulation, plywood, and unopened shingles and tar paper
  • Bricks, stone and cinderblocks
  • Sinks, toilets, bathtubs, bathroom vanities and medicine cabinets (no chips, stains or rust)
  • Hardwood and vinyl flooring over 100 sq ft, and ceramic or glass tile over 50 sq ft
  • New interior and exterior doors and new windows

It won't take used doors and windows, countertops, carpet rolls, shower doors, cast iron sinks or tubs, wall ovens, paint or toxic materials Habitat for Humanity Chicago[7] ReStore Chicago (Habitat for Humanity Chicago)[6].

Chicago's garbage trucks won't take drywall, tile, lumber or old cabinets from a remodel. A junk-removal crew can load and haul it in one trip, or you can rent a dumpster. Confirm they serve your ZIP, what materials they take, and the price before you book.

Option 2: Rent a dumpster

On the street, alley, parkway or sidewalk

A dumpster on the public way needs a CDOT dumpster permit, and it's the dumpster provider that applies for it, not you City of Chicago (CDOT)[4]. For a dumpster of 30 cubic yards or less outside the Central Business District (downtown), CDOT's fee table lists $50 for 1 to 3 days or $100 per month; fees double for bigger dumpsters and again downtown City of Chicago (CDOT)[4].

In your driveway, yard or private lot

CDOT says the dumpster permit isn't necessary when the dumpster sits on private property, though other permits may apply City of Chicago (CDOT)[4].

Bigger projects: the 50% recycling rule

Under §11-4-1905 of the Chicago Municipal Code, new construction, demolition and renovation projects that meet the code's requirements must recycle or reuse at least 50% of their construction or demolition debris, by weight chicago.gov (Department of Buildings)[3]. Contractors on those projects must chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2]:

  1. Track how much C&D debris the project generates.
  2. Recycle at least 50% of the recyclable debris.
  3. Submit a Recycling Compliance Form to the Department of Public Health at the end of the project, with an affidavit from the waste hauler or recycler.

If you've hired a contractor, ask up front whether debris removal and recycling are in their price. The City's page links to the ordinance text for which projects must comply chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2].

Leftover paint, solvents and chemicals

Paint is on ReStore's do-not-accept list Habitat for Humanity Chicago[7]. Take leftover paint, stains and solvents from a project to the City's household chemicals drop-off; see Chicago's hazardous waste and electronics drop-off guide.

Don't dump it

Illegal or "fly" dumping is dumping any waste on public or private property without a permit, and construction debris is one of the most commonly dumped materials chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[5]. The City says offenders face fines starting at $1,500 for a first offense, jail time of up to 6 months, up to 200 hours of community service, and vehicle impoundment at the owner's expense chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[5]. To report dumping, call 311 (or 911 if it may be hazardous waste) chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[5].

Frequently asked questions

Will Chicago garbage pick up construction debris?
No. The Department of Streets and Sanitation says that if you're remodeling, you're responsible for removing the discarded building materials yourself, and it notes that dumpsters can be rented for this purpose. chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1]
Can I schedule a 311 bulk pickup for drywall or old cabinets?
The City's guidance separates the two: you arrange a bulk pickup with your ward Streets and Sanitation superintendent for large items or an extra load of household trash, but remodeling debris is the homeowner's responsibility to remove. Plan on a dumpster, a hauler, or reuse instead. chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[1]
Do I need a permit for a dumpster for my remodel in Chicago?
Only if it sits on the public way, such as the street, alley, parkway or sidewalk. Then the dumpster provider needs a CDOT permit. CDOT says the permit isn't necessary if the dumpster is on private property, though other permits may apply. City of Chicago (CDOT)[4]
Does ReStore Chicago take building materials?
Yes, some. Its guidelines list things like complete kitchen cabinet sets, lumber and trim over 4 feet, new drywall, packaged insulation, unopened shingles, bricks and stone, sinks, toilets and vanities in good condition. It does not take used doors and windows, countertops, carpet rolls, shower doors or cast iron sinks and tubs. Habitat for Humanity Chicago[7] ReStore Chicago (Habitat for Humanity Chicago)[6]
What is the fine for dumping construction debris in Chicago?
The City says illegal dumpers face fines starting at $1,500 for a first offense, jail time of up to 6 months and up to 200 hours of community service, and their vehicles can be impounded at the owner's expense. chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[5]
Does my contractor have to recycle the debris?
For projects covered by the City's construction and demolition recycling ordinance, contractors must track the debris, recycle at least 50% of what's recyclable, and file a recycling compliance form with an affidavit from the hauler or recycler. chicago.gov (Department of Streets and Sanitation)[2] chicago.gov (Department of Buildings)[3]
