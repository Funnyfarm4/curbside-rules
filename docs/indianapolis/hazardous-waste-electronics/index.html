The short version

In Indianapolis, electronics and household chemicals can't go out with the trash. Indiana's e-waste law (IC 13-20.5) bans electronics from curbside and heavy trash pickup, and pouring or trashing hazardous waste creates real environmental and health hazards indy.gov[2]. The City's answer is ToxDrop, Saturday drop-offs for Marion County residents that take both household hazardous waste and electronics indy.gov[1]. For electronics on a weekday, the nonprofit RecycleForce takes drop-offs on the near east side RecycleForce[5].

Option 1: ToxDrop (Saturdays)

ToxDrop is only for Marion County residents and doesn't accept commercial waste indy.gov[1].

  • 1st Saturday, 9 a.m. to 2 p.m.: Traders Point Collection Facility, 7550 N. Lafayette Rd.
  • 2nd and 4th Saturdays, 9 a.m. to 2 p.m.: Perry Township Government Center, 4925 S. Shelby St.

The sites are closed on the third and fifth Saturdays and on all major holidays. Don't leave anything at a closed site: that's illegal dumping, with fines up to $2,500 indy.gov[1]. The City also runs occasional drive-thru disposal events when resources allow indy.gov[1].

Limits and packing

  • Up to 20 gallons of liquid or 75 pounds of solid waste per visit indy.gov[1].
  • Up to five computer systems per visit indy.gov[1].
  • Bring it in plastic crates, cardboard boxes or storage bins you can see into. Waste in trash bags may not be accepted indy.gov[1].

What ToxDrop takes

Paint and finishing: paint, paint stripper, paint thinner, wood stains, wood varnishes, floor wax, furniture polish indy.gov[1].

Garage and yard: gasoline, used motor oil, brake fluid, lighter fluid, lead acid batteries, propane tanks under 20 lbs, fertilizer, herbicides, insecticides, pesticides, pool chemicals, rock salt indy.gov[1].

Around the house: AA and AAA batteries, aerosol cans, adhesives and glue, air fresheners, ammonia, bathroom and oven cleaners, carpet cleaners, chlorine bleach, detergents, stain removers, moth crystals, nail polish and remover, used cooking oil, fire extinguishers indy.gov[1].

Bulbs and mercury: compact fluorescent bulbs, fluorescent tubes, LED bulbs, thermostats, thermometers, elemental mercury indy.gov[1].

Electronics: computers and laptops, monitors, TVs 27 inches or smaller (including flat screens), printers, scanners, fax machines, keyboards, mice, hard drives and USB drives, modems, cable and satellite boxes, DVD and VHS players, gaming systems, e-readers, cameras, GPS units, MP3 players and UPS batteries indy.gov[1].

Option 2: RecycleForce (electronics, weekdays)

RecycleForce is a nonprofit electronics recycler at 816 N. Sherman Dr. It takes consumer drop-offs Monday to Friday 8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. and Saturday 9 a.m. to 1 p.m. Pull up at the southwest corner of the building and honk; staff help unload RecycleForce[5]. Call 317-532-1367 with questions RecycleForce[5].

  • What it takes: basically anything with a cord, plus some metals and limited residential Styrofoam (one carload max) RecycleForce[5] RecycleForce[6].
  • Cost: free, except requested donations of $25 per TV or monitor (CRT, flat screen or projection) and $20 per refrigerator, freezer, air conditioner or dehumidifier RecycleForce[6] RecycleForce[5].
  • What it won't take: paint or other household hazardous waste, smoke detectors, vapes and e-cigarettes, or anything contaminated with chemicals or food RecycleForce[5] RecycleForce[6].
  • Big TVs: this is the place for a TV over 27 inches, since ToxDrop's limit is 27 inches indy.gov[1] RecycleForce[6].

Data-bearing devices are taken apart and hard drives are physically destroyed RecycleForce[5].

What about fridges and other big appliances?

Appliances containing Freon can't go in the trash indy.gov[2]. The City will pick up a refrigerator, freezer or AC unit if you schedule it through the Mayor's Action Center at 317-327-4622; allow ten business days indy.gov[4]. See our Indianapolis appliance and tire guide for the details.

Hiring a hauler for the rest

A garage or basement cleanout usually turns up more than paint cans and old TVs. Furniture and bulky items can go out as heavy trash (two items a month) or to the Citizens' Transfer Station indy.gov[3]. If you hire someone to haul it, ask to see their Waste Hauler License and don't pay without a disposal receipt indy.gov[3].

Clearing out a garage or basement?

ToxDrop and RecycleForce handle the chemicals and electronics, but not the furniture, boxes and junk around them. A junk-removal crew can haul the rest in one trip. Confirm they serve your ZIP and check the price before you book.

Don't leave it at a recycling bin site

Frequently asked questions

Where can I get rid of old paint in Indianapolis?
Take it to a ToxDrop event. Paint, paint thinner, paint stripper, stains and varnishes are all accepted from Marion County residents, up to 20 gallons of liquid per visit. RecycleForce does not take paint. indy.gov[1] RecycleForce[5]
When is ToxDrop open?
From 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. on the first Saturday of the month at Traders Point Collection Facility, 7550 N. Lafayette Rd., and on the second and fourth Saturdays at Perry Township Government Center, 4925 S. Shelby St. Sites are closed on the third and fifth Saturdays and major holidays. indy.gov[1]
Can I put a TV out for heavy trash in Indianapolis?
No. Electronics can't go in curbside or heavy trash under Indiana's e-waste law, and haulers won't collect TVs with electronic components intact. ToxDrop takes TVs 27 inches or smaller; RecycleForce takes larger TVs for a $25 requested donation. indy.gov[2] indy.gov[1] RecycleForce[6]
Who can use ToxDrop?
ToxDrop is for Marion County residents and doesn't accept commercial waste. indy.gov[1]
How do I pack hazardous waste for ToxDrop?
Bring it in plastic crates, cardboard boxes, storage bins or other containers that are easy to see into. Waste brought in trash bags may not be accepted. indy.gov[1]
What happens to the data on my old computer?
RecycleForce says it follows RIOS guidelines: PCs are taken apart by hand and hard drives are separated and physically destroyed. RecycleForce[5]
