Do You Need a Dumpster Permit in Indianapolis? Cost and How to Apply (2026)

A dumpster or container on an Indianapolis street or sidewalk needs a Right-of-Way Use permit from DBNS. Who can apply, 2026 fees, and the 7-10 day timeline.

Quick answer

  • On the street or sidewalk? You need a permit. Container placement in the public right-of-way requires a Right-of-Way Use Permit. indy.gov[2]
  • Who issues it: the Department of Business and Neighborhood Services (DBNS), 317-327-8700, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. weekdays. indy.gov[1]

The short version

If a dumpster, roll-off or storage container will sit on an Indianapolis street, parking lane or sidewalk, you need a Right-of-Way Use Permit. The City's right-of-way regulations say this permit is required for activities in the public right-of-way that aren't excavation, and they list container placement by name indy.gov[2].

The permit comes from the Department of Business and Neighborhood Services (DBNS), not the trash department. You apply online in the Accela Citizens Access Portal indy.gov[1], and DBNS says to allow 7 to 10 business days indy.gov[3].

Do you need one?

  • Street, curb lane, parking lane or sidewalk: yes. A separate permit is required for each location or street indy.gov[3].
  • Your own driveway or yard: the right-of-way rules are about public space, meaning traffic lanes, shoulders, sidewalks and parking lanes indy.gov[3]. We didn't find an indy.gov page that covers a container kept entirely on private property. If it's close to the line, or you're unsure where your property ends, call DBNS at 317-327-8700 before delivery indy.gov[1].
  • Next to parking meters: don't remove or block meters without a permit. The application has to say if you'll restrict ParkIndy meters, IndyGo buses, the Cultural Trail or other trails indy.gov[3].

What it costs in 2026

DBNS updated many of its fees on January 5, 2026, and applications received on or after that date use the new schedule indy.gov[4]. On that schedule, every right-of-way permit has a $40 application fee, plus a daily review fee based on what you close indy.gov[5]:

  • Use of sidewalk: $50 per day
  • Partial closure, regular road (inside or outside the downtown Regional Center): $75 per day
  • Partial closure, thoroughfare: $100 per day
  • Total closure, regular road: $75 per day outside the Regional Center, $100 per day inside it

The 2026 schedule doesn't have a separate line for dumpsters or containers indy.gov[5], so DBNS decides which category fits when it reviews your application. The City's 2021 right-of-way regulations listed container placement at $118 a week outside the Regional Center and $166 a week inside it indy.gov[2], but those amounts predate the 2026 fee update, so treat the 2026 schedule as current and confirm with DBNS.

What's the Regional Center? Roughly downtown: bounded by 16th Street on the north, I-65/I-70 on the east, Morris Street on the south and the Belt Railroad on the west, plus half a block on each side of Meridian Street from 16th to 30th Street indy.gov[3].

How to apply

  1. Book the dumpster first, then ask who pulls the permit. A licensed contractor isn't required for a use permit, but contractors usually make the request indy.gov[1] indy.gov[3].
  2. Create an Accela account. An account is required to apply, and no paper application is needed indy.gov[6]. All requests go through the online system indy.gov[3].
  3. Fill in the details DBNS asks for indy.gov[3]:
  • the exact legal address of the parcel nearest the container
  • the exact date or date range
  • the area you're restricting: traffic lane, shoulder, sidewalk or parking lane, and which side of the street
  • whether you'll affect parking meters, bus stops, bike lanes or trails
  1. Be ready for a traffic plan. DBNS may ask for a Maintenance of Traffic (MOT) plan showing signs, barrels and how pedestrians, bikes and wheelchairs get around the container, especially if you close a sidewalk indy.gov[3]. An unlicensed applicant on a large job that uses several lanes may need a site plan indy.gov[1].
  2. Wait for review and pay. Plan on 7 to 10 business days indy.gov[3]. Permit updates come by email, and the permit is emailed to you once it's issued indy.gov[6].

Need more time? You can extend a right-of-way permit once, before it expires, with the Amendment / Extension Form. More fees may apply, and after that you need a new permit indy.gov[3].

Questions to ask DBNS

  • General permit questions: DBNS at 317-327-8700, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. Monday to Friday, 200 E. Washington St. indy.gov[1], or permitquestions@indy.gov indy.gov[4].
  • Right-of-way office: 317-327-8583 or row@indy.gov indy.gov[7].
  • Portal trouble: ACA.Admin@Indy.gov indy.gov[6].

Picking a dumpster company

The City's advice for big cleanouts is to rent a dumpster or hire a responsible hauler, and to make sure they dispose of the waste properly. Check their background, references and whether they're licensed by the City, and ask for a copy of the disposal receipt indy.gov[8].

Only have a carload or a pickup load? The Citizens' Transfer Station, 2324 S. Belmont Ave., takes extra household and heavy trash on Saturdays from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. for $2 a carload or $5 an SUV or pickup, cash only. Trailers and moving trucks aren't allowed indy.gov[9].

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit for a dumpster in Indianapolis?
If it sits in the public right-of-way, yes. The City's right-of-way regulations say a Right-of-Way Use Permit is required for activities in the right-of-way that aren't excavation, and they name container placement as one of them. indy.gov[2]
How much is a dumpster permit in Indianapolis?
The DBNS fee schedule effective January 5, 2026 charges $40 per right-of-way application plus a daily review fee based on the closure, for example $50 a day for use of a sidewalk, $75 a day for a partial closure of a road outside the Regional Center, and $100 a day on a thoroughfare. The schedule has no separate dumpster line, so DBNS sets the exact category when it reviews your application. indy.gov[5]
Do I need a permit if the dumpster is in my driveway?
The City's right-of-way rules cover public space such as traffic lanes, shoulders, sidewalks and parking lanes. We didn't find an indy.gov page that addresses a container kept entirely on private property, so if you're unsure, ask DBNS at 317-327-8700 before delivery. indy.gov[3] indy.gov[1]
Does a storage container or POD need a permit in Indianapolis?
The regulations say container placement in the right-of-way needs a Right-of-Way Use Permit and don't separate trash dumpsters from other containers, so plan on a permit for any container placed on the street or sidewalk. indy.gov[2]
How long does a right-of-way permit take in Indianapolis?
DBNS says to expect a 7 to 10 business day turnaround on standard requests, longer if you submit several at once or need extra approvals. indy.gov[3]
Can I extend my dumpster permit?
Once. A right-of-way permit can be extended one time before it expires, using the Amendment / Extension Form, and more fees may apply. After that you need a new application. indy.gov[3]
