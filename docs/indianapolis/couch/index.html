How to Get Rid of a Couch in Indianapolis (2026)

Indianapolis takes couches and other furniture as heavy trash once a month, 2 items max. Find your day, or drop it at the transfer station for $2 to $5.

The short version

Indianapolis picks up couches for free as part of heavy trash. Household furniture such as a couch, desk, dresser, table or chairs is accepted indy.gov[1], and homes on City trash service get heavy trash pickup once a month on an assigned day indy.gov[2]. You can set out up to two heavy or bulky items, and the limit is two a month indy.gov[2].

There's no appointment to book. The catch is timing: if you just missed your heavy trash day, or you're clearing out more than two pieces, you'll need to haul the rest yourself or pay someone.

Option 1: Heavy trash day (free, 2 items a month)

  1. Find your day. Enter your address in the City's trash day lookup. It shows your regular and heavy trash days. The heavy trash day comes once a month and varies by address indy.gov[2]. You can also call the Mayor's Action Center (MAC) at 317-327-4622, Monday to Wednesday and Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., or Thursday 8 a.m. to 2 p.m. and 3 p.m. to 5 p.m. indy.gov[4]
  2. Count your items. A couch counts as one item. A couch plus a recliner is your two for the month indy.gov[2].
  3. Set it out. Put it at the curb next to your trash cart on your heavy trash day indy.gov[2]. Collection starts at 7 a.m. indy.gov[2], and the City doesn't list a separate set-out time for heavy items, so have it out before then.
  4. Holiday weeks: pickup slides one day later in a week with an observed holiday, including Thanksgiving and Christmas. Holidays that fall on a weekend aren't observed indy.gov[2].

If something isn't acceptable, your service provider leaves a tag explaining how to meet the pickup requirements indy.gov[2]. Building a sofa or deck from scratch? Construction debris isn't accepted as heavy trash indy.gov[1].

Getting rid of a mattress at the same time? Mattresses and box springs are heavy trash too, laid flat on the ground, not leaning on anything indy.gov[1], and each one counts toward the two-item limit. See our Indianapolis heavy trash guide for the full list.

Missed pickup

Don't report it the same day. Crews have until 6 p.m., and the MAC call center closes at 5 p.m. Report it the next business day at 317-327-4622 and allow three business days indy.gov[4].

Option 2: Drop it off yourself

Marion County Citizens' Transfer Station indy.gov[3]:

  • Address: 2324 S. Belmont Ave.
  • Hours: Saturdays only, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. Closed the Saturday closest to Christmas Day and the Saturday closest to New Year's Day.
  • Cost: $2 per carload or $5 per SUV or pickup truckload, cash only.
  • Limits: up to two trips a day. Moving trucks, dual axles, trailers, haulers, stake-bed trucks and truckloads larger than 3/4 ton aren't permitted.

That last rule matters for couches: if it only fits on a trailer or in a rented moving truck, the transfer station won't take that load.

South Side Landfill, 2561 Kentucky Ave., is the City's fallback if nothing else works. It's open Monday to Friday 5 a.m. to 7 p.m. and Saturday 5 a.m. to 3 p.m., closed Sunday, and pricing is based on volume indy.gov[1].

Option 3: Donate it

If the couch is still in good shape, the City suggests donating it first and keeps a list of local donation centers at indy.gov/recycle indy.gov[5]. Call ahead, since each charity sets its own rules.

Hiring a hauler

For more than two pieces, a whole-house cleanout or a couch stuck on an upper floor, the City's advice is to rent a dumpster or hire a responsible trash hauler indy.gov[6]. Before you pay:

  • Ask to see their Waste Hauler License and don't pay without a disposal receipt indy.gov[5].
  • The City says properly disposing a truckload costs contractors at least $60, so be suspicious of a lower price indy.gov[5].
  • Check their background and references, whether they're licensed by the City, and how they plan to dispose of it. Some people who offer cheap removal dump the load illegally indy.gov[6].

Don't dump it

Leaving furniture on public or private property without permission violates Sec. 601-2 of the municipal code, with fines of up to $2,500 per instance indy.gov[6]. Couches and mattresses left at City recycling drop-off sites count as illegal dumping too indy.gov[5]. If someone dumps on your property, you're still responsible for the cleanup, and if a City contractor has to do it you're billed at least $418 indy.gov[6]. Report dumping through RequestIndy or the MAC at 317-327-4622 indy.gov[6].

Frequently asked questions

Does Indianapolis pick up couches?
Yes. Household furniture, including couches, desks, dressers, tables and chairs, is accepted as heavy trash. Put it at the curb next to your trash cart on your monthly heavy trash day. indy.gov[1] indy.gov[2]
How do I schedule furniture pickup in Indianapolis?
You don't schedule it. Heavy trash is collected once a month on a set day for your address. Look up the day with the City's trash day tool or call the Mayor's Action Center at 317-327-4622. indy.gov[2] indy.gov[4]
How many pieces of furniture can I put out for heavy trash?
Two. The City allows up to two heavy or bulky items on your heavy trash day and limits you to two a month. A sofa and a loveseat would be your two items for that month. indy.gov[2]
Where can I take an old couch in Indianapolis?
The Marion County Citizens' Transfer Station at 2324 S. Belmont Ave. takes heavy trash on Saturdays from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. It costs $2 a carload or $5 for an SUV or pickup truckload, cash only, with up to two trips a day. Trailers and moving trucks aren't allowed. South Side Landfill, 2561 Kentucky Ave., is another option, priced by volume. indy.gov[3] indy.gov[1]
Can I leave a couch at a recycling drop-off site?
No. Furniture, mattresses and other non-recyclables left at City drop-off sites count as illegal dumping, with fines up to $2,500. indy.gov[5]
What if my couch wasn't picked up?
Crews have until 6 p.m., so wait until the next business day, then report it to the Mayor's Action Center at 317-327-4622 and allow three business days. If an item isn't acceptable, the crew leaves a tag explaining why. indy.gov[4] indy.gov[2]
