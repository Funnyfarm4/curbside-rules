The short version

There's no single "cheap rent" list for Indianapolis, but official data tells you what's normal and where rents run lowest. HUD's Fair Market Rents estimate the 40th-percentile gross rent (rent plus utilities) for a standard-quality unit, so about 40% of comparable rentals cost less HUD User[1]. If an ad is far below these numbers, look at it closely, because a rent well below the local norm is one of the FTC's warning signs of a scam FTC[12].

For listings, use live searches sorted by price (below) instead of any list of prices, which goes stale within days.

Indianapolis rents by bedroom count

HUD's FY 2027 Fair Market Rents for the Indianapolis-Carmel, IN HUD Metro FMR Area, which includes Marion County and eight surrounding counties HUD User[1]:

  • Studio: $1,187 a month
  • 1 bedroom: $1,336
  • 2 bedrooms: $1,536
  • 3 bedrooms: $1,991
  • 4 bedrooms: $2,425

For comparison, the Census Bureau's 2024 American Community Survey puts the median gross rent actually paid in Indianapolis at $1,219 a month (±$33) U.S. Census Bureau[4]. That median covers every rented home in the city, all sizes, including people who've been in the same place for years.

Income-based and subsidized housing

HUD-assisted apartments. HUD's map shows subsidized apartments near you: go to HUD Resource Locator, choose "Find Affordable Housing Opportunities Near Me," enter a ZIP, then call the properties. HUD notes the map shows locations and contacts only. It doesn't list vacancies or waiting lists, and you apply with each property's management HUD[10].

Indianapolis Housing Agency (IHA). IHA serves Indianapolis and Marion County. It runs public housing, about 9,000 Housing Choice Vouchers and more than 1,600 affordable units Indianapolis Housing Agency[6]:

  • Section 8 vouchers: waiting list closed. IHA says it is processing applications selected in its 2016 lottery, and future openings will be announced in advance on indyhousing.org and in the media Indianapolis Housing Agency[5].
  • Applying: IHA takes applications through its online Applicant Portal (a RentCafe login) Indianapolis Housing Agency[7].
  • Contact: 317-261-7200, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., 1919 N. Meridian St., Indianapolis, IN 46202 Indianapolis Housing Agency[6].
  • With a voucher, you rent from a private owner: an apartment, town home, house, duplex or mobile home Indianapolis Housing Agency[5].

IHCDA (state housing agency). IHCDA says it does not cover the city of Indianapolis or Marion County for vouchers. If you'd live in a surrounding county, use its map of local providers and check its waiting-list notices IHCDA[9]. IHCDA also points renters to energy bill help (the Energy Assistance Program) and to local Community Action Agencies IHCDA[8].

Help lines

  • Indiana 2-1-1: dial 2-1-1 for free, confidential help finding local resources, 24 hours a day IHCDA[8]. HUD says to press 6 for homeless services HUD[10].
  • Tenant Information Hotline: 317-327-2228. Call right away if you think your landlord will file an eviction or you've received an eviction notice indy.gov[11].
  • Housing discrimination: report it to HUD's Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity at 1-800-669-9777 HUD[10].

Avoid rental scams

The FTC's main warning signs FTC[12]:

  • Rent far below the area's rents, especially with pressure to decide fast.
  • The "owner" can't show the place and asks for money first.
  • Payment only by wire transfer, gift cards or crypto. The FTC says that's a scam every time, and the money is usually gone for good.
  • Copied listings. Search the address with the owner's or company's name. If the same address shows up with a different owner, or isn't on the management company's own site, be suspicious.
  • Requests for your SSN or credit score before you've agreed to rent. Landlords normally pull credit themselves.

To check who owns a property, the FTC suggests looking up county property records and matching them to the landlord's ID FTC[12]. IHA also tells voucher holders to see a unit in person before signing anything and to ask neighbors about the area Indianapolis Housing Agency[5]. Report scams at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, to local police and to the site where the ad appeared FTC[12].

Move-in costs and getting your deposit back

Budget for an application fee, a security deposit and the first month's rent, and get every amount in writing before you pay. When you move out, Indiana law gives the landlord 45 days after the lease ends and you return the unit to send back your deposit, minus any itemized deductions for unpaid rent, damage beyond normal wear, or unpaid utilities. You have to give the landlord a mailing address in writing first. If the landlord doesn't follow these rules, you can recover the full deposit plus reasonable attorney's fees Indiana Code via Justia[13].

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest area to rent in Indianapolis?
Going by HUD's FY 2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents, the lowest 2-bedroom benchmarks inside Indianapolis ZIP codes are 46201 and 46235 ($1,310), 46218 ($1,330) and 46229 ($1,350). These are HUD benchmarks, not asking rents, so individual listings can be higher or lower. HUD User[2]
How much is a 1-bedroom apartment in Indianapolis?
HUD's FY 2027 Fair Market Rent for a 1-bedroom in the Indianapolis-Carmel area is $1,336 a month, including utilities. By ZIP code, HUD's 1-bedroom benchmark ranges from $1,140 (46201 and 46235) to $2,000 (46236 and 46278). HUD User[1] HUD User[2]
What is the average rent in Indianapolis?
The Census Bureau's 2024 American Community Survey puts median gross rent (rent plus utilities) in Indianapolis at $1,219 a month, with a margin of error of ±$33. U.S. Census Bureau[4]
Is the Indianapolis Section 8 waiting list open?
No. The Indianapolis Housing Agency says its Section 8 waiting list is closed and that it is processing applications from its 2016 lottery. Future openings will be announced in advance on indyhousing.org and through the media. You can call IHA at 317-261-7200. Indianapolis Housing Agency[5] Indianapolis Housing Agency[6]
Can IHCDA give me a voucher if I live in Indianapolis?
No. IHCDA runs vouchers only where there's no local housing authority, and it says it does not cover the city of Indianapolis or Marion County. In Indianapolis, vouchers go through the Indianapolis Housing Agency. IHCDA[9]
How long does an Indiana landlord have to return my security deposit?
Up to 45 days after the lease ends and you hand back the unit. The landlord has to return the deposit minus itemized deductions in writing, but only after you give them a mailing address in writing. Indiana Code via Justia[13]
