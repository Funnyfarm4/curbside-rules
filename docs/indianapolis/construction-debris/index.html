How to Get Rid of Construction and Remodeling Debris in Indianapolis (2026)

Indianapolis won't take construction or remodeling debris as trash or heavy trash. Landfill rates ($25.50 a cubic yard), what to donate, and dumpster rules.

The short version

Indianapolis doesn't collect remodeling or construction debris. Construction waste can't go in your trash cart and construction debris isn't accepted as heavy trash indy.gov[1]. Drywall, lumber, shingles, broken tile, old cabinets and demo rubble all need another route: haul them to a landfill yourself, rent a dumpster, hire a hauler, or donate whatever is still usable.

Option 1: Haul it to South Side Landfill

South Side Landfill is a permitted municipal solid waste landfill that accepts construction and demolition debris, garage and basement cleanout debris, and clean fill South Side Landfill[4]. The City lists it as the place to go if none of its other options work indy.gov[1].

  • Address: 2561 Kentucky Ave., Indianapolis, IN 46221, 317-247-6808 South Side Landfill[3]
  • Hours: Monday to Friday 5 a.m. to 7 p.m., Saturday 5 a.m. to 3 p.m., closed Sunday. Holiday hours vary, so call ahead South Side Landfill[4].
  • Payment: cash, check, and debit or credit cards, but not American Express South Side Landfill[4].

What it costs

Cash customers pay by volume South Side Landfill[3]:

  • C&D and drywall: $25.50 per cubic yard
  • Shingles: $28.00 per cubic yard
  • Clean fill: $13.00 per cubic yard
  • Open-top solid waste (household junk): $25.50 per cubic yard
  • Car: $21.00
  • Pickup truck: priced from the landfill's chart by how full the bed is. SUVs and vans are charged like a similar pickup. Flat-bed, dump-bed and box trucks are measured and charged by the cubic yard.

Every load also pays a fuel surcharge that changes monthly, and solid waste carries a state fee of $0.60 per ton. Call 317-247-6808 for this month's surcharge South Side Landfill[3]. The landfill's online calculator estimates a load from its length, width and height South Side Landfill[3].

Sort your load

A mixed load is charged at the rate of the most restrictive material in it South Side Landfill[3]. Clean fill means only uncontaminated rocks, brick, concrete, road demolition material or dirt. Wood, drywall, brush and painted concrete don't count South Side Landfill[4]. Keep broken concrete and brick separate from drywall and lumber, and the concrete goes at the cheaper clean fill rate.

If your load is too heavy to unload, the landfill offers a paid pull-off service: you attach a chain or rope to the load, their equipment pulls it, and you sign a liability release South Side Landfill[4].

What the landfill won't take

No liquids, hazardous waste or infectious waste. Leftover paint has to be solidified first (sawdust, clay-based cat litter or cellulose insulation). Solvents, chemicals, used motor oil and car batteries are household hazardous waste and go to ToxDrop South Side Landfill[4]. See our Indianapolis hazardous waste and electronics guide.

What about the Citizens' Transfer Station?

The City's $2-to-$5 Saturday drop-off at 2324 S. Belmont Ave. is described as the place for extra household trash, heavy trash and bags of leaves indy.gov[2], and construction debris isn't listed. Since construction debris is specifically excluded from heavy trash indy.gov[1], call DPW at 317-327-4000 before you bring remodeling debris there indy.gov[1]. Trailers, moving trucks and loads over 3/4 ton aren't allowed in any case indy.gov[2].

Option 2: Donate usable materials

Pulling out cabinets or fixtures that still work? Habitat for Humanity's ReStore accepts kitchen and vanity cabinet sets with doors attached, countertops, bathtubs, sinks and toilets, plus working ceiling fans with their hardware, lamps and light fixtures, and clean area rugs Greater Indy Habitat for Humanity[5]. The City's donation list notes the ReStore takes new and reclaimed building materials indy.gov[6].

Pickup is free. Call the donation line at West Washington (317-969-8791), Avon (317-707-7530), Fishers (317-896-9423) or Greenfield (317-318-9458), or drop off Tuesday to Saturday, 10 a.m. to 7 p.m. Items must be in good working order, and the ReStore can refuse them because of condition or disposal costs Greater Indy Habitat for Humanity[5]. More options are in our Indianapolis furniture donation guide.

Option 3: Rent a dumpster or hire a hauler

For a kitchen, bathroom or roof job, the City's advice for loads you can't take yourself is to rent a dumpster or hire a responsible trash hauler indy.gov[7].

  • Dumpster on the street or sidewalk: that needs a right-of-way permit from the City. Our Indianapolis dumpster permit guide covers who issues it, the fees and the timeline.
  • Check the hauler: ask to see their Waste Hauler License, and don't pay without a disposal receipt. The City says disposing of a truckload properly costs contractors at least $60, so be suspicious of anything cheaper indy.gov[8].
  • Ask where it's going. Check background, references and whether they're licensed by the City. Some people offering cheap removal dump the load illegally indy.gov[7].

Need a dumpster or a crew to haul it?

Remodeling debris can't go out with City trash, so it's either a landfill run, a rented dumpster, or a junk-removal crew that loads and hauls it for you. Confirm they serve your ZIP and ask how and where they'll dispose of it before you book.

Don't dump it

Leaving debris on public or private property without permission violates Sec. 601-2 of the municipal code, with fines up to $2,500 per instance indy.gov[7]. Wood and debris left at City recycling drop-off sites count as illegal dumping too indy.gov[8]. If someone dumps on your property, you're responsible for the cleanup, and if a City contractor does it you're billed at least $418 indy.gov[7].

Frequently asked questions

Does Indianapolis heavy trash pick up construction debris?
No. The City says construction debris will not be accepted as heavy trash, and construction waste can't go in your trash cart either. indy.gov[1]
Where can I dump construction debris in Indianapolis?
South Side Landfill at 2561 Kentucky Ave. accepts construction and demolition debris. It's open Monday to Friday 5 a.m. to 7 p.m. and Saturday 5 a.m. to 3 p.m. South Side Landfill[4]
How much does South Side Landfill charge for construction debris?
Construction debris and drywall are $25.50 per cubic yard, shingles $28 and clean fill $13. Pickup trucks are priced from a chart by how full the bed is, and cars are a flat $21. Everything also carries a monthly fuel surcharge, and solid waste carries a state fee of $0.60 a ton. South Side Landfill[3]
What counts as clean fill at the landfill?
Only uncontaminated rocks, brick, concrete, road demolition material or dirt. Wood, drywall, brush and painted concrete aren't clean fill. If clean fill is mixed with other debris, the whole load is charged at the higher rate. South Side Landfill[4] South Side Landfill[3]
Can I take remodeling debris to the Citizens' Transfer Station?
The City describes the transfer station as the place for extra household trash, heavy trash and bags of leaves, and it doesn't list construction debris. Heavy trash itself excludes construction debris, so call DPW at 317-327-4000 before you load up. indy.gov[2] indy.gov[1]
What do I do with leftover paint from a remodel?
Liquid paint can't go to South Side Landfill. The landfill says to solidify it first, with sawdust, clay cat litter or cellulose insulation, and to take chemicals, solvents and similar household hazardous waste to ToxDrop. South Side Landfill[4]
