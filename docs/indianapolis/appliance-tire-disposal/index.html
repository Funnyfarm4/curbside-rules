How to Get Rid of a Refrigerator, Appliances or Tires in Indianapolis (2026)

Fridges, freezers and AC units need a Freon pickup via the Mayor's Action Center. Stoves go out as heavy trash. Tires can't go in the trash; where to take them.

The short version

Indianapolis splits appliances into two groups. Appliances with Freon or other refrigerants (refrigerators, freezers, air conditioners) can't go in your trash cart indy.gov[1] and need a separately scheduled Freon pickup indy.gov[2]. Appliances without Freon, like a stove or water heater, can go out as heavy trash indy.gov[1].

Tires are different again. Whole waste tires can't go to Indiana landfills under Indiana Code 13-20-14-1, so by law you can't put them out with curbside or heavy trash pickup indy.gov[1] in.gov/idem[8].

Refrigerators, freezers and AC units: schedule a Freon pickup

  1. Request the pickup through the Mayor's Action Center (MAC). The City's trash request service covers scheduling refrigerator, freezer and AC unit pickup indy.gov[2]. Call 317-327-4622: Monday to Wednesday and Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., Thursday 8 a.m. to 2 p.m. and 3 p.m. to 5 p.m. RequestIndy works online and in the app 24/7 indy.gov[2] indy.gov[6].
  2. Wait up to 10 business days. The City asks you to allow ten business days for a scheduled Freon pickup indy.gov[2].
  3. Don't put it out on heavy trash day. Heavy trash only covers appliances without Freon indy.gov[1] indy.gov[3].

Cost: the City pages we checked don't list a fee for Freon pickup, so ask the MAC when you book indy.gov[2].

Stoves, water heaters and other appliances: heavy trash day

Appliances without Freon, such as a stove or water heater, are on the City's list of accepted heavy trash indy.gov[1]. The rules:

  • Put it at the curb next to your trash cart on your monthly heavy trash day indy.gov[4].
  • It counts toward your limit of two heavy items a month indy.gov[4].
  • Find your heavy trash day with the City's address lookup indy.gov[4].

Full rules are in our Indianapolis heavy trash guide.

Electronics are separate. Indiana's e-waste law (IC 13-20.5) bans electronics from the trash, so they can't go out with curbside or heavy trash. Use the City's ToxDrop program or RecycleForce, 816 N. Sherman Drive, during its weekday hours indy.gov[1] indy.gov[5]. Haulers also won't take TVs with electronics inside indy.gov[1].

Tires: where to take them

Liberty Tire drop-off

The City lists Liberty Tire, a national tire recycler with a collection site at 1009 W. Troy Ave., Indianapolis indy.gov[1]:

  • Open to the public: Monday to Friday, 7:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m.
  • Passenger and light truck tires: $4 per tire
  • Commercial truck tires (semi tractors or trailers): $12.75 per tire
  • Other tires: call (317) 238-0388 for pricing

Trade them in when you buy new tires

Indiana law requires stores that sell new tires to accept at least one waste tire from you for each new tire you buy, and they must post a sign saying so in.gov/idem[9]. The City also points residents to this rule indy.gov[1].

Annual mosquito-season tire drop-off

The Marion County Public Health Department's Mosquito Control Program holds an annual tire drop-off event during mosquito season. The City doesn't post dates, so contact the health department for details indy.gov[1]; its general line is 317-221-2000 indy.gov[6].

A note on the City's FAQ

One City FAQ about the 2026 hauler change lists "automobile tires mounted on their rims/wheels" as heavy trash indy.gov[3]. The City's main Trash 101 page says tires can't go out with curbside or heavy trash pickup by law indy.gov[1], so we recommend following that stricter rule. If you're unsure, call the MAC at 317-327-4622 before setting tires out.

Too big a job to haul yourself?

If you hire someone, the City says to ask for their Waste Hauler License and not pay without a disposal receipt indy.gov[5]. Check whether they're licensed by the City and ask how they'll dispose of it indy.gov[7].

Don't dump it

Dumping tires or appliances on public or private property without permission violates Sec. 601-2 of the municipal code, with fines of up to $2,500 per instance indy.gov[7]. Open dumping or open burning of waste tires is also illegal under Indiana law in.gov/idem[8]. Report dumping through RequestIndy or the MAC at 317-327-4622 indy.gov[7].

Frequently asked questions

How do I get rid of a refrigerator in Indianapolis?
Schedule a Freon pickup through the Mayor's Action Center at 317-327-4622. The City handles refrigerators, freezers and AC units this way, not on heavy trash day, and asks you to allow ten business days for collection. indy.gov[1] indy.gov[2]
Does Indianapolis charge to pick up a refrigerator?
The City's pages we checked don't list a fee for Freon appliance pickup. Ask the Mayor's Action Center when you schedule it. indy.gov[2]
Can I put a stove or washer out for heavy trash?
Appliances without Freon, such as a stove or water heater, are on the City's list of accepted heavy trash. Each counts toward your limit of two heavy items a month. indy.gov[1] indy.gov[4]
Can I throw tires in the trash in Indianapolis?
No. Landfills can't accept whole waste tires under Indiana Code 13-20-14-1, so the City says tires can't go out with curbside or heavy trash pickup. indy.gov[1] in.gov/idem[8]
Where can I drop off old tires in Indianapolis?
The City lists Liberty Tire at 1009 W. Troy Ave., open to the public Monday to Friday, 7:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. Passenger and light truck tires cost $4 each and semi truck or trailer tires $12.75. For other tires, call (317) 238-0388. indy.gov[1]
Will a tire shop take my old tires?
If you're buying new ones, yes. Indiana law requires stores that sell new tires to accept at least one waste tire for each new tire sold. in.gov/idem[9] indy.gov[1]
