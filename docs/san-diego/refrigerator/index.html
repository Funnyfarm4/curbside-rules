How to Get Rid of a Refrigerator in San Diego (2026)

San Diego has no City bulky pickup for fridges right now. The Miramar Recycling Center takes them for $25; Goodwill's Otay Mesa Outlet accepts donated fridges.

The short version

In the City of San Diego, getting rid of a fridge right now mostly means doing the hauling yourself or paying someone:

  1. The City isn't picking up bulky items. Its planned bulky item pickup is paused until further notice sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1], and major appliances can never go in a City bin sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[2].
  2. You can drop it off at the Miramar Recycling Center for $25 sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[3], if you can get it there.
  3. To donate it, Goodwill's Otay Mesa Outlet takes refrigerators Goodwill Industries of San Diego County[7].

Why the City won't take it

When San Diego cut its solid waste fees for fiscal years 2028 and 2029, it paused two planned upgrades, weekly recycling and bulky item pickup, until further notice. Regular trash, recycling and organics collection continues sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1].

Your bins don't help either. The City's list of things that can never go in any bin includes major appliances, water heaters and items over 50 pounds sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[2].

Don't leave it on the curb. San Diego Municipal Code section 54.0209 bans placing waste in public spaces without authorization. Fines run up to $1,000 per violation, and each day it stays can count as a separate violation sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[6].

Option 1: The Miramar Recycling Center ($25)

sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[3]

  • Where: Miramar Landfill entrance, 5165 Convoy St., San Diego, CA 92111, north of State Route 52
  • Hours: Monday to Saturday, 7 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Closed Sundays and on New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving Day and Christmas Day.
  • Run by: Allan Company, for the City
  • Questions and current prices: 858-268-8971

What it costs

sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[3]

  • Refrigerators: $25 each
  • Freezers: $25 each
  • Air conditioners and other appliances containing CFCs: $25 each
  • Washers, dryers, dishwashers, stoves, microwaves, hot water heaters and other appliances: $12 each

The City says all pricing is subject to change, so call before you go sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[3].

Don't drive it into the landfill

The Miramar Landfill fee sheet says appliances, CRTs, TVs, e-waste and household hazardous materials must be properly disposed of before entering the landfill sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[4]. Stop at the Recycling Center first; it's at the same entrance sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[3]. If you have other junk in the load, a car, SUV, pickup or small trailer of general refuse from inside the City is $75 at the landfill, and uncovered loads cost an extra $50 sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[4].

Option 2: Donate it

  • Goodwill Industries of San Diego County accepts large household appliances (ovens, stoves, refrigerators) at its Otay Mesa Outlet, not at its regular donation centers Goodwill Industries of San Diego County[7]. Questions: 619-225-2200, ext. 197, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Goodwill Industries of San Diego County[7]
  • San Diego Habitat for Humanity closed both of its ReStores (Kearny Mesa and National City) in October 2024, so it no longer takes appliance donations San Diego Habitat for Humanity[8].

Not a City of San Diego resident?

The Recycling Center page doesn't list a residency requirement; call 858-268-8971 to confirm before you go sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[3]. At the landfill itself, loads from outside the City pay more: $86 instead of $75 for a Tier 1 load of general refuse sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[4].

Hiring someone to haul it

San Diego Municipal Code §66.0108 bars anyone from collecting and hauling solid waste in the City for hire without a City franchise agreement, and their vehicles must carry a City-issued tag or decal San Diego Municipal Code[5]. You can always haul your own fridge to a licensed facility yourself San Diego Municipal Code[5].

Want it hauled away?

The City of San Diego isn't picking up bulky items right now, and the Miramar Recycling Center only helps if you can load the fridge into a truck and drive it there yourself. A junk-removal crew can carry it out of the kitchen and haul it off. San Diego requires anyone hauling waste for hire in the City to hold a City franchise, so ask the company to confirm it does, check that it serves your ZIP, and get the price before you book.

Is there an SDG&E rebate?

Not that SDG&E's website shows right now. Its Rebates page lists offers for smart thermostats, heat pump water heaters and room air conditioners, and no refrigerator recycling or pickup program sdge.com (San Diego Gas & Electric)[9].

What about the Freon?

Under federal rules, the final person in the disposal chain, such as a scrap metal recycler or landfill, is responsible for making sure refrigerant is recovered before a household refrigerator or freezer is disposed of. If an appliance arrives with its refrigerant already gone, that facility must keep a signed statement from whoever dropped it off saying who recovered it and when U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (epa.gov)[10].
