Do You Need a Dumpster Permit in San Diego? (2026)

A dumpster in a San Diego street or sidewalk needs a Traffic Control Permit: $176.57 for up to 4 weeks plus an $87.68 processing fee. Rules and lead time.

The short version

Renting a dumpster for a cleanout or remodel? Two City rules matter:

  1. If the dumpster will sit in the street, a parking space, an alley or over the sidewalk, you need a Traffic Control Permit from the Development Services Department sandiego.gov (Development Services)[1] sandiego.gov (Development Services)[2]. It costs $176.57 for up to 4 weeks plus an $87.68 processing fee sandiego.gov (Development Services)[1] sandiego.gov (Development Services)[3].
  2. The company hauling it must hold a City of San Diego franchise. City code bars anyone from collecting and hauling solid waste in the City without one San Diego Municipal Code[5].

Start early. The bulletin asks for at least 3 working days' notice, but the City warns that new Traffic Control Permits can take about 15 business days to process sandiego.gov (Development Services)[1] sandiego.gov (Development Services)[2].

When you need a permit

The City requires a Traffic Control Permit for a temporary storage container or dumpster that encroaches into the public right of way, including the sidewalk area (San Diego Municipal Code §129.0702) sandiego.gov (Development Services)[1]. The Traffic Control Permit page lists "the installation of a temporary dumpster or storage container in the public right of way" as one of the things that needs one, and says there are no exemptions sandiego.gov (Development Services)[2].

The City's homeowner permit guide puts it plainly: moving pods, dumpsters and other temporary structures "require a permit if they encroach on the public right of way, such as sidewalks or streets" sandiego.gov (Development Services)[4]. A dumpster that sits entirely on your own driveway or lot, without hanging over the sidewalk, isn't what these rules describe.

What it costs

sandiego.gov (Development Services)[1] sandiego.gov (Development Services)[3]

  • Traffic Control dumpster fee: $176.57, covering up to 4 weeks (the July 2026 bulletin lists it as "per month")
  • Traffic Control processing fee: $87.68, flat
  • Metered parking: $20 per meter, per day for each meter the dumpster blocks

For one dumpster that doesn't block a meter, that's $264.25 in City fees. Pay online by e-check or credit card (bank and processing fees apply), by check made out to "City Treasurer" in the drop-off safe at the downtown Development Services Center, or at an in-person payments appointment sandiego.gov (Development Services)[1].

A moving pod or storage container is a different, shorter permit: $87.68 per week, for a maximum of one week, plus the same processing fee sandiego.gov (Development Services)[1].

How to apply

  1. Fill out the Traffic Control Plan/Permit Form (DS-269) and the Project Contacts Information form (DS-345) sandiego.gov (Development Services)[2].
  2. Draw a traffic control plan. For a dumpster it needs the start and end dates, the street and nearest cross streets, the owner's name, address, phone and email, and a plan view showing exactly where the dumpster goes, with distances to the nearest driveways, a north arrow, any parking meters (with meter numbers), painted curb zones, bus stops and driveway openings, plus the street width, parking lane width and the dumpster's dimensions sandiego.gov (Development Services)[1].
  3. Apply online. Everything is submitted electronically through the City's Traffic Control Permit page, where you create an account sandiego.gov (Development Services)[1] sandiego.gov (Development Services)[2]. PDFs must meet the City's upload requirements or review is delayed sandiego.gov (Development Services)[2].
  4. Questions: contact Traffic Control by virtual appointment or email DSDTrafficSafety@sandiego.gov sandiego.gov (Development Services)[1].

Ask your dumpster company whether it will prepare and file the permit for you before you book.

Placement rules

sandiego.gov (Development Services)[1]

  • Not in front of a painted curb: no red (no parking), white (passenger loading) or blue (accessible parking) zones.
  • At least 10 feet of clearance from the dumpster door to the nearest obstruction.
  • At least 25 feet from the dumpster door to the alley or curb return.
  • At least 15 feet between the dumpster door and a fire hydrant.
  • Other cars can't park in the leftover space of a dumpster zone; violators can be cited.

Before and during the rental

  • Post Tow-Away/No Parking signs 72 hours before the dumpster is placed, and deactivate any parking meters where applicable sandiego.gov (Development Services)[1].
  • Extensions: submit a new request with your latest approved, stamped permit. After three extensions, the City requires a new signed DS-269 sandiego.gov (Development Services)[2].
  • Inspections: City staff can inspect without notice and change the approved plan if field conditions call for it sandiego.gov (Development Services)[1].
  • No permit? Work in the right of way without an approved permit can bring a stop-work order, a notice of violation and fines, and the City back-bills the fees sandiego.gov (Development Services)[3].

Pick a City franchised hauler

San Diego Municipal Code §66.0108 says no person may collect and haul residential or commercial solid waste in the City without a current City franchise agreement, and every collection vehicle must carry a City-issued tag or decal San Diego Municipal Code[5]. The City's Environmental Services Department publishes a Franchise Hauler Directory listing the approved companies, their phone numbers and the equipment they offer, roll-offs included. The City doesn't assign haulers or set their rates, so call a few for prices sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[6].

There are exceptions. You can haul your own waste to a licensed facility yourself, and a licensed contractor can remove the construction debris from its own job with its own employees and equipment San Diego Municipal Code[5].

Remodeling? The debris rules

  • Not in your City bins. Building and construction debris, including dirt, rocks, carpet and large scraps, can never go in a City trash, recycling or organics bin sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[8].
  • Permitted projects must recycle. Most construction, demolition and remodeling projects that need a building, combination or demolition permit fall under the City's C&D Debris Deposit Ordinance: submit a Waste Management Form, pay a refundable deposit, divert at least 65% of the debris by weight (for permits issued since July 1, 2016), and turn in weight receipts within 180 days of final inspection to get the deposit back. The City recommends using a franchised hauler to help ensure compliance sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[7].
  • Hauling it yourself? At the Miramar Landfill, a car, SUV, pickup or trailer up to 5 ft x 9 ft x 4 ft (Tier 1) of C&D debris from inside the City is $108 a load, or $122 from outside the City, through June 30, 2027. Uncovered loads cost an extra $50 sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[9].

Need a dumpster, or want to skip the permit?

If the job is a one-time cleanout, a junk-removal crew loads and hauls everything the same day, so there's no container in the street, no Traffic Control Permit and no 72-hour sign posting. San Diego requires anyone hauling waste for hire in the City to hold a City franchise, so ask the company to confirm it does, check that it serves your ZIP, and get the price before you book.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit for a dumpster in San Diego?
Only if it encroaches into the public right of way. The City requires a Traffic Control Permit for a temporary dumpster or storage container in the street, sidewalk, parking lane or alley. Its permit guide says dumpsters and moving pods need a permit if they encroach on the public right of way, such as sidewalks or streets. sandiego.gov (Development Services)[1] sandiego.gov (Development Services)[2] sandiego.gov (Development Services)[4]
How much is a dumpster permit in San Diego?
Development Services lists a Traffic Control dumpster fee of $176.57 (up to 4 weeks) plus a flat $87.68 processing fee, so about $264.25 for one placement. If the dumpster blocks metered parking, add $20 per meter per day. sandiego.gov (Development Services)[1] sandiego.gov (Development Services)[3]
How long does a San Diego dumpster permit take?
The bulletin requires at least three working days' notice before the dumpster is placed, but the Traffic Control Permit page warns that new permits and extensions can take about 15 business days to process. Apply as early as you can. sandiego.gov (Development Services)[1] sandiego.gov (Development Services)[2]
Where can't a dumpster go in San Diego?
Not in front of a red, white or blue painted curb. The City also requires at least 10 feet of clearance from the dumpster door to the nearest obstruction, 25 feet to an alley or curb return, and 15 feet to a fire hydrant. sandiego.gov (Development Services)[1]
Do I need a permit if the dumpster is in my driveway?
The City's bulletins tie the Traffic Control Permit to containers that encroach into the public right of way, including the sidewalk. If the dumpster sits entirely on your property and doesn't hang over the sidewalk, those permit rules don't describe it. If you're unsure, email DSDTrafficSafety@sandiego.gov. sandiego.gov (Development Services)[1] sandiego.gov (Development Services)[4]
Can any company rent me a dumpster in San Diego?
City code says no person may collect and haul residential or commercial solid waste in the City without a City franchise agreement, and their trucks must carry a City-issued tag or decal. The City publishes a Franchise Hauler Directory you can choose from. San Diego Municipal Code[5] sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[6]
