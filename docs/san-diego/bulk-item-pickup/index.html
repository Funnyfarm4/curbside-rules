Does San Diego Have Bulk Item Pickup? What to Do Instead (2026)

San Diego's City bulky item pickup is paused. Where to take furniture, mattresses and appliances instead, with Miramar Landfill fees and hours.

The short version

The City of San Diego doesn't currently pick up bulky items. A bulky item pickup service was planned, but the City paused it until further notice when it lowered solid waste fees for fiscal years 2028 and 2029 sandiego.gov[2] sandiego.gov[1]. The City's own mattress page puts it plainly: it "does not provide bulky item pickup or curbside pick-up services" sandiego.gov[4].

Your regular trash, recycling and organics pickup continues as normal sandiego.gov[1]. But big items can't ride along: the City says furniture, appliances and auto parts (water heaters, major appliances, couches and chairs) and anything over 50 pounds must never go in any City bin sandiego.gov[3].

So you'll need to drop it off, catch a community cleanup event, or hire a hauler. Here's how each works.

Option 1: Mattresses and box springs (free drop-off)

Mattresses are the easiest. Through the Bye Bye Mattress program, the City takes mattresses and box springs for free at the Miramar Landfill sandiego.gov[4]:

  • Where: Mattress Collection Site, east side of the entrance at 5161 Convoy St., San Diego, CA 92111, next to the Household Hazardous Waste Transfer Facility.
  • When: Monday to Friday, 7 a.m. to 3 p.m. Closed Saturdays, Sundays and six holidays (New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving and Christmas).
  • Limit: none.
  • Futons: futon mattresses count, but only if they come off the frame.

Not accepted: mattresses that are severely damaged, wet, twisted, frozen, soiled or infested with bedbugs; toppers and pads; air mattresses, waterbeds and rollaways; crib mattresses; sofa beds and futon frames; and mattresses still in a box or plastic bag sandiego.gov[4]. The City says mattresses too soiled to recycle go in the trash, and landfill fees may apply if you bring a mattress in with other waste sandiego.gov[4].

The Bye Bye Mattress program doesn't do curbside pickup either. It's a network of drop-off points and recyclers; the City links to its find-a-facility map for other locations sandiego.gov[4].

Option 2: Haul it to Miramar Landfill

For couches, tables, carpet and general junk, the City's landfill is the main option. Miramar Landfill, 5180 Convoy St., is open Monday to Saturday, 7 a.m. to 4 p.m., closed Sundays and the same six holidays sandiego.gov[5]. Self-dumping vehicles are accepted until 4:30 p.m. sandiego.gov[5], but there are no hand unloads after 4 p.m. sandiego.gov[6].

What it costs (July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027)

Cars, pickups and small trailers pay a flat rate per load based on the vehicle and the kind of waste sandiego.gov[6]. For general refuse sandiego.gov[7]:

  • Tier 1 (car, sedan, crossover, SUV, minivan, pickup, van, or any trailer up to 5 ft W x 9 ft L x 4 ft H): $75 for waste from inside the City, $86 from outside it.
  • Tier 2 (pickup with trailer, larger trailers, medium-heavy trucks): $148 City, $156 non-City.
  • Construction and demolition debris costs more: $108 per Tier 1 load.

Rules to know before you go

  • Cover your load. Uncovered loads are charged an extra $50 sandiego.gov[6].
  • No appliances, TVs, electronics or hazardous waste in your landfill load. They must be disposed of properly before you enter the landfill sandiego.gov[6]. See Option 3.
  • Payment: checks, major credit cards and Visa or Mastercard debit cards with matching ID sandiego.gov[6]. Cash is no longer accepted sandiego.gov[7].
  • Bad weather: some vehicles and trailers may be restricted in rain. Recorded information: 858-492-6100 sandiego.gov[7].
  • Fee questions: Miramar Recycling Center, 858-268-8971 sandiego.gov[6].

Option 3: Appliances, TVs and electronics

Take these to the Miramar Recycling Center, at the landfill entrance at 5165 Convoy St. It's open Monday to Saturday, 7 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. sandiego.gov[8]. Posted prices, which the City says are subject to change sandiego.gov[8]:

  • Refrigerators, freezers, air conditioners (Freon appliances): $25 each
  • Washers, dryers, dishwashers, stoves, microwaves, water heaters and other appliances: $12 each
  • TVs, computer monitors, CPUs and laptops: free
  • Small electronics like vacuums and coffee makers: $0.25 per pound (49-pound limit per item)
  • Tires (18 inches and under, no rims): $7 each

Call 858-268-8971 to confirm pricing and accepted items before you go sandiego.gov[8].

Option 4: Community cleanup events

The City runs nearly 100 community cleanup and recycling events a year, organized with each City Council district, where residents can get rid of unwanted bulky items and other accepted materials sandiego.gov[9]. The City says council offices and staff pick the neighborhoods most in need, so check with your council district office for upcoming dates near you sandiego.gov[9].

Option 5: Don't have a truck? Hire a hauler

If you can't haul it yourself, you'll need a private junk removal company. If you live on a private road, in a multifamily property, or anywhere already served by a private waste hauler, the City says to use a City-approved private franchise hauler sandiego.gov[3]; the City publishes a franchised hauler list sandiego.gov[5].

Don't leave it on the curb

Putting furniture, mattresses, appliances or other waste on a street, sidewalk, park or someone else's property without permission is illegal dumping under San Diego Municipal Code section 54.0209 sandiego.gov[9]. Fines run up to $1,000 per violation, and each day the waste stays can count as a separate violation sandiego.gov[9].

If you spot dumped items, report them through the City's Get It Done illegal dumping form or call 858-694-7000. After hours, on weekends or holidays, urgent hazards go to 619-527-7500 sandiego.gov[9].

Frequently asked questions

Does San Diego pick up bulk items?
Not right now. The City says bulky item pickup was a planned upgrade that is paused until further notice, and its mattress page states that the City does not provide bulky item pickup or curbside pick-up services. sandiego.gov[1] sandiego.gov[4]
When will San Diego start bulky item pickup?
The City hasn't given a date. When it reduced solid waste fees for fiscal years 2028 and 2029 in June 2026, it paused the planned bulky item pickup until further notice to cut costs. sandiego.gov[2] sandiego.gov[1]
Can I put a couch or furniture in my trash bin?
No. The City lists furniture, appliances and items over 50 pounds among the things that can never go in any City bin. sandiego.gov[3]
Where can I drop off a mattress for free in San Diego?
At the Mattress Collection Site at Miramar Landfill, 5161 Convoy St., next to the Household Hazardous Waste Transfer Facility. It's open Monday to Friday, 7 a.m. to 3 p.m., with no drop-off limit. Mattresses must not be wet, badly damaged, soiled or infested with bedbugs. sandiego.gov[4]
How much does it cost to dump at Miramar Landfill?
For City-generated general refuse, a car, SUV, minivan, pickup, van or trailer up to 5 ft x 9 ft x 4 ft (Tier 1) is $75 a load; from outside the City it's $86. Larger vehicles pay more, and uncovered loads cost an extra $50. sandiego.gov[7]
Can I leave furniture on the curb in San Diego?
Leaving waste on a public road, right-of-way or someone else's property without permission is illegal dumping under San Diego Municipal Code section 54.0209, with fines of up to $1,000 per violation. sandiego.gov[9]
