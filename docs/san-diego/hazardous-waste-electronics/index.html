Where to Drop Off Electronics, Paint and Hazardous Waste in San Diego (2026)

San Diego takes paint, batteries and chemicals free by appointment at Miramar, Wed and Sat. Electronics go to the Miramar Recycling Center instead. Rules.

The short version

If you live in the City of San Diego, paint, batteries, motor oil, pesticides, cleaners and other household hazardous waste go to the City's Household Hazardous Waste (HHW) Transfer Facility at the Miramar Landfill. Drop-off is free, but every visit needs an appointment, and it's open only Wednesdays and Saturdays sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1] sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[3].

Electronics are different. The HHW facility won't take them sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1]. Take TVs, monitors, computers and laptops to the Miramar Recycling Center next door, where they're free to recycle sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[5].

Option 1: The HHW Transfer Facility (by appointment)

Where and when

sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1]

  • Address: Miramar Landfill entrance, 5161 Convoy St., San Diego, CA 92111, north of State Route 52
  • Hours: Wednesday and Saturday, 9 a.m.-3 p.m., except holidays
  • Upcoming closures: November 28, 2026, December 26, 2026 and January 2, 2027

Expect traffic delays: the facility shares its entrance with the landfill and recycling center sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1].

How to book

  1. Go to the City's Get It Done HHW appointment page (sandiego.gov/HHW) sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[3] sandiego.gov (Get It Done)[2].
  2. Check off the types of waste you're bringing and enter the residential address where the waste is sandiego.gov (Get It Done)[2].
  3. Pick a time between 9 a.m. and 3 p.m. sandiego.gov (Get It Done)[2].

Need help instead? On the same form you can ask for help if you have more than 15 gallons or 125 pounds, can't transport it, don't know what it is, or have industrial-grade chemicals. The City says it will contact you within 3 business days sandiego.gov (Get It Done)[2].

Who can use it and what it costs

  • Free for City of San Diego residents sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1].
  • Bring proof of City residency: a picture ID or a recent bill showing your name and address sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1] sandiego.gov (Get It Done)[2].
  • No business waste (contractors, landscapers, homeowner associations) and no waste from nonprofits sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1].
  • Not a City resident? Unincorporated San Diego County residents can call 877-713-2784; residents of other cities should contact their own city sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1].

What it takes

Leftover or unused products labeled Caution, Warning, Danger, Poison, Toxic, Flammable or Corrosive, used for routine home, yard or vehicle maintenance sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1]. The City's list includes sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[3] sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1]:

  • Paint (unused and surplus)
  • Motor oil and oil filters, antifreeze
  • Batteries: all types of home and automotive batteries
  • Pesticides, pool and garden chemicals
  • Household cleaners and aerosols
  • Fluorescent tubes, CFLs and LED bulbs (incandescent bulbs can go in the trash)
  • Needles, lancets and sharps
  • Prescription and over-the-counter medications (no prescription painkillers)
  • Propane cylinders and fire extinguishers

What it won't take

sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1]

  • Electronics and appliances
  • Swollen or reacting batteries
  • Treated wood, asbestos, lead paint waste
  • Containers larger than 5 gallons
  • Compressed gas cylinders other than propane and fire extinguishers (no oxygen, SCUBA, welding, refrigerant or CO2 tanks)
  • Explosives (ammunition, fireworks, marine flares), radioactive material, PCB capacitors and transformers

For anything on that list, enter your information on the appointment form and an inspector will contact you within three business days sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1].

Limits and what to expect

  • Maximum 15 gallons or 125 pounds per trip. The 15 gallons means the actual contents, not the container size sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1]. You certify on the booking form that you won't exceed it sandiego.gov (Get It Done)[2].
  • Stay in your vehicle. Staff unload for you sandiego.gov (Get It Done)[2] sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[3].
  • Keep the materials in the trunk or truck bed, not the passenger cabin sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1].
  • Call the Environmental Services Department at 858-694-7000 about loads over 50 pounds, leaky or open containers, products you can't identify, or special services for elderly or disabled residents without transportation sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1].

Option 2: One-day collection events (oil, batteries, bulbs)

The City runs free one-day collection events, 9 a.m.-1 p.m., for a short list of items: used oil, oil filters, auto batteries, household batteries and fluorescent bulbs. No other waste is accepted sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[4]. Upcoming dates sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[4]:

  • Saturday, February 13, 2027: San Diego Miramar College, 10440 Black Mountain Rd., Parking Lot 2 (92126)
  • Saturday, March 13, 2027: South Shores, 404 Sea World Dr. (92109)

Electronics: the Miramar Recycling Center

The Miramar Recycling Center sits at the same landfill entrance, 5165 Convoy St., and is run for the City by Allan Company sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[5].

  • Hours: Monday-Saturday, 7 a.m.-4:30 p.m.; closed Sunday and major holidays including Thanksgiving and Christmas sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[5]
  • Free: computer monitors, CPUs, laptops and televisions sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[5]
  • Small electronics (coffee makers, vacuums) and small computer devices (printers, keyboards, mice): $0.25 per pound, 49-pound limit per item sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[5]
  • Appliances: refrigerators, freezers and air conditioners $25 each; dishwashers, dryers, water heaters, microwaves, stoves and washers $12 each sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[5]
  • Prices can change; call 858-268-8971 to confirm sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[5]

Paint, batteries and medications: other drop-offs

  • Paint: besides the HHW facility, you can drop off paint free at PaintCare California sites. Most take up to 5 gallons, in containers of 5 gallons or smaller, and won't take leaking, unlabeled or empty cans sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1].
  • Batteries: the City points to Call2Recycle drop-off locations, including for e-bike batteries. Store used batteries by type, tape the terminals, and bag any that leak sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1].
  • Medications: the San Diego Police Department runs a prescription drug drop-off program sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1].

Why you can't just toss it

Disposing of hazardous waste in the sewer, storm drain, ground or trash is illegal, the City says sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1]. Home-generated sharps have been banned from trash, recycling and green waste containers under California law since Sept. 1, 2008 sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1].

Frequently asked questions

Where can I drop off hazardous waste in San Diego?
City of San Diego residents can drop off household hazardous waste free at the Household Hazardous Waste Transfer Facility at the Miramar Landfill entrance, 5161 Convoy St., north of State Route 52. It's open by appointment only on Wednesdays and Saturdays from 9 a.m. to 3 p.m., except holidays. sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1]
How do I make a San Diego HHW appointment?
Book online through the City's Get It Done HHW appointment page (sandiego.gov/HHW). You pick the materials, give the address the waste comes from, and choose a time between 9 a.m. and 3 p.m. You'll be asked for proof of City of San Diego residency, such as a driver's license or recent utility bill, at your appointment. sandiego.gov (Get It Done)[2] sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[3]
Does the San Diego hazardous waste facility take electronics?
No. The City's HHW Transfer Facility doesn't accept electronic waste or appliances. The Miramar Recycling Center next door recycles computer monitors, CPUs, laptops and TVs for free and charges fees for some other electronics and appliances. sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1] sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[5]
Where can I recycle a TV in San Diego?
The Miramar Recycling Center, 5165 Convoy St., lists televisions, computer monitors, CPUs and laptops as free to recycle. It's open Monday to Saturday, 7 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Call 858-268-8971 to confirm accepted items. sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[5]
Can I throw batteries in the trash in San Diego?
No. The City says all types of batteries are considered hazardous waste in California and aren't allowed at the solid waste landfill. Take them to the HHW Transfer Facility by appointment or a City one-day collection event. sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1]
What if I live outside the City of San Diego?
The City facility only accepts household hazardous waste from City residents. Residents of unincorporated San Diego County can call 877-713-2784; residents of other cities should contact their own city. sandiego.gov (Environmental Services)[1] sandiego.gov (Get It Done)[2]
