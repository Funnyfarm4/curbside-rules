Do You Need a Dumpster Permit in Phoenix? (2026)

Phoenix rents residents dumpsters for $165 a pull plus $55 a ton. Putting a private dumpster on the street? Here's the right-of-way permit and who to call.

Quick answer

  • The City will rent you one. Phoenix Public Works rents 10, 20, 30 and 40 cubic yard dumpsters to residents with an active, paid-up city services account. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]
  • City price: $165 per pull (pickup and emptying) plus $55 per ton of what's inside, for up to 10 days. Longer rentals cost extra. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]
  • How to request: the myPHX311 Roll-Off Rental form. Staff call within 48 hours to set size, date and placement. Questions: 602-262-6251. myPHX311 (City of Phoenix)[2]
  • Private dumpster in the street or on the sidewalk? Temporary use of a Phoenix street, sidewalk or bike lane falls under a right-of-way temporary use permit. There's no application fee; file at least 2 business days ahead (3 for a full closure). Phoenix City Code[3] Phoenix City Code[4]
  • Who to ask: Street Transportation's Right-of-Way Management Office, 602-262-6235 (weekdays 6 a.m. to 2:30 p.m.) or rmp@phoenix.gov. phoenix.gov (Street Transportation)[5]

The short version

Phoenix gives you two routes to a dumpster:

  1. Rent one from the City. Public Works rents roll-off dumpsters to residents for $165 per pull plus $55 per ton, for up to 10 days phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]. You request it online and City staff work out the size, date and placement with you myPHX311 (City of Phoenix)[2].
  2. Rent from a private company. If the container will sit in a public street, sidewalk or bike lane, that's temporary use of the City's right-of-way, which City Code handles with a right-of-way temporary use permit from the Street Transportation Department Phoenix City Code[3]. There's no application fee Phoenix City Code[4].

Phoenix doesn't publish a dumpster-specific permit page. The City Code rules below cover any temporary use of a street or sidewalk. Before a private dumpster is dropped in the street, confirm with Right-of-Way Management at 602-262-6235 or rmp@phoenix.gov phoenix.gov (Street Transportation)[5].

Option 1: Rent a dumpster from the City of Phoenix

Who can rent

  • Residents with an active city services account phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]
  • The account must be current, with no past-due amount phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]
  • You'll sign a liability release and a service contract before the dumpster is delivered phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]

What it costs

  • $165 per pull. That's what the City charges to pick up and empty the dumpster phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].
  • $55 per ton, based on what the dumpster weighs at pickup phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].
  • Up to 10 days. There's an additional fee if you need it longer phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].

So a load that weighs 2 tons comes to $165 + $110 = $275 at the City's posted rates. Heavy material like dirt, roofing or tile adds up fast at $55 a ton.

Sizes

  • 10 yard (13 x 8 x 4 feet)
  • 20 yard (23 x 8 x 5 feet)
  • 30 yard (23 x 8 x 6 feet)
  • 40 yard (23 x 8 x 8 feet) phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]

You don't pick the size on the form. A customer service specialist decides it with you after you submit your request phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].

How to request

  1. Fill out the Roll-Off Rental Request form on myPHX311. It asks for your address, desired delivery date, and whether the bin should go on your property or in front of your property myPHX311 (City of Phoenix)[2].
  2. A Public Works employee contacts you within 48 hours to set the bin size, delivery date and placement myPHX311 (City of Phoenix)[2].
  3. Your request isn't confirmed until that call happens myPHX311 (City of Phoenix)[2].

Questions about a request: 602-262-6251 myPHX311 (City of Phoenix)[2].

Not allowed: hazardous waste phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].

Option 2: A private dumpster in the street or on the sidewalk

Which permit applies

City Code lets the City issue right-of-way temporary use permits for the temporary use or restriction of the public right-of-way, "including, but not limited to, a street, sidewalk, or designated bicycle lane" Phoenix City Code[3]. Street Transportation's Right-of-Way Management Office coordinates temporary street restrictions phoenix.gov (Street Transportation)[5].

Cost and lead time

  • Application fee: none for a right-of-way temporary use permit Phoenix City Code[4].
  • Partial street restriction or sidewalk restriction: apply at least 2 business days before the first day Phoenix City Code[4].
  • Full restriction (closing the street): at least 3 business days ahead Phoenix City Code[4].
  • The City's TRACS application form says permits take 48 hours to process; submit by email to RMP@phoenix.gov phoenix.gov (Street Transportation)[6].

What permit holders must do

  • Carry insurance naming the City as an additional insured, and agree to indemnify the City Phoenix City Code[3].
  • Follow the City of Phoenix Traffic Barricade Manual. Cones, barricades and other temporary traffic control devices must be put up and taken down by someone with the proper certification Phoenix City Code[3].
  • The City can suspend or revoke a temporary use permit Phoenix City Code[3].

In practice, that means the dumpster company (or the contractor running your job) is usually better placed to apply than a homeowner. Ask whoever is renting you the dumpster whether they'll handle the right-of-way permit and barricades.

Contact

  • Right-of-Way Management Office: 602-262-6235, Monday to Friday except City holidays, 6 a.m. to 2:30 p.m. phoenix.gov (Street Transportation)[5]
  • Email: rmp@phoenix.gov phoenix.gov (Street Transportation)[5]

Remodeling? Debris can't go in your City trash cart

Phoenix City Code bars construction and demolition debris from City trash, recycling and green organics containers. If you generate it, you're responsible for removing it promptly, and it can't be stored where it might blow off the site Phoenix City Code[8]. The City may provide containers for construction and demolition debris for a separate fee Phoenix City Code[8], which is the dumpster rental above.

Bulk trash pickup won't solve it either: it doesn't take construction or demolition material over 25 pounds or longer than 4 feet, or bricks, concrete, dirt, rocks, asphalt, or roofing shingles and tiles phoenix.gov (Public Works)[7]. Do I need a permit for a dumpster in Phoenix?

How far ahead do I need to apply?
At least 2 business days before the first day for a partial street restriction or a sidewalk restriction, and at least 3 business days for a full closure. The City's TRACS application form says permits take 48 hours to process. Phoenix City Code[4] phoenix.gov (Street Transportation)[6]
