Phoenix Bulk Trash Pickup: How to Book an Appointment and What They Take (2026)

Phoenix bulk trash is by appointment only: 4 pickups a year, a 10-cubic-yard pile, holiday blackout weeks, what's banned, and free transfer station drop-off.

Quick answer

  • Appointment only. Book online on the City's Bulk Trash page or call 602-262-6251, at least 3 business days ahead and up to a year out. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]
  • 4 pickups a year per household. Cancel at least 2 business days ahead and it won't count. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]
  • One pile, 10 cubic yards max, on your property within 5 feet of the curb and 5 feet from fixed objects, out by 5:30 a.m. on your date and no more than 7 days early. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1] Phoenix City Code[3]

The short version

Phoenix collects bulk trash by appointment only. You get four pickups a year, each a single pile of up to 10 cubic yards at the edge of your property phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].

Book on the City's Bulk Trash page or by calling 602-262-6251 phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].

Heads-up for November: the scheduler blocks Halloween and the 7 days after it, and does the same for Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]. If you need something gone during those weeks, the transfer stations or a hauler are your options.

What counts as bulk trash

The City describes bulk trash as items too large for your garbage or recycling container, such as furniture, small appliances, toilets and large amounts of vegetation phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]. Small appliances without refrigerant, cardboard boxes and up to 25 pounds of household construction debris are also allowed under City Code Phoenix City Code[3].

Mattresses: call first. City Code Section 27-22 lists "all mattresses or furniture" among items not acceptable for normal residential bulk collection Phoenix City Code[3], while the Public Works page names furniture as typical bulk trash phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]. Before putting a mattress or couch out, confirm with Public Works at 602-262-6251.

How to book

  1. Check for a community appointment. Some HOAs and neighborhoods have a group date already set. The City posts the list on its Bulk Trash page; check it before booking your own phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].
  2. Book online or call 602-262-6251. Appointments can be made as late as 3 business days ahead and as far as one year out. Most areas have openings within a couple of weeks phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].
  3. Watch for your confirmation email. It has the link to cancel phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].

You get four collections a year. If you cancel at least 2 business days ahead, using the link in your confirmation email or by calling, the appointment doesn't count against your four phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].

How to set out your pile

  • Book first, then set out. Put items out no more than 7 days before your appointment. Setting out earlier violates City ordinance phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1] Phoenix City Code[3].
  • Out by 5:30 a.m. on your appointment day phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].
  • One pile per household, up to 10 cubic yards. The City's flyer puts that at under 17 feet long, 4 feet wide and 4 feet high phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1] phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2].
  • Place it curbside on your own property, parallel to the street, no more than 5 feet from the curb or front edge of the property phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].
  • Keep it 5 feet from fixed objects, and not on top of a water meter or in the alley phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1] phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2].
  • Bag and tie grass, leaves, weeds, twigs and hedge clippings. Cut branches to under 12 inches across and 4 feet long phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].
  • Don't combine piles with neighbors. Each household needs its own appointment phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].
  • Sweep or rake the spot after pickup if needed phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].

What the City won't take

  • More than 10 cubic yards, loose litter or regular household trash
  • Loose grass, leaves, weeds, twigs and clippings
  • Household hazardous waste such as antifreeze, pool chemicals, oil, batteries and paint
  • Anything in a Bagster bag, or more than 2 wooden pallets
  • Bricks, concrete, dirt, rocks, asphalt and roofing shingles or tiles
  • Construction or demolition material over 25 pounds or longer than 4 feet
  • Metal or car parts over 20 pounds or longer than 4 feet
  • Tires of any size
  • Glass such as windows, shower doors, patio doors, mirrors and fluorescent tubes
  • Tree trunks or stumps longer than 4 feet and thicker than 12 inches
  • Refrigerators, freezers and air conditioners phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]

Under City Code, if you set out banned items or an oversized pile, you're responsible for removing and disposing of it at your own expense Phoenix City Code[3].

Appliances: a separate paid pickup

Refrigerant appliances aren't bulk trash, but the City will pick up large appliances for a fee if you call 602-262-6251 phoenix.gov (Public Works)[5]:

  • Up to 5 non-refrigerant appliances (washers, dryers, stoves, dishwashers, water heaters, microwaves, evaporative coolers): $30 total on your next city services bill.
  • Refrigerators, freezers and AC units: $25 each. Remove the doors, hinges, lids, glass shelves and latches on fridges and freezers first, as state law requires.
  • TVs: $30 for up to 2 CRT sets; no more than 2 TVs per pickup.
  • A cancellation fee applies if you don't cancel before the confirmed day.

Haul it yourself: free monthly transfer station drop-off

Phoenix residential trash customers can drop off up to one ton a month free at either City transfer station. Bring your city services (water) bill and a photo ID with a matching name or address phoenix.gov (Public Works)[4].

  • 27th Avenue Transfer Station, 3060 S. 27th Ave., Phoenix 85009
  • North Gateway Transfer Station, 30205 N. Black Canyon Hwy., Phoenix 85085
  • Hours: Monday to Friday 5:30 a.m. to 5 p.m., Saturday 6 a.m. to 3 p.m., closed Sunday and on City holidays, including Veterans Day, Wednesday, November 11, and Thanksgiving and the day after phoenix.gov (Public Works)[4].
  • Over one ton: $55 a ton. Secure your load or pay a $20 penalty phoenix.gov (Public Works)[4].
  • Appliances and TVs: one refrigerant appliance a year and 2 CRT TVs a month are free; after that it's $20 each plus disposal fees phoenix.gov (Public Works)[5] phoenix.gov (Public Works)[4].
  • Tires: up to 5 car tires a year, rims removed phoenix.gov (Public Works)[4].
  • Spas and boats must be cut into fourths or you pay a $50 penalty, and you need the registration or title phoenix.gov (Public Works)[4].

Frequently asked questions

How do I schedule bulk trash pickup in Phoenix?
Use the scheduling tool on the City's Bulk Trash page or call Public Works at 602-262-6251. Book at least 3 business days ahead; you can book up to a year out, and most areas have openings within a couple of weeks. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]
How many bulk trash pickups do I get in Phoenix?
Four a year per household. An appointment you cancel at least 2 business days ahead doesn't count against the four. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]
Why can't I book bulk trash right after Halloween or Thanksgiving?
The City's scheduler blocks Halloween, Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day and the 7 days after each, to keep bulk piles off the street during those peak weeks. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]
How early can I put bulk trash out in Phoenix?
No more than 7 days before your appointment, and only after you've booked it. Putting it out earlier violates City ordinance. Have it out by 5:30 a.m. on the day. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1] Phoenix City Code[3]
Will Phoenix bulk trash take a refrigerator?
No. Appliances with refrigerant, like fridges, freezers and air conditioners, aren't collected as bulk trash. Book a paid appliance pickup ($25 per refrigerant appliance, billed to your city services bill) or drop it at a transfer station, where one refrigerant appliance a year is free. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1] phoenix.gov (Public Works)[5]
Can I take bulk trash to the dump myself in Phoenix?
Yes. Phoenix residential trash customers can drop off up to one ton a month free at the 27th Avenue or North Gateway transfer station with their city services bill and a matching photo ID. Beyond that it's $55 a ton. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[4]
