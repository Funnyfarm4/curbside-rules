The short version

The City of Phoenix's clear path for an old hot tub is its transfer stations: drain it, cut it into fourths, bring the registration or title, and drop it off. If it isn't cut into fourths, you pay a $50 penalty phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]. Phoenix trash customers get their first ton each month free phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].

Bulk trash pickup isn't a good fit: its page doesn't mention spas, and its weight and length limits rule out a whole tub phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2]. Your other options are a City dumpster rental phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3] or a junk-removal crew that does the tear-down and haul for you.

Why bulk trash isn't the answer

Phoenix's appointment-based bulk trash pickup doesn't list hot tubs or spas as accepted or banned. But it tells you not to include phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2]:

  • Metal or car parts over 20 pounds or over 4 feet long
  • Construction or demolition materials over 25 pounds or over 4 feet long
  • Glass such as windows, shower doors and mirrors
  • Household hazardous waste, including pool chemicals
  • More than 10 cubic yards per residence

A full-size spa is far bigger and heavier than those limits. If you're thinking of setting cut-up panels or the wood surround out for bulk, call Public Works at 602-262-6251 first phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2]. Each household gets 4 bulk pickups a year, booked at least 3 business days ahead phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2]. More: Phoenix bulk trash pickup guide.

Option 1: Cut it up and take it to a City transfer station

The spa rules phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]:

  1. Cut it into fourths. Boats and spas that aren't cut into fourths get a $50 penalty fee.
  2. Bring proof of ownership: the spa's registration or title.

Free monthly load. A Phoenix resident paying full-service trash fees can drop off one load up to 1 ton (2,000 pounds) each calendar month at no charge phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]. At the scale house, show:

  • A copy of your city services (water, sewer, trash) bill showing an active, current account, and
  • A photo ID with a name or address matching the bill. No substitutions phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].

Fees beyond the free load phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]:

  • Over 1 ton: leave a deposit, weigh out, and pay $55 a ton for the extra.
  • All other loads: $55 a ton, $40 minimum. Cash, credit and debit accepted; no checks or money orders.
  • Deposits are cash only. They're required when a vehicle and trailer weigh more than 9,000 pounds for a resident with their water bill (6,000 pounds otherwise), and usually run $65 to $180 for residential loads.
  • Secure your load: the unsecured-load penalty is $20.

Locations phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]:

  • 27th Avenue Transfer Station, 3060 S. 27th Ave., Phoenix 85009 (south of Lower Buckeye Rd.)
  • North Gateway Transfer Station, 30205 N. Black Canyon Hwy., Phoenix 85085 (half a mile northeast of the Dixileta Dr. exit, exit 220)

Hours: Monday to Friday 5:30 a.m. to 5 p.m., Saturday 6 a.m. to 3 p.m., closed Sunday phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]. Both close on major holidays, including Thanksgiving and the day after, Christmas and New Year's Day; Christmas Eve hours are 6 a.m. to noon phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].

Option 2: Rent a dumpster from the City

If you're tearing out the tub along with a deck or patio, Phoenix Public Works rents roll-off dumpsters to residents with an active city services account that's current phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3].

  • Price: $165 per pull plus $55 per ton at pickup phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3]
  • Sizes: 10, 20, 30 or 40 yards; a customer service specialist confirms the size after you request phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3]
  • Rental period: up to 10 days, with an extra fee beyond that phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3]
  • You sign a liability release and service contract before delivery, and hazardous waste is prohibited phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3]

The rental page doesn't mention spas, so tell the specialist you're disposing of one when they call. Putting a private dumpster in the street instead? See Do you need a dumpster permit in Phoenix?

Leftover spa chemicals

Pool chemicals are household hazardous waste and can't go out with bulk trash phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2]. See Phoenix hazardous waste and electronics disposal for the City's household hazardous waste options.

Don't dump it

Phoenix asks residents to report illegal dumping in alleys, dumpsters, vacant lots and open desert. If it's happening now, call 911 for emergencies or Crime Stop at 602-262-6151; otherwise call Public Works at 602-262-6251 phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2].

Frequently asked questions

Can I take a hot tub to the Phoenix dump?
Yes, at either City transfer station (27th Avenue or North Gateway). Spas must be cut into fourths to avoid a $50 penalty, and you must bring the registration or title to prove ownership. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]
Is it free to dump a hot tub in Phoenix?
It can be. Phoenix residents paying full-service trash fees get one load of up to 1 ton (2,000 pounds) free each calendar month with a current city services bill and a photo ID with a matching name or address. Anything over a ton is $55 a ton, and an uncut spa adds a $50 penalty. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]
Will Phoenix bulk trash pick up a hot tub?
The City's bulk trash page doesn't list hot tubs or spas either way. It does exclude metal over 20 pounds or 4 feet long, construction or demolition material over 25 pounds or 4 feet long, glass, and pool chemicals, so a whole spa won't meet those limits. Call Public Works at 602-262-6251 before setting any spa pieces out. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2]
What do I do with leftover hot tub chemicals in Phoenix?
Don't put them in bulk trash; pool chemicals are listed as household hazardous waste. Our Phoenix hazardous waste guide covers the City's options for them. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2]
Can I put a hot tub in a City of Phoenix dumpster?
The City's rental page only says hazardous waste is prohibited; it doesn't mention spas. Rentals cost $165 per pull plus $55 per ton, for up to 10 days, for residents with a current city services account. Ask the customer service specialist who calls to confirm your request about the spa and bin size. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3]
