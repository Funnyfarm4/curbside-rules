How to Get Rid of Construction and Remodeling Debris in Phoenix (2026)

Phoenix bulk trash won't take concrete, shingles or debris over 25 lbs. Free monthly ton at transfer stations, City dumpster prices, and reuse drop-off.

The short version

Phoenix's appointment-based bulk trash pickup is not a way to clear out a remodel. It won't take masonry, roofing or concrete at all, and it caps other construction and demolition material at 25 pounds and 4 feet long phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].

Your realistic options are: haul it to a City transfer station (one load up to a ton a month is free for Phoenix trash customers) phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2], rent a City dumpster phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3], donate what's reusable phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2] Stardust Building Supplies[4], or hire a crew to load and haul it.

What bulk trash won't take

The City's bulk trash page tells you not to include phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]:

  • Bricks, concrete, dirt, rocks, asphalt and roofing shingles or tiles
  • Construction or demolition materials over 25 pounds or over 4 feet long
  • Glass such as windows, shower doors, patio doors, mirrors and fluorescent tubes
  • More than 2 wooden pallets
  • Metal or car parts over 20 pounds or over 4 feet long
  • Paint and other household hazardous waste
  • Materials placed in Bagster bags
  • More than 10 cubic yards in total

Small, light leftovers that fit the limits can go in a regular bulk pile. Book an appointment online or at 602-262-6251 at least 3 business days ahead; each household gets 4 pickups a year phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]. Heads-up: the scheduler blocks Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day plus the 7 days after each phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]. Full details: Phoenix bulk trash pickup guide.

Option 1: Haul it to a City transfer station

Phoenix residential trash customers paying full-service trash fees can dispose of one load of up to 1 ton (2,000 pounds) each calendar month free phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2]. At the scale house, show:

  1. A copy of your city services (water, sewer, trash) bill showing an active, current account, and
  2. A photo ID with a name or address matching the bill. No substitutions phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2].

Locations phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2]:

  • 27th Avenue Transfer Station, 3060 S. 27th Ave., Phoenix 85009 (south of Lower Buckeye Rd.)
  • North Gateway Transfer Station, 30205 N. Black Canyon Hwy., Phoenix 85085 (half a mile northeast of the Dixileta Dr. exit, exit 220)

Hours: Monday through Friday 5:30 a.m. to 5 p.m., Saturday 6 a.m. to 3 p.m., closed Sunday phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2]. Both are closed on Thanksgiving and the day after, Christmas and New Year's Day, among other holidays phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2].

Fees beyond the free load phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2]:

  • Over 1 ton: leave a deposit, weigh out, and pay $55 a ton for the extra.
  • All other loads: $55 a ton, $40 minimum. Cash and cards accepted; no checks.
  • Cash deposits are required when a vehicle and trailer weigh more than 9,000 pounds for a resident with their water bill (6,000 pounds otherwise). Residential deposits usually run $65 to $180, cash only.
  • Cover your load: the unsecured-load penalty is $20.

Asbestos: no friable asbestos is accepted. Non-friable asbestos waste needs prior approval; call 602-262-6251 phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2]. Regulated hazardous waste is prohibited, so leftover paint and chemicals go to the City's household hazardous waste program instead. See Phoenix hazardous waste and electronics drop-off.

Option 2: Rent a dumpster from the City

Phoenix Public Works rents roll-off dumpsters to residents with an active city services account that's current with no past-due amount phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3].

  • Price: $165 per pull (to pick up and empty it) plus $55 per ton at pickup phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3]
  • Sizes: 10 yard (13'x8'x4'), 20 yard (23'x8'x5'), 30 yard (23'x8'x6') or 40 yard (23'x8'x8'); a customer service specialist confirms the size after you request phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3]
  • Rental period: up to 10 days, with an extra fee beyond that phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3]
  • You must sign a liability release and service contract before delivery, and hazardous waste is prohibited in the bin phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3]

Option 3: Donate reusable materials

At the transfer station. Bring gently used construction items to the 27th Ave. Transfer Station and ask the scale house attendant about the reusable building materials drop-off, collected by nonprofit Stardust Building Supplies phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2]. Accepted items include phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2]:

  • Cabinets (sets, singles, doors and drawers) and stone, granite or marble countertops
  • Interior, exterior, security and screen doors
  • Tile with no grout, new wood flooring, ceiling tile, new sheetrock and new insulation
  • Bricks, pavers, shingles, lumber and siding
  • Sinks, tubs, toilets, faucets and PVC pipe
  • Working light fixtures and ceiling fans; dual-paned windows, blinds, shutters and screens

Directly to Stardust. Stardust accepts new, excess or gently used building materials, appliances and furniture in resalable condition, and gives a receipt for tax purposes Stardust Building Supplies[4]. You can drop off at its Glendale or Mesa reuse centers six days a week (call ahead for large gifts), or book a home pickup in Maricopa County, Monday to Friday, for a $25 nonrefundable fee on gifts worth at least $100: call 480-964-0177 Stardust Building Supplies[4]. Planning a remodel? Stardust also offers deconstruction services at the same number Stardust Building Supplies[4].

Don't dump it

The City asks residents to report illegal dumping in alleys, dumpsters, vacant lots and open desert. If it's in progress, call 911 for emergencies or Crime Stop at 602-262-6151; otherwise call Public Works at 602-262-6251 phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].

Frequently asked questions

Will Phoenix bulk trash pick up construction debris?
Only small amounts. The bulk trash page says not to include bricks, concrete, dirt, rocks, asphalt or roofing shingles and tiles, or any construction or demolition material over 25 pounds or over four feet long. Windows, shower doors and patio doors are excluded too. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]
Can I take construction debris to the Phoenix dump for free?
Phoenix residential trash customers paying full-service trash fees can drop off one load of up to 1 ton (2,000 pounds) a month free at the 27th Avenue or North Gateway transfer station. Bring your city services bill and a photo ID with a matching name or address. Over a ton is $55 a ton. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2]
How much does it cost to rent a dumpster from the City of Phoenix?
Public Works charges $165 per pull (to pick up and empty the dumpster) plus $55 per ton of what it weighs at pickup. Sizes run from 10 to 40 yards, and you can keep it up to 10 days before an extra fee applies. You need an active, current city services account. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3]
Where can I donate leftover cabinets, doors or tile in Phoenix?
Ask the scale house attendant at the 27th Ave. Transfer Station about the reusable building materials drop-off, which Stardust Building Supplies collects. Stardust also takes gifts at its Glendale and Mesa reuse centers and offers home pickup in Maricopa County for a $25 fee on gifts worth at least $100. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2] Stardust Building Supplies[4]
Does the Phoenix transfer station take asbestos?
No friable asbestos is accepted. Non-friable asbestos-containing waste may be accepted in some cases, but only with prior approval; call 602-262-6251. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2]
