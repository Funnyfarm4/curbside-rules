Where to Get Rid of Electronics, Paint and Hazardous Waste in Phoenix (2026)

Phoenix picks up paint, chemicals, batteries and electronics free at your door once a year. Limits, how to book, what's banned, and where TVs go.

The short version

Phoenix doesn't run a walk-in hazardous waste drop-off. Instead, Public Works picks up household hazardous waste (HHW) at your home, free, once per calendar year, if you have a City residential solid waste account phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[1].

That pickup covers paint, chemicals, automotive fluids, batteries, propane cylinders and a long list of electronics phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[2]. Bigger items like TVs and appliances can also go to the City's two transfer stations phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[3]. Don't put any of it in your trash or recycling container phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[1].

Option 1: Free home pickup (once a year)

Who qualifies

Only customers with a residential solid waste account with the City. You'll need the account number from the upper right corner of your City Services Bill. If you're not sure you qualify, email hhwcollection@phoenix.gov phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[1].

How to book

  1. Check the accepted items list below phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[1].
  2. Fill out the City's HHW collection request form, which shows open appointments within the next 30 days. Have your current bill, account number and service address ready phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[1].
  3. No dates showing? Email hhwcollection@phoenix.gov; the City says its team will work with you on collection accommodations phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[1].
  4. You'll get an email reminder 1-2 business days before pickup phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[1].

General Public Works questions go to 602-262-6251 or solid.waste@phoenix.gov phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[1].

How to set it out

phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[1]

  • Put everything in sealed, leakproof containers, then in a box clearly labeled "HHW." Put a leaking bucket inside a labeled, sealed, leakproof container.
  • Place the box next to your garage or front door, where staff can see and reach it. Not at the curb, in the street or on the sidewalk.
  • Have it out by 7 a.m. Crews come between 7 a.m. and 5 p.m. and don't give a set time.

What it takes, and how much

phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[2]

  • Paint (10 gallons total): household, acrylic, enamel, oil or latex paint; paint thinner and stripper; stains.
  • Chemicals (5 gallons total): cleaning chemicals, small batches of photochemicals, pool chemicals, aerosols.
  • Automotive (5 gallons total): antifreeze, motor oil and other fluids, gasoline, diesel and kerosene, car wash and wax, tire cleaner.
  • Garden (5 gallons total): fertilizers and pesticides.
  • Batteries: single-use alkaline, rechargeable lithium-ion, and lead-acid car batteries.
  • Cylinders: camp stove and grill propane tanks, fire extinguishers.
  • Miscellaneous: fluorescent and CFL bulbs, mercury thermostats, circuit boards, road flares (no shooting flares).
  • Electronics: phones, computers and hard drives, tablets and e-readers, printers, scanners and fax machines, keyboards and mice, modems and routers, cameras, video game equipment, DVD and Blu-ray players, speakers, cables, remotes, power strips, ink cartridges and holiday lights. One monitor and one TV under 50 inches, both with unbroken glass.

What it won't take

Ammunition or explosives, biomedical waste, volatile materials, compressed gases like MAPP or acetylene, medications and sharps, radioactive waste, business or commercial waste, and regular or bulk trash phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[2].

Option 2: Transfer stations for TVs and appliances

Phoenix's two transfer stations take appliances and televisions, including old CRT TVs phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[3]:

  • North Gateway Transfer Station: 30205 N. Black Canyon Hwy, Phoenix, AZ 85085
  • 27th Avenue Transfer Station: 3060 S. 27th Ave, Phoenix, AZ 85009

Hours: Monday to Friday 5:30 a.m. to 5 p.m., Saturday 6 a.m. to 3 p.m., closed Sunday and City holidays. Both are closed Wednesday, November 11 for Veterans Day phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[3].

Cost phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[3]:

  • Residents may bring two CRT units per household per month free. After that it's $20 per CRT plus disposal at $55 a ton ($40 minimum). CRTs aren't accepted from businesses or nonprofits.
  • Residential customers paying full trash fees get one free load of up to 1 ton a month. Bring your City Services Bill and a photo ID that matches it.
  • Up to five car tires a year, rims removed.

The transfer stations don't accept regulated hazardous waste, so paint and chemicals still go through the home pickup phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[3]. For appliances and bulk items, see our Phoenix bulk trash guide.

Old medications

Medications and sharps can't go in the HHW pickup phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[2]. The City suggests pharmacy disposal kiosks, drug take-back events, or a Phoenix Police medication drop box, which takes pills, patches, ointments, vitamins, pet medications and prescription drugs, but not needles, inhalers, aerosols, liquid medicines or thermometers phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix)[4]. Drop boxes include phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix)[4]:

  • Police Downtown Headquarters, 620 W. Washington St.
  • Cactus Park Precinct, 12220 N. 39th Ave.
  • Central City Precinct, 1902 S. 16th St.
  • Mountain View Precinct, 2075 E. Maryland Ave.
  • South Mountain Precinct, 400 W. Southern Ave.

Clearing out a garage, closet or whole house?

Frequently asked questions

Does Phoenix have a household hazardous waste drop-off?
The City's current program is a home collection, not a drop-off: Public Works picks up HHW at your home once per calendar year for residential solid waste customers. The City's transfer stations list regulated hazardous waste as prohibited. phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[1] phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[3]
How do I schedule a hazardous waste pickup in Phoenix?
Use the City's HHW collection request form, which shows appointments within the next 30 days. If you see no dates, email hhwcollection@phoenix.gov. Have your City Services Bill account number and service address ready. phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[1]
Is Phoenix's hazardous waste pickup free?
Yes. The City describes it as a free collection service for Phoenix solid waste customers, limited to one collection per customer per calendar year. phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[1]
How much paint can I get rid of in Phoenix?
Paint, thinner, stripper and stains have a combined 10-gallon limit per home pickup. Chemicals, automotive fluids and garden products each have a 5-gallon limit. phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[2]
Where do I take an old TV in Phoenix?
Either transfer station accepts TVs. Residents can bring up to two CRT units per household per month free; after that it's $20 per CRT plus disposal fees. The home HHW pickup also takes one TV under 50 inches with unbroken glass. phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[3] phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[2]
Can I put old medicine in the HHW pickup?
No. Medications and sharps are on the HHW unacceptable list. Phoenix Police precincts have medication drop boxes for pills, patches and prescription drugs, but not needles, inhalers or liquid medicines. phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix Public Works)[2] phoenix.gov (City of Phoenix)[4]
