How to Get Rid of a Refrigerator in Phoenix (2026)

Phoenix bulk trash won't take fridges. Book the City's $25 appliance pickup (602-262-6251), drop one off free a year, or donate a working one.

The short version

Refrigerators can't go out with Phoenix bulk trash phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2]. You have three official routes:

  1. City appliance pickup for $25 per refrigerator or freezer, added to your city services bill. Call 602-262-6251 phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].
  2. Drop it at a transfer station, where your first refrigerant appliance each year is free phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].
  3. Donate it if it still works: the City recommends Stardust, and St. Vincent de Paul and Goodwill pick up appliances too phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1] St. Vincent de Paul of Arizona[6] Goodwill of Central and Northern Arizona (goodwillaz.org)[8].

Don't count on a utility buyback: SRP says its refrigerator recycling program is no longer available Salt River Project (srpnet.com)[5].

Option 1: The City's paid appliance pickup

Phoenix Public Works runs a scheduled curbside pickup of large appliances for a fee, separate from bulk trash phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].

Cost phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]:

  • Refrigerators, freezers and air conditioners: $25 each, on your next city services bill. Two fridges cost $50.
  • Non-refrigerant appliances (dishwashers, dryers, microwaves, stoves, washers, water heaters, evaporative coolers): $30 for up to five. More than five is priced case by case.
  • Mixing them: a stove, a dryer and a fridge cost $55 ($30 + $25).
  • Cancel before the confirmed day, or you'll be charged a cancellation fee.

How to book: call the Public Works Customer Care Center at 602-262-6251 phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].

Prep rules phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]:

  1. Empty the appliance.
  2. Remove the doors, hinges, lids, glass shelves and latches from refrigerators and freezers. Tape any remaining doors shut or take them off completely.
  3. Put it in front of your property, just behind the sidewalk (or behind the curb if there's no sidewalk).
  4. Not in the alley, and not in your garage or carport.

The door rule isn't just City policy. Under A.R.S. 36-1651, leaving a discarded refrigerator or freezer (1.5 cubic feet or larger) anywhere children can reach it without removing the doors, hinges, lids or latches is a class 3 misdemeanor Arizona State Legislature (azleg.gov)[4].

The City hauls collected appliances to a facility that removes regulated materials such as refrigerants, then sends the metal to a scrap dealer phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].

Option 2: Free drop-off at a transfer station

Phoenix residential solid waste customers can drop off one appliance with refrigerant per year at no cost. After that, there's a $20 environmental fee per appliance, plus disposal fees of $55 per ton ($40 minimum) phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]. To skip the refrigerant fee, have the Freon removed by a certified reclaimer and bring the certificate of removal to the scale house phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].

Bring a copy of your city services (water) bill and a photo ID whose name or address matches the bill phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3].

  • North Gateway Transfer Station: 30205 N. Black Canyon Hwy., Phoenix, AZ 85085 phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3]
  • 27th Avenue Transfer Station: 3060 S. 27th Ave., Phoenix, AZ 85009 phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3]
  • Hours: Monday-Friday 5:30 a.m.-5 p.m., Saturday 6 a.m.-3 p.m., closed Sunday and on City holidays including Thanksgiving and the day after phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3]

Secure your load: the unsecured-load penalty is $20 phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3].

Option 3: Donate a working fridge

  • Stardust Building Supplies. The City's appliance page suggests donating working appliances to Stardust, a local nonprofit, and notes donations are tax-deductible phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]. Stardust's home pickup runs Monday to Friday in Maricopa County for a $25 nonrefundable fee, and your donation must be worth at least $100. Call 480-964-0177 Stardust Building Supplies (stardustbuilding.org)[9].
  • St. Vincent de Paul. Its standard pickup is free and accepts most gently used appliances, but drivers only collect from outside the home (curb, driveway or open garage with someone home) on the first floor, and you need to book at least 3 days ahead. Call 602-254-3338 St. Vincent de Paul of Arizona[6]. Non-working appliances aren't accepted St. Vincent de Paul of Arizona[7]. For removal from inside the home, its Priority Pickup starts at $110 St. Vincent de Paul of Arizona[6].
  • Goodwill Arizona. Its home pickup form lists large appliances (stove, fridge, washer). Items must be in good condition and at ground level, since drivers can't use stairs or elevators Goodwill of Central and Northern Arizona (goodwillaz.org)[8].

Want it hauled away?

The City's appliance pickup is curbside only: you have to empty the fridge, strip the doors and shelves, and get it out front yourself. If you can't move it, need it gone from inside the house, or have more than one to clear out, a junk-removal crew can carry it out for you. Confirm they serve your ZIP and check the price before you book.

What about the Freon?

Federal rules put the duty to make sure refrigerant is recovered on the final person in the disposal chain, such as the scrap metal recycler or landfill. If an appliance arrives with no refrigerant left, that facility must keep a signed statement naming who recovered it and when U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (epa.gov)[10]. The City's transfer stations charge a $20 environmental fee for refrigerant appliances beyond your free one unless you bring a certificate of removal phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1].

Frequently asked questions

Will Phoenix bulk trash pick up a refrigerator?
No. The City's bulk trash page lists large appliances that use refrigerants, such as freezers, refrigerators and air conditioners, among items not collected. Use the separate appliance pickup or a transfer station instead. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[2] phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]
How much does Phoenix charge to pick up a refrigerator?
$25 per refrigerant appliance, added to your next city services bill. Two refrigerators cost $50. A cancellation fee applies if you don't cancel before the confirmed day. Call the Public Works Customer Care Center at 602-262-6251. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]
Can I drop off a refrigerator for free in Phoenix?
Yes, once a year. Phoenix residential solid waste customers can drop off one appliance with refrigerant at no cost per year at a transfer station. After that, there's a $20 environmental fee per appliance plus disposal fees of $55 a ton (minimum $40). Bring your city services bill and photo ID. phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1] phoenix.gov (Public Works)[3]
Does SRP pick up old refrigerators?
Not anymore. SRP says it offered a refrigerator recycling program in the past but it's no longer available. Salt River Project (srpnet.com)[5]
Why do I have to take the doors off a refrigerator in Arizona?
Arizona law makes it a class 3 misdemeanor to discard or leave a refrigerator or freezer where children can reach it without removing the doors, hinges, lids or latches. Phoenix's pickup rules also require glass shelves to be removed. Arizona State Legislature (azleg.gov)[4] phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]
Will a charity pick up a working refrigerator in Phoenix?
Some will. St. Vincent de Paul's free standard pickup accepts most gently used appliances but not non-working ones, and Goodwill Arizona lists large appliances such as fridges on its home pickup form. The City also points residents to Stardust for working appliances. St. Vincent de Paul of Arizona[6] St. Vincent de Paul of Arizona[7] Goodwill of Central and Northern Arizona (goodwillaz.org)[8] phoenix.gov (Public Works)[1]
